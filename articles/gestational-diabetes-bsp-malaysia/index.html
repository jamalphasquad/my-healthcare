      Gestational diabetes and the BSP chart: the glucose test, normal blood sugar in pregnancy, and what to do

      Gestational diabetes (GDM, or kencing manis semasa mengandung) is high blood sugar that first appears in pregnancy and usually goes away after the birth. It rarely causes symptoms, so it is found by testing. Controlling blood sugar lowers the risks for mother and baby. In Malaysia you will hear about the MGTT (glucose test) and the BSP (blood sugar profile). Here is what they mean.

      Key points
      • In the National Health and Morbidity Survey, the share of Malaysian mothers who said they had been told they had diabetes in pregnancy rose from 12.5% in 2016 to 27.1% in 2022.
      • Under Ministry of Health guidelines, women with risk factors have a 75 g glucose test (OGTT/MGTT) at booking and again at 24 to 28 weeks. Women aged 25 or over without other risk factors are tested at 24 to 28 weeks.
      • GDM is diagnosed in Malaysia if fasting glucose is 5.1 mmol/L or more, or the 2-hour result is 7.8 mmol/L or more.
      • BSP (blood sugar profile) targets: before meals 5.3 or lower, 1 hour after meals 7.8 or lower, 2 hours after meals 6.7 or lower.
      • About half of women with GDM later develop type 2 diabetes, so have a glucose test about 6 weeks after the birth, then yearly checks.

      What is gestational diabetes?

      During pregnancy, hormones from the placenta make the body less responsive to insulin. Most women make enough extra insulin to cope. In gestational diabetes, the body can’t keep up, and blood sugar rises. It usually develops in the second half of pregnancy and goes away after delivery.

      It usually causes no symptoms, which is why testing matters. A few women notice more thirst, passing more urine or tiredness, which are also common in normal pregnancy.

      In Malaysia, it is becoming much more common. The share of mothers in the National Health and Morbidity Survey who said they had been told they had diabetes in pregnancy more than doubled between 2016 and 2022, to 27.1%. The risk was higher with older age, overweight, obesity and high blood pressure.

      Who should be tested?

      The Ministry of Health’s guideline on diabetes in pregnancy lists these risk factors:

      • BMI over 27 (see our BMI calculator)
      • gestational diabetes in a previous pregnancy
      • a parent, brother or sister with diabetes
      • a previous baby weighing more than 4 kg
      • a previous unexplained stillbirth, baby with birth defects, or difficult birth with the baby’s shoulder stuck
      • sugar in the urine on two occasions
      • high blood pressure, too much fluid around the baby, or taking steroid medicines

      Timing:

      • With a risk factor: a glucose tolerance test at your first antenatal visit (booking), repeated at 24 to 28 weeks if normal.
      • Aged 25 or over with no other risk factor: a test at 24 to 28 weeks.

      The guideline notes that testing every pregnant woman is ideal where resources allow. HbA1c and random blood sugar tests aren’t used to diagnose gestational diabetes. See our guide to early pregnancy and booking.

      The glucose test (OGTT / MGTT)

      In Malaysian clinics, the 75 g oral glucose tolerance test is often called the MGTT or MOGTT (modified glucose tolerance test).

      1. Fast overnight for at least 8 hours. Plain water is usually allowed.
      2. A blood sample is taken (fasting glucose).
      3. You drink a sweet drink containing 75 g of glucose, within a few minutes.
      4. Rest quietly for 2 hours. Don’t eat, and avoid walking around.
      5. A second blood sample is taken at 2 hours.
      Result (mmol/L)NormalGestational diabetes
      FastingBelow 5.15.1 or more
      2 hours after the drinkBelow 7.87.8 or more

      Either result being high is enough for a diagnosis. Much higher results, such as fasting 7.0 or more, may mean diabetes that was already there before pregnancy, and need a repeat test. Malaysia uses a lower 2-hour cut-off than some international criteria. For normal blood sugar outside pregnancy, see our guide to blood sugar levels.

      The BSP (blood sugar profile) and targets

      After diagnosis, your blood sugar is checked several times a day to see how well it is controlled. A BSP (blood sugar profile) is a set of these checks, traditionally done at the clinic or in hospital. The guideline prefers home monitoring with a fingerprick meter, as it better reflects everyday levels. Where that isn’t possible, a clinic BSP is done about every 2 weeks until delivery.

      WhenTarget (mmol/L)
      Fasting and before meals5.3 or lower
      1 hour after starting a meal7.8 or lower
      2 hours after starting a meal6.7 or lower
      • Your clinic will tell you whether to test 1 or 2 hours after meals, and how often. If you are on diet alone or one type of medicine, it is usually at least once a day until you reach your targets.
      • If you take insulin or metformin, keep your readings above 4.0, and learn the signs of low blood sugar: shaking, sweating, hunger or confusion.
      • Write down every reading, with the time and what you ate, and bring the record, along with your pink book, to every check-up.

      Managing gestational diabetes

      Diet is the first step. The Malaysian guideline recommends a carbohydrate-controlled meal plan, with at least 175 g of carbohydrate a day spread across meals, and low-GI choices:

      • Keep rice, noodles and bread to measured portions. The guideline’s sample menu uses 2 ladles of rice at lunch and dinner.
      • Choose wholegrains and plenty of vegetables, and include protein with each meal.
      • Sugar counts towards your carbohydrates. Choose plain water instead of sweetened drinks.
      • Have smaller, regular meals and snacks rather than large ones.
      • A dietitian can tailor a plan to the foods you eat.

      Activity: 20 to 30 minutes of moderate exercise on most days, such as brisk walking, swimming or pregnancy yoga, unless your doctor advises otherwise.

      Medicines: if targets aren’t reached within 1 to 2 weeks of diet and exercise, a doctor may start metformin tablets, and insulin if needed. Insulin is started straight away if fasting sugar is very high. Both are safe in pregnancy, and insulin can usually be started without a hospital stay.

      Delivery: women controlled by diet alone without complications are usually advised to deliver by 40 weeks, and those on medicines between 37 and 39 weeks. Your obstetrician will plan this with you.

      Why control matters

      Uncontrolled gestational diabetes can lead to:

      • a large baby, making birth more difficult, including the baby’s shoulder getting stuck
      • more need for induction or caesarean birth
      • pre-eclampsia (high blood pressure in pregnancy)
      • premature birth and too much fluid around the baby
      • low blood sugar and jaundice in the baby after birth
      • rarely, stillbirth

      Treatment works: the Malaysian guideline notes that treating gestational diabetes lowers the risk of pre-eclampsia, shoulder problems at birth and large babies.

      After the birth

      • Most women can stop insulin or tablets as soon as the baby is born.
      • Have a glucose tolerance test about 6 weeks after delivery, then a check every year. About half of women with gestational diabetes go on to develop type 2 diabetes.
      • Breastfeeding for at least 3 months is linked to a lower risk of later diabetes.
      • Keep active and aim for a healthy weight.
      • In a future pregnancy, you will have a glucose test early, at booking. Tell your doctor before trying to conceive.

      Where to get tested and checked

      Antenatal clinics at Klinik Kesihatan and private clinics carry out glucose tolerance tests and BSP monitoring. A GP can also check your blood sugar after the birth. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a normal BSP in pregnancy?

      Malaysian targets are 5.3 mmol/L or lower before meals, 7.8 or lower 1 hour after a meal, and 6.7 or lower 2 hours after a meal.

      What does BSP mean?

      Blood sugar profile: a series of blood sugar checks through the day, before and after meals, to monitor diabetes in pregnancy.

      What is the MGTT test?

      The 75 g oral glucose tolerance test: a fasting blood test, a sugary drink, then another blood test 2 hours later.

      What result means gestational diabetes?

      In Malaysia, a fasting glucose of 5.1 mmol/L or more, or a 2-hour result of 7.8 mmol/L or more.

      When is the glucose test done in pregnancy?

      At booking if you have risk factors, and at 24 to 28 weeks. Women aged 25 or over without other risk factors are tested at 24 to 28 weeks.

      Will gestational diabetes harm my baby?

      Well-controlled gestational diabetes usually leads to a healthy baby. Poor control raises the risk of a large baby, birth complications and low blood sugar after birth.

      Does gestational diabetes go away after birth?

      Usually, yes, but about half of women later develop type 2 diabetes, so have a glucose test about 6 weeks after delivery and yearly checks.

      Can I eat rice with gestational diabetes?

      Yes, in measured portions as part of a carbohydrate-controlled plan, with plenty of vegetables and protein. A dietitian can help.
