      Tanda sakit jantung: signs of heart disease, from angina to heart failure, and when to get checked

      Tanda sakit jantung means signs of heart disease. A heart attack is the best-known emergency, but heart disease often shows itself earlier: chest tightness when you walk uphill, breathlessness, swollen ankles or an irregular pulse. Recognising these signs, and knowing your risk, gives you the chance to get treated before an emergency.

      Key points
      • Ischaemic heart disease was Malaysia’s leading cause of death in 2024, with 17,421 deaths. It is the top cause of death in men and in people aged 41 to 59.
      • Angina is chest tightness that comes on with exertion or stress and eases with rest, usually within 5 minutes. Angina at rest, or that is getting worse, is an emergency.
      • In a US study, 95% of women noticed new symptoms such as unusual tiredness, poor sleep or breathlessness in the month before a heart attack, and 43% had no chest pain during it.
      • Heart failure causes breathlessness on exertion or lying flat, swollen ankles, sudden weight gain and tiredness. An irregular pulse can mean atrial fibrillation, which raises the risk of stroke.
      • Malaysian guidelines suggest a heart risk check from age 30. A third of Malaysian adults have high cholesterol and nearly a third have high blood pressure.

      Angina: chest tightness on exertion

      Angina is chest pain or tightness caused by narrowed heart arteries that can’t deliver enough blood when the heart works harder.

      • Stable angina comes on with exertion, such as walking uphill or climbing stairs, emotional stress, cold weather or a heavy meal. It usually eases with rest within 5 minutes, and feels similar each time.
      • Unstable angina happens at rest, is more severe, lasts longer, or doesn’t ease with rest or medicine. Call 999.

      If you have angina and a GTN spray: stop and rest, use your GTN, take a second dose after 5 minutes if needed, and call 999 if the pain continues 5 minutes after the second dose. See a doctor if your angina is getting worse, more frequent, longer, or comes on at rest.

      Angina means a higher risk of heart attack and stroke, so it should always be assessed. See our guide to chest pain.

      Warning signs before a heart attack

      A widely cited US study of 515 women who had had a heart attack found that about 95% had noticed new or different symptoms a month or more beforehand:

      • unusual tiredness (71%)
      • sleep problems (48%)
      • breathlessness (42%)
      • indigestion (39%)
      • anxiety (35%)

      Fewer than 30% had chest discomfort beforehand, and 43% had no chest pain even during the heart attack. Instead they had breathlessness, weakness, tiredness, a cold sweat and dizziness.

      A caution: these symptoms are common and usually have other causes, and the study couldn’t show they reliably predict a heart attack. Social media posts promising “5 signs before a heart attack” overstate this. The real lesson is that no chest pain doesn’t mean no heart attack, especially in women, older people and people with diabetes. If new symptoms worry you, and you have risk factors, see a doctor.

      For heart attack symptoms and what to do, see our guide to heart attack and stroke.

      Heart failure

      Heart failure means the heart isn’t pumping as well as it should. It is often caused by past heart attacks or long-term high blood pressure. Signs include:

      • breathlessness with activity, or when lying flat
      • tiredness, weakness or dizziness
      • swollen ankles, legs or tummy
      • sudden weight gain over a few days
      • a cough at night, or frothy pink phlegm

      Call 999 for severe breathlessness, blue or grey lips, or collapse. Treatment can improve symptoms and help people live longer.

      Irregular heartbeat and palpitations

      A normal resting pulse is steady and usually 60 to 100 beats a minute. See our guide to normal heart rate (bacaan jantung normal) and palpitations.

      Atrial fibrillation (AF) causes an irregular pulse with no set pattern, sometimes fast, with palpitations, tiredness, breathlessness or dizziness. Some people have no symptoms at all. AF raises the risk of stroke, so doctors use a risk score to decide on blood-thinning medicine.

      Call 999 if a fast or irregular heartbeat comes with chest pain, fainting, breathlessness or signs of stroke.

      Valve disease, cardiomyopathy and young people

      • Heart valve disease can cause breathlessness, tiredness, dizziness, palpitations, swollen ankles or chest pain, or no symptoms with only a murmur heard by a doctor.
      • Cardiomyopathy (heart muscle disease) is often inherited. It can cause dizziness, chest pain, breathlessness or blackouts, and many people have no symptoms. Relatives can be tested.
      • Young people: chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, light-headedness or fainting, especially with a family history of sudden unexplained death at a young age, should be checked. An ECG can detect many of the conditions involved.

      Your risk and getting checked

      Risk factors in Malaysia (National Health and Morbidity Survey 2023 and other national data):

      • high cholesterol: 33% of adults
      • high blood pressure: 29%
      • diabetes: 16%
      • overweight or obesity: 54%
      • smoking: 19% of people aged 15 and over
      • physical inactivity, and a family history of early heart disease (a parent or sibling affected before 55 in men or 65 in women)

      Malaysians tend to have heart attacks younger than people in neighbouring countries, with an average age of about 58 to 59 in Malaysian registries.

      Getting checked: Malaysia’s heart disease prevention guideline recommends a risk check from age 30, using a score based on your age, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes and smoking. People with high blood pressure or diabetes should have an ECG at their first assessment.

      Tests your doctor may arrange include an ECG (heart tracing), blood tests for cholesterol and sugar, an echocardiogram (heart ultrasound), an exercise stress test, a CT scan of the heart arteries, or an angiogram. Routine stress tests and echocardiograms aren’t recommended for people without symptoms. Free screening is available through some schemes. See our guide to free health screening.

      Find a clinic for a heart check

      For chest pain now, call 999. For a heart risk check, blood pressure, cholesterol, sugar and an ECG, a GP can help and refer you to a cardiologist if needed. Our directory lists clinics across Malaysia, including those open at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are the signs of heart disease?

      Chest tightness on exertion that eases with rest (angina), breathlessness, swollen ankles, an irregular or racing pulse, dizziness or fainting, and unusual tiredness.

      Are there warning signs before a heart attack?

      Many people, especially women, notice new tiredness, poor sleep, breathlessness or indigestion in the weeks before. These are common and not specific, so see a doctor if you are worried and have risk factors.

      Can you have a heart attack without chest pain?

      Yes. In one study, 43% of women had no chest pain during their heart attack. Breathlessness, weakness, sweating and dizziness can be the main signs.

      What is a normal heart rate?

      For adults at rest, usually 60 to 100 beats a minute, with a steady rhythm.

      At what age should I check my heart?

      Malaysian guidelines suggest a heart risk assessment from age 30, and earlier if you have diabetes, high blood pressure or a strong family history.

      What tests check for heart disease?

      An ECG, blood tests for cholesterol and sugar, an echocardiogram, an exercise stress test, a CT scan of the heart arteries or an angiogram, depending on your symptoms.

      When should I call 999?

      For chest pain or tightness at rest or lasting more than a few minutes, severe breathlessness, fainting, or a racing or irregular heartbeat with chest pain.
