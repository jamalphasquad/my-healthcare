      Klinik pergigian kerajaan: government dental clinics in Malaysia, fees, appointments and services

      Klinik pergigian kerajaan are government dental clinics run by the Ministry of Health. Many sit inside a Klinik Kesihatan. Treatment costs a few ringgit for Malaysians, young children are treated free, and you can book through MySejahtera. Here is what the official fee schedule says, and how to get an appointment.

      Key points
      • The Ministry of Health had 1,737 oral health facilities in 2023, including 595 dental clinics inside health clinics, 911 school dental clinics and 40 mobile dental clinics.
      • For Malaysian outpatients, the official schedule lists scaling at RM2 a visit, extractions at RM1 a tooth and fillings at RM2 a surface.
      • Children aged 1 to 6 receive free dental treatment, babies under 1 are exempt from fees, and pregnant mothers referred with the pink antenatal book are treated free.
      • You can book an appointment through MySejahtera, available at 610 clinics by the end of 2023. Dental emergencies may be seen as walk-ins after assessment.
      • Root canals, braces, dentures and specialist gum or oral surgery care are available, but some have waiting lists.

      What government dental services are there?

      In 2023, the Ministry of Health had 1,737 oral health facilities:

      • 595 dental clinics inside Klinik Kesihatan or maternal and child health clinics
      • 63 standalone dental clinics, and 77 in hospitals
      • 911 school dental clinics
      • 26 in urban and rural transformation centres (UTC and RTC)
      • 40 mobile dental clinics, with teams visiting schools, kindergartens, toddlers and older people

      Services at primary dental clinics include check-ups, scaling, fillings, extractions, simple root canal treatment at some clinics, dentures, emergency treatment and an oral cancer check for new adult patients.

      School dental service: pupils from Standard 1 to Form 5 in government and government-aided schools get a yearly dental check, with prevention, scaling, fillings and extractions.

      Specialist care: the ministry had 475 dental specialists in 2023, covering orthodontics (braces), gum disease, oral surgery, children’s dentistry, restorative dentistry and special care dentistry. You are usually referred by a government dental officer.

      How much does it cost?

      Charges are set by the Fees (Medical) Order. These are the official outpatient charges for Malaysian citizens, from the ministry’s Oral Health Programme schedule:

      TreatmentCharge
      Scaling and polishingRM2 per visit
      ExtractionRM1 per tooth
      FillingRM2 per surface
      Root canal treatmentRM4 per tooth
      CrownRM3
      Partial dentureRM10, plus RM2 per tooth
      Full dentureRM50 per jaw
      Surgical removal of a toothRM15 (simple) to RM50 (complex impacted tooth)
      Fixed bracesRM500 per arch
      Removable orthodontic applianceRM100 to RM200

      A general outpatient visit costs RM1. Some websites quote higher figures for fillings, so check the current charges at your clinic.

      Who is treated free:

      • Babies under 1: exempt from visit and treatment fees
      • Children aged 1 to 6: free dental treatment, apart from orthodontic appliances. Bring a letter from their nursery or kindergarten to waive the visit fee.
      • Pregnant mothers: free registration, and free treatment when referred from antenatal care with the pink book (Buku Rekod Ibu). Dentures and orthodontic appliances aren’t covered, and walk-ins without a referral are charged normally.
      • Civil servants: exempt from outpatient treatment charges

      Non-citizens pay much higher charges, which vary between facilities. Ask at the clinic before treatment.

      How to get an appointment

      • MySejahtera: dental appointments have been bookable nationwide since 15 May 2023. Open Appointments, choose the dental service, then pick a clinic, date and time. By the end of 2023, 610 dental clinics were on the system.
      • Walk-in: dental emergencies such as a painful tooth may be seen without an appointment after assessment, but routine care is mainly by appointment.
      • Bring: your MyKad, your child’s MyKid or birth certificate, the pink book if you are pregnant, and any referral or exemption letter.
      • Finding a clinic: the app shows nearby clinics, and the Oral Health Programme and MyGOV websites list government dental clinics by state.

      Opening hours vary. Most clinics keep government office hours, so check before you go.

      Waiting times and when to go private

      Government dental care is very affordable, but demand is high. The Malaysian Dental Association and industry groups have raised concerns about long waiting times, and in 2026 about contract dental officers whose contracts ended. Waits are usually longest for specialist care such as braces.

      A private dentist may suit you better if you need treatment quickly, after hours or at weekends, or want cosmetic treatment. Private clinics set their own fees, so ask for a quote first.

      Go to a hospital emergency department or call 999 for facial swelling spreading towards the eye or neck, difficulty swallowing or breathing, a high fever with dental pain, or a serious injury to the face or jaw. See our guide to toothache (sakit gigi).

      Oral health in Malaysia

      • Of 2.66 million primary school pupils examined in 2023, 38% were free of tooth decay.
      • Only about a third of Malaysians aged 60 and over have 20 or more natural teeth, against a national target of half by 2030.
      • Only 1 in 10 adults used government primary dental care in 2023.
      • Pregnant mothers are referred for a dental check, but in 2023 only 62.5% received one.

      A yearly check-up helps catch decay and gum disease early, when treatment is simpler and cheaper. Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and cut down on sugary food and drinks.

      Frequently asked questions

      How much does a government dental clinic charge?

      For Malaysian outpatients, the official schedule lists scaling at RM2 a visit, extractions at RM1 a tooth, fillings at RM2 a surface and root canal treatment at RM4 a tooth.

      How do I book a government dental appointment?

      Use the MySejahtera app: open Appointments, choose the dental service, then pick a clinic, date and time.

      Is dental treatment free for children?

      Yes for young children. Babies under 1 are exempt from fees, and children aged 1 to 6 receive free dental treatment, apart from orthodontic appliances. School pupils have yearly checks through the school dental service.

      Can I walk in to a government dental clinic?

      Dental emergencies such as toothache may be seen after assessment. Routine treatment is mainly by appointment.

      Can I get braces at a government clinic?

      Yes, through a specialist orthodontic referral. Fixed braces are charged at RM500 per arch, and there are often waiting lists.

      Are dental check-ups free for pregnant women?

      Yes, when you are referred from antenatal care with the pink book. Dentures and orthodontic appliances aren’t covered.

      What is the English for klinik pergigian?

      Dental clinic. Klinik pergigian kerajaan means a government dental clinic.
