      Tanda-tanda awal kanser: early warning signs of the most common cancers in Malaysia

      Tanda-tanda awal kanser means early warning signs of cancer. In Malaysia, about 2 in 3 cancers are diagnosed at stage 3 or 4, when treatment is harder. Most of these symptoms turn out to be something less serious, but getting them checked early can make a big difference. Here are the signs to know for the most common cancers in Malaysia.

      Key points
      • Malaysia recorded 168,823 new cancers between 2017 and 2021. The most common are breast, colorectal, lung, lymphoma and liver cancer. About 1 in 8 Malaysians will get cancer in their lifetime.
      • About 65% of cancers in Malaysia are found at stage 3 or 4. For lung cancer the figure is about 95%, and for colorectal cancer about 75%.
      • General warning signs include unexplained weight loss, ongoing tiredness, night sweats, a new lump, unexplained bleeding, and a cough, sore or ulcer that doesn’t heal.
      • See a doctor for a cough lasting more than 3 weeks, a mouth ulcer lasting more than 3 weeks, or any symptom that doesn’t get better, gets worse or keeps coming back.
      • Free or subsidised screening includes mammograms through LPPKN, HPV self-sampling through Program ROSE, and checks through PeKa B40.

      Late diagnosis is a major reason for poorer survival. For cervical cancer, for example, five-year survival in a Malaysian study was about 75% at stage 1 but 23% at stage 4.

      General warning signs

      See a doctor if you notice any of these, especially if they don’t go away, get worse or keep coming back:

      • unexplained weight loss
      • tiredness that doesn’t improve
      • heavy night sweats or unexplained fevers
      • a new lump anywhere in the body
      • unexplained bleeding or bruising
      • a sore, ulcer or rash that won’t heal
      • ongoing indigestion, bloating or difficulty swallowing
      • a hoarse voice or cough that won’t go away
      • yellow skin or eyes (jaundice)

      Cancer Research UK stresses that most of the time these are caused by something less serious, but they are still worth checking.

      Breast, cervical, womb and ovarian cancer

      • Breast cancer (tanda kanser payudara): a lump in the breast, chest or armpit, dimpled skin like orange peel, a change in breast size or shape, nipple discharge (sometimes bloody), a nipple that turns inwards, or a rash on the nipple. Breast pain alone is not usually a sign. Men can get breast cancer too. See our breast screening guide.
      • Cervical cancer (tanda kanser servik): bleeding during or after sex, between periods or after the menopause, changes in vaginal discharge, and pelvic or back pain. See our guide to Pap smears and HPV tests.
      • Womb cancer (tanda kanser rahim): bleeding after the menopause is the key sign. Unusually heavy periods or bleeding between periods also need checking.
      • Ovarian cancer: persistent bloating, tummy or pelvic pain, feeling full quickly and needing to pass urine more often, on most days. See our guide to ovarian cysts and fibroids.

      Bowel, lung, liver and nasopharyngeal cancer

      • Colorectal cancer (tanda kanser usus): a change in bowel habit, blood in the stool (red or black), bleeding from the back passage, tummy pain or a lump, bloating, weight loss, and tiredness or breathlessness from anaemia. See our bowel cancer screening guide.
      • Lung cancer (tanda kanser paru-paru): a cough lasting more than 3 weeks, coughing up blood, breathlessness, chest or shoulder pain, repeated chest infections and weight loss. A Malaysian lung specialist group says more than 60% of women with lung cancer have never smoked, so don’t ignore these signs if you don’t smoke.
      • Nasopharyngeal cancer: particularly common among Chinese and some Bumiputera communities in Sarawak. Signs include a painless lump in the neck (the most common), nosebleeds, a blocked nose, blood in saliva, a blocked ear or hearing loss on one side, ringing in the ear, headache and facial numbness.
      • Liver cancer: often has no early symptoms. Later signs include yellow skin or eyes, weight loss, pain or a lump under the right ribs, and feeling full quickly. Hepatitis B is the leading cause in Malaysia, so get tested and vaccinated. See our hepatitis B guide.

      Mouth, blood and bone cancers

      • Mouth cancer: a mouth ulcer that lasts more than 3 weeks, a red or white patch, a lump, ongoing pain or hoarseness. Smoking, alcohol and chewing betel quid increase the risk. See our guide to mouth ulcers.
      • Lymphoma: painless swelling in the neck, armpit or groin, night sweats, fever, itching and weight loss.
      • Leukaemia: tiredness, pale skin, breathlessness, easy bruising or bleeding, frequent infections and bone aches. See our guide to anaemia.
      • Bone cancer (tanda kanser tulang): bone pain that is often worse at night, swelling and a limp. Bone cancer is rare, and most bone pain has other causes.
      • Prostate cancer: often has no early symptoms. See our guide to the PSA test.

      When to see a doctor, and free screening

      See a doctor if a symptom doesn’t get better, gets worse or keeps coming back. For a cough or a mouth ulcer, see a doctor if it lasts more than 3 weeks. You aren’t wasting the doctor’s time.

      Screening can find some cancers before symptoms start:

      • Mammogram (LPPKN): free for women with a household income of RM10,000 or less, with a subsidy for others, at registered centres.
      • Cervical screening: Program ROSE offers HPV self-sampling for women aged 30 to 65, free depending on nationality and income, with results by SMS.
      • Bowel screening: a stool test (FOBT) is recommended from around age 50. The National Cancer Society Malaysia offers it for a small fee.
      • PeKa B40: includes a clinical breast examination and, for men at risk, a prostate check, for eligible Malaysians aged 40 and over.

      See our guide to free health screening in Malaysia for more options.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are the early signs of cancer?

      Unexplained weight loss, ongoing tiredness, night sweats, a new lump, unexplained bleeding, and a cough, sore or ulcer that doesn’t heal. Most have other causes, but get them checked.

      What are the signs of breast cancer?

      A lump in the breast or armpit, dimpled skin, a change in size or shape, nipple discharge or a nipple that turns inwards. Breast pain alone is not usually a sign.

      What are the signs of bowel cancer?

      A change in bowel habit, blood in the stool, tummy pain or a lump, weight loss, and tiredness from anaemia.

      What are the signs of cervical cancer?

      Bleeding after sex, between periods or after the menopause, and changes in discharge. Regular screening can find changes before cancer develops.

      Can non-smokers get lung cancer?

      Yes. In Malaysia, more than 60% of women with lung cancer have never smoked, according to lung specialists. See a doctor for a cough lasting more than 3 weeks or coughing up blood.

      Is cancer screening free in Malaysia?

      Some is. LPPKN mammograms are free for lower-income households, Program ROSE offers free HPV self-tests depending on eligibility, and PeKa B40 includes some checks.

      How common is late diagnosis of cancer in Malaysia?

      About 65% of cancers are found at stage 3 or 4, including about 95% of lung cancers and 75% of colorectal cancers.
