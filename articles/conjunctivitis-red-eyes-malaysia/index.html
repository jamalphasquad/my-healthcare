      Conjunctivitis and red eyes (sakit mata, mata merah): causes, treatment, how long it’s contagious, and emergencies

      Conjunctivitis (sakit mata or mata merah) is inflammation of the thin, clear layer covering the white of the eye. It makes eyes red, watery, itchy or sticky. Most cases are caused by viruses or allergies and get better without treatment, but some red eyes are an emergency, especially with pain or blurred vision.

      Key points
      • Viral conjunctivitis is the most common type. It causes watery, red eyes, often with a cold, and usually clears in 1 to 2 weeks without treatment.
      • Bacterial conjunctivitis causes thick yellow or green sticky discharge. It often clears in a few days, and antibiotic drops help it clear a little faster.
      • Allergic conjunctivitis causes intense itching in both eyes. It isn’t contagious, and haze can trigger it: during the October 2026 haze, conjunctivitis cases at sentinel clinics rose by about a quarter in a week.
      • Wash your hands often, don’t share towels or pillows, and stop wearing contact lenses until your eyes are better.
      • See an eye doctor or go to an emergency department for eye pain, blurred vision, sensitivity to light, a chemical splash, an injury, or a red eye while wearing contact lenses.

      Types of conjunctivitis

      TypeTypical signsContagious?
      ViralRed, watery eyes, gritty feeling, often starts in one eye and spreads to the other, often with a cold or sore throatYes, very
      BacterialRed eye with thick yellow or green discharge, eyelids stuck together in the morningYes
      AllergicIntense itching, watering and puffy eyelids, usually both eyes, often with sneezingNo
      IrritantRedness and watering after smoke, haze, dust, chlorine or chemicalsNo

      Acute haemorrhagic conjunctivitis is a viral type that causes outbreaks in tropical countries, with bright red patches of blood on the white of the eye. A large outbreak in Melaka in 2002 to 2003 affected more than 10,000 people. In 2022, an outbreak in schools in Sri Aman, Sarawak affected 228 students.

      Haze is a common trigger of allergic and irritant conjunctivitis and dry eyes in Malaysia. See our guide to protecting your family during haze.

      Treatment (ubat sakit mata)

      For all types:

      • Clean away discharge with cotton wool soaked in cooled boiled water, wiping from the inner to the outer corner, using a fresh piece for each eye.
      • A cool, damp cloth over closed eyes soothes them.
      • Lubricating eye drops (artificial tears) from a pharmacy ease grittiness.
      • Don’t wear contact lenses until your eyes are back to normal. Throw away disposable lenses and cases you used while infected.
      • Don’t wear eye make-up, and throw away eye make-up and drops you used since it started.

      Viral: there is no specific treatment and antibiotics don’t help. It usually clears in 1 to 2 weeks, sometimes longer.

      Bacterial: mild cases often clear in 2 to 5 days on their own. A Cochrane review found antibiotic eye drops modestly improve the chance of clearing quickly. In Malaysian government clinics the usual first choice is chloramphenicol drops.

      Allergic: avoid triggers where you can, rinse your eyes, and use antihistamine eye drops or tablets from a pharmacy.

      Avoid steroid eye drops unless an eye doctor prescribes them. Used wrongly, they can cause glaucoma and cataracts and make some infections, such as herpes, much worse. Don’t put breast milk, herbs or other home remedies in the eyes.

      Stopping it spreading

      • Wash your hands with soap for at least 20 seconds, especially after touching your eyes or putting in drops.
      • Don’t rub your eyes.
      • Don’t share towels, pillows, face cloths, eye make-up or eye drops.
      • Wash pillowcases and towels in hot water.
      • Avoid swimming pools until your eyes are better.

      School and work: advice varies. UK health guidance says there is no need to stay off for conjunctivitis unless you feel unwell, while US advice is to stay home while the eyes are red and discharging if you can’t avoid close contact. Malaysian schools and workplaces may have their own rules, especially during outbreaks, so check with them. Viral conjunctivitis spreads easily while the eyes are red and watery.

      When a red eye is an emergency

      Go to an emergency department or see an eye doctor the same day if you have:

      • eye pain, rather than just grittiness or itch
      • blurred or reduced vision that doesn’t clear with blinking
      • pain when looking at bright light
      • a very red eye with a severe headache, nausea or vomiting, or rainbow-coloured haloes around lights. These can be signs of acute glaucoma, which can quickly damage sight.
      • a red, painful eye while wearing contact lenses. Take the lenses out. It can be a serious corneal infection (keratitis).
      • an eye injury, or something stuck in the eye
      • blisters or a cold sore near the eye
      • a newborn baby (under 4 weeks) with red, sticky eyes

      Chemical in the eye: rinse it straight away with lots of clean running water for at least 20 minutes, keep rinsing on the way, and go to an emergency department.

      See a GP if conjunctivitis hasn’t improved after a week or two, keeps coming back, or you are unsure what is causing it. For a sore lump on the eyelid, see our guide to styes.

      A GP can tell most types of conjunctivitis apart, prescribe drops if needed and refer you to an eye doctor for anything more serious. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late and 24 hours.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is sakit mata in English?

      Sakit mata or mata merah usually means conjunctivitis, also called pink eye: inflammation of the thin layer covering the white of the eye.

      How long is conjunctivitis contagious?

      Viral and bacterial conjunctivitis can spread while the eyes are red and discharging. Viral conjunctivitis usually lasts 1 to 2 weeks.

      Do I need antibiotic eye drops?

      Not for viral or allergic conjunctivitis. For bacterial conjunctivitis, drops help it clear a little faster, but many cases clear on their own within a few days.

      How do I know if conjunctivitis is viral or bacterial?

      Viral usually causes watery eyes, often with a cold. Bacterial causes thick yellow or green discharge that sticks the eyelids together. Allergic causes intense itching in both eyes.

      Can haze cause red eyes?

      Yes. Haze can trigger allergic and irritant conjunctivitis and dry eyes. Limit time outdoors and use lubricating drops.

      When is a red eye an emergency?

      With eye pain, blurred vision, sensitivity to light, a severe headache with vomiting, an injury or chemical splash, or a painful red eye while wearing contact lenses.

      Can I wear contact lenses with conjunctivitis?

      No. Stop wearing them until your eyes are back to normal, and throw away disposable lenses and cases you used while infected.

      Should my child stay home from school with conjunctivitis?

      Advice differs between countries, and Malaysian schools may have their own rules, especially during outbreaks. Check with the school, and keep the child home if they feel unwell.
