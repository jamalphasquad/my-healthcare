      Sakit perut sebelah kiri atau kanan: what tummy pain on each side can mean, and when it’s an emergency

      Sakit perut means tummy pain, and sebelah kiri and sebelah kanan mean the left and right side. Where the pain is gives doctors useful clues, although it is never a diagnosis on its own. Here is what pain in each area commonly means, how causes differ for women and children, and the warning signs that need an emergency department straight away.

      Key points
      • Upper right pain is often from the gallbladder or liver. Upper middle and left pain can be gastritis, an ulcer, or pancreatitis, which spreads to the back.
      • Appendicitis usually starts around the belly button and moves to the lower right within hours.
      • In Western countries diverticulitis is usually on the left, but in Asian studies it is often on the right, so it can mimic appendicitis.
      • Women with lower tummy pain who could be pregnant should take a pregnancy test. One-sided pain with shoulder-tip pain or fainting can be an ectopic pregnancy.
      • Go to an emergency department for sudden severe pain, a hard swollen tummy, vomiting blood or green vomit, black stools, or pain in pregnancy.

      Pain can move, and different people feel the same problem in different places. Use this table as a guide only. See our guides to pain in the side and lower back and chest pain.

      Sakit perut sebelah kanan: right-sided pain

      • Gallstones: pain in the middle of the tummy or under the right ribs, lasting more than 30 minutes and sometimes several hours, often with feeling sick, and sometimes spreading to the right shoulder blade. Get urgent help if it lasts more than 30 minutes, and call 999 if you also have a fever or shivering, yellow skin or eyes, or pain spreading to your back with vomiting.
      • Appendicitis: pain usually starts around the belly button and moves to the lower right within a few hours. It gets worse when you move, cough or press on it, often with a fever, poor appetite or vomiting. In pregnancy, young children and older people, the pain may be milder or in a different place.
      • Diverticulitis on the right: in Western countries more than 90% of diverticular disease is on the left. In Asian studies it is often on the right. In one Singapore study, 73% of uncomplicated diverticulitis was right-sided, mostly in people around 50. It can feel like appendicitis, with constant pain, fever and sometimes blood or mucus in the stool.
      • Kidney stone or infection on the right side, often with pain spreading to the groin, urine symptoms or fever.

      Sakit perut sebelah kiri: left-sided pain

      • Constipation and trapped wind are very common causes of lower left pain, often with bloating and relief after opening your bowels.
      • Irritable bowel syndrome causes cramping pain, bloating and changes in bowel habit that come and go.
      • Diverticulitis in Western patterns is usually lower left, with constant severe pain, fever and sometimes blood in the stool.
      • Pancreatitis: sudden severe pain in the upper tummy that spreads to the sides and back, is worse after eating or lying down, and eases when you lean forward. It is usually caused by gallstones or heavy drinking, and is an emergency.
      • Spleen: pain under the left ribs, sometimes felt in the left shoulder. A ruptured spleen after an injury is an emergency.
      • Kidney stone or infection on the left side.

      Causes in women

      • Ectopic pregnancy: usually between 4 and 12 weeks of pregnancy, with one-sided lower tummy pain, vaginal bleeding, and pain at the tip of the shoulder, which can be a sign of internal bleeding. Sudden sharp pain with dizziness or fainting is an emergency. Take a pregnancy test if there is any chance you are pregnant.
      • Ovarian cysts: a cyst that bursts or twists causes sudden, severe, sharp pain on one side of the pelvis. Get urgent help.
      • Endometriosis, period pain and pelvic infection cause lower tummy pain, often on both sides.

      Tummy pain in children

      Most tummy pain in children is caused by constipation, a stomach bug, a urine infection or a recent virus, and often no clear cause is found. Watch for:

      • Mesenteric adenitis: swollen glands in the tummy after a virus, causing lower right pain and fever. It can look like appendicitis, and settles within 1 to 4 weeks.
      • Appendicitis can occur at any age. Young children may not describe it clearly.
      • Intussusception: the most common cause of a blocked bowel between about 6 months and 3 years. Episodes of severe pain last 15 to 20 minutes, the child seems well in between, and there may be vomiting and dark red, jelly-like stools. This is an emergency.
      • Testicular torsion: boys, especially aged 12 to 18, may feel the pain of a twisted testicle in the tummy. Almost all testicles can be saved within 4 to 6 hours, so go to an emergency department straight away for sudden testicle or groin pain.

      See a doctor if a child’s tummy pain lasts more than 24 hours, and urgently if they won’t move, have a fever over 38°C, green vomit or a lump in the groin.

      What to tell the doctor

      • where the pain is, and whether it has moved or spreads anywhere
      • what it feels like: sharp, cramping, burning, constant or coming in waves
      • whether it started suddenly or gradually, and how bad it is
      • what makes it better or worse, such as eating, moving or lying down
      • other symptoms: vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation, fever, yellow eyes, black stools, blood in the urine, weight loss
      • your medicines, including steroids and blood thinners, and alcohol use
      • whether you could be pregnant

      Painkillers: hospital studies have found that giving strong painkillers while doctors assess tummy pain doesn’t lead to missed diagnoses. Don’t let painkillers delay you getting checked, and avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers if the pain could be from your stomach or if you might have dengue.

      When tummy pain is an emergency

      Call 999 or go to an emergency department if you have:

      • sudden, severe tummy pain, or pain that suddenly gets much worse
      • a hard, swollen or very tender tummy
      • vomiting blood, or vomit that is green or looks like coffee grounds
      • black, sticky stools, or a lot of blood in your stool
      • pain with yellow skin or eyes, or fever and shivering
      • pain after an injury
      • pain with a rash that doesn’t fade when pressed, or difficulty breathing
      • being unable to pass urine, stool or wind
      • pain in pregnancy
      • upper tummy pain with sweating, breathlessness or pain spreading to the chest, arm or jaw, which can be a heart attack

      Don’t drive yourself. Older people, and those taking steroids or immune-suppressing medicines, can have serious problems with milder pain, so get checked early. See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      Find a clinic for tummy pain

      For pain that isn’t severe, a GP can examine you, test your urine, arrange blood tests or an ultrasound, and refer you if needed. For severe pain, go to an emergency department. Our directory lists clinics across Malaysia, including those open late and 24 hours.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is sakit perut in English?

      Tummy ache or abdominal pain. Sebelah kiri means the left side and sebelah kanan means the right side.

      What causes sakit perut sebelah kanan bawah?

      Appendicitis is the most important cause to rule out. Constipation, right-sided diverticulitis (common in Asians), and in women ovarian cysts or ectopic pregnancy are also possible.

      What causes sakit perut sebelah kiri bawah?

      Constipation, trapped wind and irritable bowel syndrome are common causes. Diverticulitis, kidney stones and, in women, ovarian or pregnancy problems are also possible.

      What causes pain under the right ribs?

      Often the gallbladder, especially after a fatty meal, or the liver. Get urgent help if it lasts more than 30 minutes or comes with fever or yellow eyes.

      Can a heart attack feel like stomach pain?

      Yes. A heart attack can feel like indigestion or upper tummy pain, often with sweating, breathlessness or pain in the chest, arm or jaw. Call 999.

      When should I go to hospital for stomach pain?

      For sudden severe pain, a hard swollen tummy, vomiting blood, black stools, pain with fever and yellow eyes, or pain in pregnancy.

      Should I take painkillers before seeing a doctor?

      Painkillers don’t usually hide the cause, but don’t let them delay you getting checked. Avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers if the pain could be from your stomach or if you might have dengue.
