      Bacaan kolesterol normal: cholesterol levels in mmol/L, LDL targets, and how to lower cholesterol

      Bacaan kolesterol normal means a normal cholesterol reading. A lipid test reports four numbers: total cholesterol, LDL (“bad”) cholesterol, HDL (“good”) cholesterol and triglycerides. Malaysian guidelines don’t give one “normal” LDL for everyone. Your target depends on your overall risk of heart disease. Here is how to read your results.

      Key points
      • Malaysia’s guideline flags total cholesterol above 5.2 mmol/L, HDL below 1.0 in men or 1.2 in women, and triglycerides above 1.7 mmol/L.
      • There is no single normal LDL. Under the 2017 guideline, people at low or intermediate risk aim for below 3.0 mmol/L. People at high or very high risk aim much lower, and the 2023 update lowered these targets to 1.8 and 1.4 mmol/L.
      • You usually don’t need to fast for a cholesterol test. Adults should be screened from age 30, or from 18 with a strong family history or diabetes.
      • A third of Malaysian adults have high cholesterol, and about half of them don’t know it. About 1 in 100 Malaysians has inherited high cholesterol.
      • Cutting saturated fat (including santan and coconut oil), eating more fibre, exercising and losing weight help. Statins lower LDL by up to about half, and most muscle aches people blame on them aren’t caused by the statin.

      What is a normal cholesterol reading?

      These are the levels Malaysia’s dyslipidaemia guideline flags as abnormal:

      TestFlagged as abnormalIn mg/dL
      Total cholesterolAbove 5.2 mmol/LAbove about 200
      HDL (“good”) cholesterolBelow 1.0 (men) or 1.2 (women)Below about 39 or 46
      TriglyceridesAbove 1.7 mmol/LAbove about 150
      LDL (“bad”) cholesterolDepends on your risk (see below)Optimal is below about 100
      • The guideline describes an LDL of 1.3 to 1.8 mmol/L as physiologically normal, and below 2.6 as optimal for adults.
      • Non-HDL cholesterol (total cholesterol minus HDL) is a useful extra measure, especially if you have diabetes or high triglycerides. Its target is your LDL target plus 0.8.
      • To convert cholesterol from mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply by about 38.7. For triglycerides, multiply by about 88.6.

      See also our guide to blood test results.

      Your LDL target depends on your risk

      Doctors first decide your risk of a heart attack or stroke. If you already have heart disease, diabetes or kidney disease, you are automatically at high or very high risk. Otherwise, your doctor uses a score based on your age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes and smoking to estimate your 10-year risk.

      RiskLDL target (2017 guideline)2023 update
      Low (under 10% in 10 years)Below 3.0 mmol/LBelow 3.0 mmol/L
      Intermediate (10 to 20%)Below 3.0 mmol/LReported as below 2.6 mmol/L
      High (over 20%, diabetes, kidney disease)2.6 mmol/L, or a 50% reduction1.8 mmol/L or less, and at least a 50% reduction
      Very high (heart attack, stroke, narrowed arteries)Below 1.8 mmol/L, or a 50% reduction1.4 mmol/L or less, and at least a 50% reduction

      An LDL above 4.9 mmol/L on its own puts you at high risk. The 2023 targets come from published summaries of the update, so your doctor may use slightly different figures. See our guide to signs of heart disease.

      Testing: who, when and do I need to fast?

      • Who: all adults over 30 should have a full lipid profile. Start from age 18 if you have a family history of early heart disease, diabetes, obesity around the waist or a known inherited cholesterol problem.
      • Fasting: usually not needed. Results differ by only about 0.2 mmol/L. You may be asked to fast if your triglycerides were very high, or if you are having other tests that need fasting.
      • How often: typically every few years if normal. If you start or change treatment, your doctor will recheck in 1 to 3 months, then every 6 to 12 months.

      In Malaysia: the 2023 National Health and Morbidity Survey found high cholesterol in a third of adults, about 7.5 million people, and about half didn’t know it. Free screening is available through some schemes. See our guide to free health screening.

      Inherited high cholesterol (familial hypercholesterolaemia) affects about 1 in 100 Malaysians. Suspect it if your LDL is very high, a parent or sibling had heart disease before 55 (men) or 65 (women), or you have fatty lumps on the tendons of your fingers or heels. Family members should be tested too.

      How to lower cholesterol with diet and lifestyle

      • Cut saturated fat to less than 10% of your energy. Santan, coconut oil and palm oil are high in saturated fat, as are fatty meat and fried food. A review of 16 trials found coconut oil raised LDL compared with other vegetable oils.
      • Avoid trans fats, found in some biscuits, pastries and fried snacks.
      • Eat more fibre: aim for 20 to 30g a day, from vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains. Oats with 3g of beta-glucan a day lower LDL by about 0.25 mmol/L. Juicing removes fibre, so eat whole fruit.
      • Reduce refined carbohydrates such as white rice and sugary drinks, especially if your triglycerides are high.
      • Exercise: regular aerobic exercise raises HDL and lowers triglycerides.
      • Lose weight if you are overweight. Even 5 to 10% helps. See our BMI calculator.
      • Stop smoking, which raises HDL.

      Eggs: cholesterol in food has only a weak effect on blood cholesterol for most people. The American Heart Association says one egg a day is reasonable as part of a healthy diet.

      Statins and other medicines

      Statins lower LDL by about 21 to 55%, depending on the type and dose. Common generic statins include atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and pravastatin. Other options, such as ezetimibe or injections (PCSK9 inhibitors), may be added if targets aren’t reached.

      • Muscle aches: in blinded trials, muscle aches were almost as common on placebo (26.6%) as on statins (27.1%), and more than 90% of reported muscle symptoms weren’t caused by the statin. If you do get symptoms, most people can take a different statin.
      • Liver: a mild rise in liver tests happens in fewer than 3 in 100 people. Serious liver problems are very rare.
      • Diabetes: statins slightly increase the chance of developing diabetes, but the benefit for the heart is much greater.
      • Pregnancy: stop statins before trying for a baby, and don’t take them while pregnant or breastfeeding.
      • Grapefruit can interact with some statins.

      Supplements:

      • Red yeast rice contains monacolin K, which is the same as the statin lovastatin. In Malaysia, these products must be registered, are limited to 10mg of monacolin K a day, and must carry warnings. Don’t take them with a prescribed statin, and check for a MAL number.
      • Fish oil at high doses lowers triglycerides, but doesn’t replace statins for LDL.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a normal cholesterol level in Malaysia?

      Malaysia’s guideline flags total cholesterol above 5.2 mmol/L, HDL below 1.0 (men) or 1.2 (women) and triglycerides above 1.7 mmol/L. The LDL target depends on your heart risk.

      What is a normal LDL level?

      An LDL below 2.6 mmol/L is optimal for most adults. People at low or intermediate risk aim for below 3.0, and people at high or very high risk aim much lower, down to 1.4 mmol/L.

      Do I need to fast for a cholesterol test?

      Usually not. Your doctor may ask you to fast if your triglycerides were very high before, or for other tests.

      How can I lower my cholesterol naturally?

      Cut saturated fat such as santan and fried food, eat more fibre and oats, exercise regularly, lose weight if needed and stop smoking.

      Are eggs bad for cholesterol?

      For most people, eating eggs has only a small effect on blood cholesterol. About one egg a day is reasonable as part of a healthy diet.

      Do statins cause muscle pain?

      Muscle aches are common whether or not people take statins. Trials found that more than 90% of reported muscle symptoms weren’t caused by the statin.

      Is red yeast rice safe?

      It contains the same substance as a statin. Use only registered products with a MAL number, and don’t take it with a prescribed statin.
