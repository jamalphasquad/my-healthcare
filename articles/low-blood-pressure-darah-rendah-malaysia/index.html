      Low blood pressure (darah rendah): normal readings, symptoms, causes and what to do

      Low blood pressure (tekanan darah rendah, or “darah rendah”) generally means a reading below 90/60 mmHg. For many people, especially the young and fit, it is normal and harmless. It only matters if it causes symptoms such as dizziness or fainting, or if it comes on suddenly. Here is what the numbers mean, why it happens and what to do.

      Key points
      • Low blood pressure generally means below 90/60 mmHg. If you feel well, it usually needs no treatment.
      • Symptoms include dizziness or light-headedness, blurred vision, nausea, weakness and fainting, especially on standing up.
      • Common causes are dehydration, medicines for blood pressure, depression or Parkinson’s disease, pregnancy, and heat. Sudden low blood pressure can come from bleeding or severe infection.
      • Stand up slowly, drink more water, eat smaller meals, avoid standing for long, and don’t stop prescribed medicines on your own.
      • Call 999 for fainting with chest pain, or cold, clammy, pale skin with fast breathing, confusion or heavy bleeding, which can mean shock.

      What is a low blood pressure reading? (Bacaan tekanan darah rendah)

      CategoryTop number (systolic)Bottom number (diastolic)
      LowBelow 90Below 60
      OptimalBelow 120Below 80
      Normal120 to 12980 to 84

      The optimal and normal bands come from Malaysia’s Clinical Practice Guidelines on hypertension, which set no lower limit. The “below 90/60” figure is the commonly used definition of low blood pressure. For high readings and how to measure at home, see our guides to high blood pressure and checking your blood pressure at home.

      Low blood pressure often isn’t a problem. Fit people and those with a family history of low blood pressure may naturally have low readings and feel completely well.

      “Darah rendah” or “kurang darah”? People sometimes mix these up because the symptoms overlap. Darah rendah means low blood pressure. Kurang darah means anaemia, a low level of haemoglobin in the blood. They are different conditions with different treatments. See our guide to anaemia.

      Symptoms

      • dizziness or light-headedness (see our guide to vertigo and dizziness)
      • blurred vision
      • feeling sick
      • weakness and tiredness
      • headache or palpitations
      • confusion
      • fainting

      Types of low blood pressure:

      • Postural (orthostatic) hypotension: blood pressure falls when you stand up, by at least 20 points on the top number or 10 on the bottom within 3 minutes. It causes dizziness on standing and becomes more common with age.
      • After meals (postprandial): a fall in blood pressure within 2 hours of eating, mostly in older people.
      • Vasovagal fainting: a sudden drop triggered by heat, standing for a long time, pain, stress or the sight of blood, often with nausea, sweating, paleness and tunnel vision first.

      Causes (punca darah rendah)

      • Dehydration, from hot weather, not drinking enough, vomiting or diarrhoea. See our guide to food poisoning and rehydration.
      • Medicines, including blood pressure tablets, water tablets (diuretics), alpha-blockers for the prostate, and some medicines for depression or Parkinson’s disease
      • Pregnancy: blood pressure normally dips in the first half of pregnancy, and usually needs no treatment. Lying on your side can help. Fainting in early pregnancy with pain or bleeding needs urgent care. See our guide to early pregnancy warning signs.
      • Long periods of bed rest
      • Diabetes affecting the nerves that control blood pressure, and Parkinson’s disease
      • Heart problems, such as a heart attack or heart failure
      • Anaemia, or blood loss from an injury or inside the body
      • Adrenal gland problems (Addison’s disease)
      • Severe infection (sepsis) or a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)

      Hot weather: during the 2026 hot spell, the Ministry of Health advised people to avoid being outdoors from 11 am to 4 pm, drink at least 8 glasses of water and limit caffeinated drinks.

      Fasting: older people who fast in Ramadan should review their blood pressure medicines with a doctor beforehand, rehydrate well between iftar and sahur, and take care when standing up.

      What helps (cara menaikkan tekanan darah rendah)

      • Stand up slowly, especially first thing in the morning. Sit on the edge of the bed for a minute first.
      • Drink more water through the day, unless you have been told to limit fluids.
      • Eat smaller, more frequent meals, with fewer heavy carbohydrates, if you feel dizzy after eating.
      • Avoid standing still for long, hot baths, and too much alcohol.
      • Tensing your legs, crossing them or squeezing your thighs helps push blood back up.
      • Support stockings may help some people. Ask a doctor first.
      • Raising the head of your bed slightly can help with dizziness in the morning.
      • More salt helps some people, but only on a doctor’s advice, especially if you have heart or kidney disease or high blood pressure.

      If you feel faint: lie down and raise your legs, or sit with your head between your knees, until you feel better. Stand up slowly afterwards.

      Medicines: if a medicine may be the cause, your doctor may change it. Don’t stop prescribed medicines by yourself. Medicines to raise blood pressure are rarely needed.

      Food: there is no good evidence that particular foods, such as goat meat, cure low blood pressure. Regular meals and enough fluids matter more.

      Older adults and falls

      Postural hypotension becomes more common with age and is linked to falls, even in people without symptoms. If an older relative feels dizzy on standing, or has had a fall, ask a doctor to check their blood pressure lying down and then standing, and to review their medicines.

      When to get help

      Call 999 or go to an emergency department if someone:

      • faints and has chest pain, an irregular heartbeat, a seizure, or doesn’t wake within a minute
      • faints during exercise or while lying down
      • has pale, cold, clammy skin with fast, shallow breathing, a weak pulse, or confusion or restlessness. These are signs of shock. Lay them down with their legs raised.
      • has black, tarry stools, heavy bleeding or is vomiting blood
      • has signs of severe dehydration or infection, such as blotchy skin, drowsiness or a high fever

      See a doctor if you often feel dizzy or faint, have fallen, or your low readings are new or come with symptoms. A doctor can check your blood pressure lying and standing, and may arrange blood tests and a heart tracing (ECG). See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      Where to get your blood pressure checked

      A GP can check your blood pressure lying and standing, look for causes such as dehydration, anaemia or medicines, and adjust your treatment. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is considered low blood pressure?

      Generally below 90/60 mmHg. If you feel well, it usually isn’t a problem and needs no treatment.

      What are the symptoms of low blood pressure?

      Dizziness, light-headedness, blurred vision, nausea, weakness and fainting, particularly on standing up.

      What causes low blood pressure?

      Common causes are dehydration, some medicines, pregnancy, heat and long bed rest. Heart problems, bleeding and severe infections can also cause it.

      Is darah rendah the same as kurang darah?

      No. Darah rendah means low blood pressure. Kurang darah means anaemia, or low haemoglobin. They are different conditions.

      How can I raise low blood pressure quickly?

      If you feel faint, lie down and raise your legs, or sit with your head between your knees. Drink water, and stand up slowly afterwards.

      What should I eat for low blood pressure?

      Regular, smaller meals and enough fluids help most. No particular food is proven to cure it. Check with a doctor before adding extra salt.

      Is low blood pressure dangerous?

      Usually not, but fainting can cause injuries, and sudden low blood pressure with chest pain, confusion, or cold, clammy skin is an emergency.

      Why do I feel dizzy when I stand up?

      Blood pressure can drop briefly on standing, which is called postural hypotension. It is common with dehydration, some medicines and older age.
