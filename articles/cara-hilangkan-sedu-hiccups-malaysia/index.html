      Cara hilangkan sedu (hiccups): what causes them, remedies to try, babies, and when hiccups need a doctor

      Sedu (also called cegukan) is the Malay word for hiccups. They happen when the diaphragm, the muscle under your lungs, suddenly tightens and the vocal cords snap shut, making the “hic” sound. Most bouts stop on their own within minutes. Hiccups that last more than 2 days are uncommon and worth checking with a doctor.

      Key points
      • Hiccups are often triggered by eating too much or too fast, fizzy drinks, alcohol, spicy food, swallowing air, stress or excitement. Often there is no obvious cause.
      • Popular remedies include holding your breath, sipping ice-cold water, swallowing a spoonful of sugar and pulling your knees to your chest. They are safe to try, but there is no good evidence that any one works best.
      • Baby hiccups are common and harmless, and usually stop within 5 to 15 minutes.
      • See a doctor if hiccups last more than 48 hours or keep coming back. Causes include acid reflux, some medicines such as dexamethasone, and rarely nerve or brain problems.
      • Get urgent help for hiccups with headache, weakness, numbness or loss of balance, or with chest pain or breathlessness.

      What causes hiccups?

      Hiccups are repeated spasms of the diaphragm. Each spasm is followed by the vocal cords closing suddenly, which makes the “hic”. Doctors group them by how long they last:

      • Short-lived: a few minutes. Almost everyone gets these.
      • Persistent: more than 48 hours, up to a month
      • Intractable: more than a month

      Common triggers:

      • eating too much or too quickly
      • fizzy drinks and alcohol
      • spicy, very hot or very cold food, or something very cold straight after something hot
      • swallowing air, for example when chewing gum, smoking or talking while eating
      • stress, strong emotions or excitement

      Cara hilangkan sedu: remedies to try

      These are commonly suggested, including by the NHS. They are generally safe, but there is no good evidence that one works better than another, or that they work for everyone:

      • hold your breath for a short while
      • sip ice-cold water, or gargle with it
      • swallow a teaspoon of granulated sugar
      • bite on a lemon or taste a little vinegar
      • pull your knees up to your chest and lean forward
      • breathe into a paper bag, keeping it away from your face. Never use a plastic bag.

      What about special straws? A 2021 online study of a rigid drinking straw that makes you suck hard and swallow reported success in about 9 out of 10 users. But the study had no comparison group, relied on users’ own reports, and one of the authors invented the device, so better trials are needed.

      To prevent hiccups: eat slowly, have smaller meals, and cut down on fizzy drinks and alcohol.

      Hiccups in babies

      Hiccups are very common in babies and are usually harmless. A Malaysian Paediatric Association doctor notes that they often last 5 to 15 minutes, sometimes a couple of hours, and tend to bother parents more than the baby.

      • They are often caused by feeding quickly, swallowing air, or crying for a long time.
      • Try changing your baby’s position, burping them and helping them relax.
      • Feed when your baby is calm, check the latch, feed in a more upright position, and keep your baby upright for about 30 minutes after feeds.

      See a doctor if hiccups come with frequent forceful vomiting, poor weight gain, crying or arching the back during feeds, or coughing or choking. These can be signs of reflux.

      Hiccups that won’t stop

      Hiccups lasting more than 2 days are uncommon and usually have a cause. They can make it hard to eat, sleep and talk.

      Possible causes:

      • acid reflux (GERD) and gastritis, among the most common
      • medicines, including the steroid dexamethasone, sleeping tablets (benzodiazepines), strong painkillers and some chemotherapy. See also our guide to steroid tablets.
      • recent surgery, especially under general anaesthetic
      • chest problems such as pneumonia or pleurisy
      • kidney failure and low calcium levels. See our guide to kidney disease.
      • shingles
      • rarely, stroke, a brain tumour, multiple sclerosis, or a tumour in the chest or tummy

      What doctors do: blood tests for salts and kidney function, a chest X-ray and an ECG, and sometimes a camera test for reflux or a scan. Treating the cause, for example reflux, often stops the hiccups. Don’t stop a prescribed medicine without talking to your doctor.

      If hiccups continue, doctors may try medicines such as baclofen, gabapentin, metoclopramide or chlorpromazine. Evidence for these is limited, and they don’t work for everyone.

      When to see a doctor

      See a GP if:

      • hiccups last more than 48 hours
      • they keep coming back and affect your daily life, eating or sleep
      • a baby’s hiccups come with feeding problems or poor weight gain

      Get urgent help or call 999 if hiccups come with:

      • headache, weakness, numbness, slurred speech or loss of balance, which can be signs of a stroke
      • chest pain or sudden breathlessness. See our guide to chest pain.

      For hiccups that last more than 2 days, a GP can look for a cause such as reflux or a medicine, arrange tests and suggest treatment. Our directory lists clinics across Malaysia, including those open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is sedu in English?

      Hiccups. Sedu is also called cegukan in Malay.

      How do I stop hiccups fast?

      Try holding your breath, sipping ice-cold water, swallowing a spoonful of sugar or pulling your knees to your chest. They are safe, but no remedy works for everyone, and most hiccups stop on their own within minutes.

      Why do I keep getting hiccups?

      Common triggers are eating too fast, big meals, fizzy drinks, alcohol, spicy food and stress. If they keep coming back or last more than 2 days, see a doctor to check for reflux or a medicine side effect.

      Are hiccups in babies normal?

      Yes. They are common and harmless. See a doctor if they come with forceful vomiting, poor weight gain or distress during feeds.

      How long is too long for hiccups?

      See a doctor if they last more than 48 hours.

      Can medicines cause hiccups?

      Yes. Steroids such as dexamethasone, sleeping tablets, strong painkillers and some chemotherapy can cause hiccups. Don’t stop a medicine without talking to your doctor.
