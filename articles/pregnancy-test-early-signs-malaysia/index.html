      Pregnancy test and early signs of pregnancy (tanda-tanda hamil): when to test, reading results, and your first antenatal visit

      A missed period is often the first sign of pregnancy, but early symptoms can feel a lot like a period coming. A home pregnancy test is reliable from the first day of a missed period. If it is positive, the next step in Malaysia is to register at a Klinik Kesihatan before 12 weeks, where folic acid and antenatal checks are provided.

      Key points
      • Early signs include a missed period, light spotting, nausea, sore breasts, tiredness and needing to pass urine more often. Many can also happen before a period, so a test is the only way to know.
      • Home pregnancy tests detect the hormone hCG and are about 99% accurate from the first day of a missed period, if used correctly.
      • A faint line usually still means pregnant. A negative result is more likely to be wrong than a positive one, usually because the test was done too early.
      • In Malaysia, register at a Klinik Kesihatan before 12 weeks of pregnancy. You will get the pink book (Buku Pink), free folic acid and iron, and checks for HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis, diabetes and blood pressure.
      • Get emergency care for severe tummy pain on one side, shoulder-tip pain, heavy bleeding, dizziness or fainting, which can be signs of an ectopic pregnancy.

      Early signs of pregnancy (tanda-tanda hamil)

      Symptoms usually start around 4 to 6 weeks after the first day of your last period:

      • a missed period, the most reliable early sign if your cycle is regular
      • very light spotting around the time your period is due (implantation bleeding), which can be mistaken for a light period
      • nausea or vomiting (morning sickness), which can happen at any time of day
      • sore, tender or larger breasts
      • tiredness
      • needing to pass urine more often
      • mood changes, a metallic taste, a stronger sense of smell, cravings, more vaginal discharge or constipation

      Period or pregnancy? Breast tenderness, tiredness, mood changes and mild cramps can happen before a period too, so symptoms alone can’t tell you whether you are pregnant. Take a test.

      When to take a pregnancy test

      • Pregnancy tests detect hCG, a hormone made once a fertilised egg implants, about 6 days after fertilisation. Levels rise quickly over the following days.
      • Test from the first day of your missed period. If you don’t know when your period is due, test at least 21 days after the last time you had unprotected sex.
      • Some sensitive tests claim to work a few days before a missed period, but they are more likely to give a false negative that early.
      • You can use urine from any time of day. If you are testing early, first-morning urine is the most concentrated. Don’t drink a lot of fluid just before testing.

      Reading the result

      • Follow the instructions for how long to wait, and read the result within the time stated, usually within 10 minutes. A faint line that appears much later may be an evaporation line.
      • A faint line within the reading time usually means you are pregnant. hCG may still be low early on.
      • Positive: a positive result is very likely to be right. Rarely, it can follow a very early pregnancy loss (chemical pregnancy) or some fertility treatments that contain hCG.
      • Negative but no period: you may have tested too early. Test again in a few days. If it is still negative and your period hasn’t come, see a doctor.

      Missed periods without pregnancy can be caused by stress, sudden weight loss or gain, heavy exercise, breastfeeding, hormonal contraception, thyroid problems or PCOS (PMOS). See a doctor if you have missed 3 periods in a row. See also our guide to thyroid problems.

      Blood pregnancy tests

      A blood test can detect hCG earlier than a urine test, about 7 to 10 days after conception, and is the only test that measures the exact level. Doctors use it during fertility treatment, if there is bleeding or pain in early pregnancy, or if they suspect a problem such as an ectopic pregnancy. In a healthy early pregnancy, hCG roughly doubles every 2 days, so the test may be repeated to compare. Most women don’t need a blood test to confirm pregnancy.

      Positive test? Your next steps in Malaysia

      1. Register for antenatal care before 12 weeks of pregnancy at your nearest Klinik Kesihatan, or with a private doctor. The Ministry of Health counts registration after 12 weeks as late.
      2. At your first visit, you will usually have your pregnancy confirmed, your blood pressure, weight and urine checked, an ultrasound, and blood tests including haemoglobin, HIV, hepatitis B and syphilis. You may also be screened for diabetes, depending on your risk.
      3. You will be given the pink book (Buku Pink), your maternal health record. Bring it to every check-up, including at private clinics and hospitals.
      4. Government clinics provide folic acid, iron and multivitamins free. Since May 2025, the Tdap vaccine is also offered free in pregnancy. See our guide to the tetanus vaccine.

      Folic acid: it helps prevent spina bifida and other neural tube defects. The usual dose is 400 micrograms a day, ideally from 3 months before pregnancy until 12 weeks. Women with diabetes, those taking some epilepsy medicines, or who have had a previous pregnancy affected by a neural tube defect need a higher dose prescribed by a doctor. If you didn’t take it before pregnancy, start as soon as you know.

      Also: stop smoking and vaping, avoid alcohol, and check any medicines with a doctor or pharmacist, but don’t stop prescribed medicines without advice.

      Warning signs in early pregnancy

      Go to an emergency department or call 999 if you have:

      • sudden, severe tummy pain, especially on one side
      • pain in the tip of your shoulder
      • heavy bleeding (soaking a pad soon after putting it on)
      • dizziness or fainting

      These can be signs of an ectopic pregnancy, where the pregnancy grows outside the womb, usually between 4 and 12 weeks. It can cause dangerous internal bleeding.

      Get urgent advice for any bleeding or spotting in pregnancy, even without pain. Bleeding isn’t always serious, but it should be checked.

      Severe vomiting: see a doctor soon if you are vomiting often, can’t keep food or fluids down, or have signs of dehydration such as dark or very little urine and dizziness. This may be hyperemesis gravidarum, which can be treated.

      Unplanned pregnancy and support

      If the pregnancy is unplanned or you aren’t sure what to do, you can talk confidentially to a doctor. Other sources of support include:

      • Talian Kasih 15999 (24 hours, or WhatsApp 019-261 5999), the Ministry of Women, Family and Community Development’s helpline for counselling and support
      • LPPKN’s KafeTEEN centres, which offer reproductive health services and counselling for young people

      For preventing pregnancy in future, see our guide to contraception.

      Where to confirm your pregnancy

      A GP can confirm your pregnancy, check your health, start folic acid and refer you for antenatal care. You can also register directly at your nearest Klinik Kesihatan. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      When can I take a pregnancy test?

      From the first day of your missed period. If you don’t know when your period is due, wait at least 21 days after unprotected sex.

      How accurate are home pregnancy tests?

      About 99% accurate from the first day of a missed period, if used as directed. Negative results are more often wrong than positive ones, usually because the test was done too early.

      Does a faint line mean I am pregnant?

      A faint line that appears within the reading time usually means you are pregnant. Lines that appear long after may be evaporation lines.

      What are the early signs of pregnancy?

      A missed period, light spotting, nausea, sore breasts, tiredness and needing to pass urine more often, usually from about 4 to 6 weeks.

      Can I tell if I am pregnant before my period is due?

      Not reliably. Early symptoms are similar to those before a period, and tests are less accurate before a missed period.

      When should I go to the clinic after a positive test?

      Register for antenatal care before 12 weeks of pregnancy at a Klinik Kesihatan or private clinic. Go to an emergency department straight away for severe pain, heavy bleeding or fainting.

      How much folic acid should I take?

      Usually 400 micrograms a day from before pregnancy until 12 weeks. Some women, such as those with diabetes or a previous neural tube defect pregnancy, need a higher dose from a doctor.

      What is the pink book?

      Buku Pink is your maternal health record in Malaysia, given when you register your pregnancy. Bring it to every antenatal check-up.
