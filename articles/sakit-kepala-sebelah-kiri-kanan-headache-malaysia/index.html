      Sakit kepala sebelah kanan, kiri atau belakang: what headache location can mean, relief and red flags

      Sakit kepala means headache, and sebelah kanan, sebelah kiri and bahagian belakang mean the right side, left side and back of the head. Where a headache sits gives useful clues, but it isn’t a diagnosis on its own. Most headaches are tension-type headaches or migraines and aren’t dangerous. Here is what each pattern can mean, and the warning signs to know.

      Key points
      • A one-sided throbbing headache with nausea or sensitivity to light is typical of migraine. A tight band on both sides or at the back of the head is typical of a tension-type headache.
      • Severe pain around one eye with a red, watering eye, lasting 15 minutes to 3 hours, suggests cluster headache. Pain over the cheeks and forehead with a blocked nose suggests sinusitis.
      • Pain at the back of the head that gets worse when you move your neck may come from the neck. A new headache at the temples in someone over 50 needs checking the same day.
      • Take painkillers early, but on no more than 2 days a week. Taking them more often can cause medication-overuse headaches. Avoid ibuprofen and aspirin if you might have dengue.
      • Call 999 for a sudden, severe headache that peaks within seconds or minutes, or a headache with fever and a stiff neck, weakness, confusion, a seizure or loss of vision.

      Sakit kepala sebelah kanan atau kiri

      Migraine is the most common cause of a one-sided headache. About 9 in 100 Malaysians have migraine. Typical features:

      • a throbbing pain, often on one side, that gets worse with movement
      • nausea or vomiting, and sensitivity to light and sound
      • attacks lasting 4 hours to 3 days
      • in about 1 in 10, an aura before the headache, such as zigzag lights, numbness or difficulty speaking, lasting up to an hour

      Cluster headache is rarer but extremely painful: always on one side, around or above the eye or at the temple, lasting 15 minutes to 3 hours, up to several times a day, often with restlessness. It is more common in men in their 30s and 40s. Ordinary painkillers don’t work, so see a doctor.

      Trigeminal neuralgia causes sudden electric-shock pain on one side of the face or jaw, lasting seconds, often triggered by eating, brushing teeth, touching the face or air-conditioning. It usually starts after age 50.

      Sakit kepala bahagian belakang

      • Tension-type headache often starts at the back of the head or neck and spreads forwards, feeling like pressure or a tight band. It lasts from 30 minutes to several days, and is linked to stress, poor posture, tiredness and long hours at a screen.
      • Neck-related (cervicogenic) headache usually starts on one side at the base of the skull, may spread to the front or behind the eye, and gets worse when you move your neck.
      • Occipital neuralgia causes piercing, burning or electric-shock pain at the back of the head, sometimes spreading behind one eye.

      High blood pressure rarely causes headaches unless it is very high, so don’t rely on a headache to tell you your blood pressure is up. Check it with a monitor. See our guide to blood pressure.

      Common triggers

      • not drinking enough, especially in hot weather, and time in the hot sun
      • skipping meals
      • too much or too little sleep
      • stress, and the “let-down” at weekends
      • bright lights, screens and eye strain. Get your eyes tested if you often have headaches when reading.
      • periods and hormonal changes
      • alcohol, and cutting down suddenly on coffee or tea if you usually drink more than about 2 cups a day
      • taking painkillers too often

      Keeping a headache diary can help you spot your triggers.

      Cara hilangkan sakit kepala: relief

      • Paracetamol (adults): 500mg to 1g, at least 4 hours apart, no more than 4g in 24 hours.
      • Ibuprofen (adults): 200 to 400mg with food, at least 4 hours apart, no more than 1,200mg a day without a doctor’s advice. Not suitable with stomach ulcers, some types of asthma, kidney or heart disease, or in pregnancy.
      • Aspirin (adults only): not for anyone under 16.
      • Dengue caution: a severe headache and pain behind the eyes can be dengue. If you have a fever and could have dengue, take paracetamol only and avoid ibuprofen and aspirin.
      • Migraine: painkillers work best taken early in an attack. If they don’t help, a doctor can prescribe a triptan.
      • Rest in a quiet, dark room, drink water, eat something if you have skipped a meal, and apply a cool or warm compress.

      Don’t take painkillers too often. Using simple painkillers on 15 or more days a month, or triptans or codeine-containing painkillers on 10 or more days a month, can cause a medication-overuse headache. Keep painkillers to no more than 2 days a week, and see a doctor if you need them more. See our painkiller guide.

      When a headache is an emergency

      Call 999 or go to an emergency department for a headache that:

      • comes on suddenly and severely, reaching its worst within seconds to minutes (a “thunderclap” headache)
      • comes with a fever and stiff neck, or a rash that doesn’t fade when pressed
      • comes with weakness, numbness, slurred speech, confusion, a seizure or loss of vision. See our guide to stroke.
      • follows a head injury

      See a doctor the same day if you are over 50 with a new headache, scalp tenderness or jaw pain when chewing, especially with vision changes, as giant cell arteritis can cause blindness if untreated. Pregnant women with a severe headache, vision problems or swelling should be seen urgently, as this can be pre-eclampsia.

      See a doctor soon if your headaches are getting more frequent or severe, change pattern, are worse when coughing, sneezing or bending, wake you from sleep, or come with vomiting.

      Children: migraine affects 4 to 10 in 100 children, and attacks are often shorter, sometimes with tummy pain. See a doctor if headaches start before age 7, change pattern, or come with behaviour changes, fits or balance problems.

      Frequently asked questions

      What does sakit kepala sebelah kanan mean?

      A headache on the right side. It is often a migraine, and less often a cluster headache or a neck-related headache. The same causes apply to the left side.

      What causes a headache at the back of the head?

      Usually a tension-type headache, often linked to stress, posture or long screen time. A neck problem or occipital neuralgia can also cause it.

      Is a one-sided headache serious?

      Usually not. Migraine is the most common cause. See a doctor urgently if it is sudden and severe, or comes with weakness, confusion or vision loss.

      How do I get rid of a headache quickly?

      Take paracetamol or ibuprofen early, drink water, eat if you have skipped a meal, and rest in a quiet, dark room. Avoid ibuprofen if you might have dengue.

      Can high blood pressure cause headaches?

      Rarely, unless it is very high. Most people with high blood pressure have no symptoms, so check it with a monitor.

      Why do I keep getting headaches?

      Common reasons include stress, poor sleep, dehydration, skipped meals, eye strain and taking painkillers too often. See a doctor if they are frequent or changing.

      When is a headache an emergency?

      When it is sudden and severe, or comes with fever and a stiff neck, weakness, confusion, a seizure or loss of vision, or after a head injury. Call 999.
