      Mouth ulcers (ulser mulut) and cold sores: causes, what helps, and when to see a doctor

      Mouth ulcers (ulser mulut) are small, painful sores inside the mouth. They are common, aren’t contagious, and usually heal on their own within 1 to 2 weeks. Cold sores are different: they appear on or around the lips, are caused by a virus and are contagious. Occasionally, a sore that won’t heal is something more serious, so it is worth knowing when to get it checked.

      Key points
      • Mouth ulcers are usually round and white or yellow, on the inside of the cheeks, lips or tongue. They clear in 1 to 2 weeks and aren’t contagious.
      • Triggers include biting your cheek, braces, sharp teeth, stress, tiredness, hormonal changes and some foods.
      • Ulcers that keep coming back can be a sign of low iron, vitamin B12 or folate, or of bowel conditions, and are worth a blood test.
      • A mouth ulcer that hasn’t healed in 2 to 3 weeks must be checked by a dentist or doctor. In Malaysia, more than 60% of mouth cancers are found late.
      • Cold sores are caused by the herpes simplex virus. Antiviral cream works best at the first tingle. Keep them away from newborn babies and your eyes.

      Mouth ulcers (ulser mulut)

      Common mouth ulcers (recurrent aphthous stomatitis) are shallow, round sores with a white or yellow centre and a red edge, on the inside of the cheeks or lips, or under the tongue. Most are small and heal within 10 to 14 days. Larger ones can take weeks and may scar. They often run in families.

      Common triggers (punca ulser mulut):

      • biting your cheek, sharp teeth, braces or dentures that rub, hard brushing, or burns from hot food
      • stress, tiredness and hormonal changes, such as around periods
      • stopping smoking
      • certain foods in some people, such as chocolate, coffee, peanuts, cheese and tomatoes
      • toothpaste containing sodium lauryl sulphate (SLS). Switching to SLS-free toothpaste may help, but the evidence is limited.

      If ulcers keep coming back, see a doctor. Possible causes include low iron, folate or vitamin B12, coeliac disease, Crohn’s disease or ulcerative colitis, Behçet’s disease, a weakened immune system, and some medicines, such as NSAIDs and nicorandil. See our guides to anaemia and blood tests.

      What helps (cara hilangkan ulser mulut)

      • Use a soft toothbrush, and avoid spicy, salty, sour, crunchy or very hot food until it heals.
      • Have cool drinks through a straw, and soft foods.
      • Rinse with warm salt water (half a teaspoon in a glass of warm water, then spit it out). This is a traditional, low-risk remedy, although it hasn’t been well studied.
      • Ask a pharmacist about an antiseptic mouthwash (such as alcohol-free chlorhexidine), a numbing gel or spray, a protective paste, or low-strength steroid lozenges.
      • A dentist or doctor can prescribe stronger steroid treatments for severe or frequent ulcers.
      • Honey applied to ulcers shortened healing in some small studies, but it won’t suit everyone. Don’t give honey to babies under 1.

      Numbing gels and children: the US Food and Drug Administration warns that benzocaine, a numbing ingredient in some gels, can rarely cause a dangerous blood problem (methaemoglobinaemia), and young children are at particular risk. Ask a pharmacist before using any numbing product on a child.

      When an ulcer could be mouth cancer

      See a dentist or doctor if an ulcer hasn’t healed in 2 weeks, and don’t leave it beyond 3 weeks. Also get checked for:

      • a red or white patch in the mouth
      • a lump in the mouth, on the lip or in the neck
      • numbness in the mouth or lips, or loose teeth with no obvious reason
      • persistent pain, difficulty swallowing or speaking, or a hoarse voice

      Most of these aren’t cancer, but mouth cancer is much easier to treat when found early. According to the Ministry of Health’s 2025 oral health guideline, Malaysia had 742 new cases and 403 deaths in 2020, and more than 60% are detected late. It is more common in people of Indian origin and among indigenous communities in Sabah and Sarawak.

      Main risk factors: smoking, chewing betel quid (sirih) or tobacco, alcohol, and HPV infection. Betel quid with tobacco raises the risk 3 to 8 times. Regular dental checks can pick up early changes. Government dental clinics examine new patients aged 18 and over for signs of mouth cancer.

      Cold sores

      Cold sores are small blisters on or around the lips, usually caused by the herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1). Most people catch it in childhood, and the virus stays in the body for life, sometimes flaring up.

      • They start with tingling, itching or burning, then blisters form within about 2 days, burst, scab over and heal in about 10 days.
      • Triggers include illness, sun, stress and periods.
      • Contagious from the first tingle until fully healed. Avoid kissing and oral sex, and don’t share cups, towels or lip balm.

      Treatment: antiviral cream (such as aciclovir) from a pharmacy works best if started at the first tingle. A doctor can prescribe antiviral tablets for large, painful or frequent cold sores. Lip balm with SPF 15 or more can help prevent sun-triggered ones.

      Important:

      • Never kiss a newborn baby if you have a cold sore, and wash your hands. Herpes in a young baby can be very dangerous. A baby with a fever of 38°C or more, poor feeding or sores needs urgent care.
      • Keep it away from your eyes. See a doctor urgently for eye pain, redness, blurred vision or sensitivity to light.
      • See a doctor if a cold sore hasn’t healed after 10 days, or you have a weakened immune system.

      Sores on the genitals have other causes. See our guide to STI testing.

      Mouth sores in children

      • Hand, foot and mouth disease: sores in the mouth with a rash on the hands and feet. See our HFMD guide.
      • Herpes gingivostomatitis: a first infection with the cold sore virus, usually at ages 1 to 5, causing fever, swollen, bleeding gums and many mouth sores. It usually clears in about 2 weeks. A doctor can advise whether antiviral treatment is worthwhile.

      The main risk is dehydration, as children refuse to drink because of the pain. Offer cold drinks, ice lollies and oral rehydration fluid, and give paracetamol for pain. Fluids matter more than food. See a doctor urgently if your child refuses all drinks, has far fewer wet nappies or dark urine, or is hard to wake. See our guide to fever in children.

      When to see a doctor or dentist

      • an ulcer lasting more than 2 to 3 weeks
      • ulcers that keep coming back, are unusually large, or are spreading or near the throat
      • ulcers that bleed or become redder and more painful
      • sores on the skin or genitals as well as in the mouth, or swollen joints
      • a sharp tooth, brace or denture that keeps rubbing

      Where to get a mouth ulcer checked

      A GP can check a persistent ulcer, arrange blood tests for recurrent ulcers, prescribe antiviral or steroid treatment, and refer you to a dentist or oral surgeon if needed. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late.

      Frequently asked questions

      What causes mouth ulcers?

      Usually minor injury, such as biting your cheek or braces, stress, tiredness, hormonal changes or some foods. Ulcers that keep returning can be linked to low iron, B12 or folate.

      How long do mouth ulcers last?

      Most heal within 1 to 2 weeks. See a dentist or doctor if one hasn’t healed after 2 to 3 weeks.

      How can I get rid of a mouth ulcer quickly?

      There is no instant cure, but a soft toothbrush, avoiding spicy or sour food, an antiseptic mouthwash, a numbing gel from a pharmacy and salt-water rinses can ease it while it heals.

      Which vitamins help mouth ulcers?

      If you are low in iron, vitamin B12 or folate, treating that may reduce ulcers. Ask a doctor for a blood test rather than guessing.

      Are mouth ulcers contagious?

      No, ordinary mouth ulcers aren’t contagious. Cold sores are.

      Can a mouth ulcer be cancer?

      Most aren’t, but an ulcer that doesn’t heal in 2 to 3 weeks, especially if you smoke, drink or chew sirih, must be checked.

      What is the difference between a mouth ulcer and a cold sore?

      Mouth ulcers are inside the mouth and aren’t contagious. Cold sores are blisters on or around the lips, caused by a virus, and are contagious.

      How do I treat a cold sore?

      Use antiviral cream at the first tingle. A doctor can prescribe tablets for severe or frequent cold sores. Avoid kissing babies until it has fully healed.
