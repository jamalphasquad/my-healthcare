      Anaemia and iron deficiency (kurang darah): symptoms, tests, iron tablets and what to eat

      Anaemia (kurang darah) means you don’t have enough healthy red blood cells or haemoglobin to carry oxygen around the body. The most common cause is iron deficiency, but it isn’t the only one. In Malaysia, thalassaemia trait can look similar, and in men and older women, iron deficiency can be a sign of bleeding from the bowel, so it is worth finding out why you are anaemic.

      Key points
      • Anaemia means haemoglobin below 13 g/dL in men, 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women, or 11 g/dL in pregnancy. About 3 in 10 Malaysian women aged 15 to 49 are affected.
      • Common symptoms are tiredness, breathlessness, a fast heartbeat, pale skin and headaches.
      • Iron deficiency is the most common cause, often from heavy periods or pregnancy. Men and women past the menopause with iron deficiency need their stomach and bowel checked.
      • Get tested before taking iron. Thalassaemia trait causes similar blood results, and needs different advice.
      • Take iron tablets once a day or on alternate days, away from tea, coffee and milk, and keep them out of children’s reach. An iron overdose can be fatal for children.

      What is anaemia?

      Red blood cells contain haemoglobin, which carries oxygen. In anaemia, haemoglobin (Hb) is too low. The World Health Organization’s cut-offs are:

      GroupAnaemia if haemoglobin is below
      Men13 g/dL
      Non-pregnant women12 g/dL
      Pregnant women11 g/dL (10.5 g/dL in the second trimester)

      In Malaysia, national health surveys have found anaemia in 21.3% of the population in 2019, including about 3 in 10 women aged 15 to 49, about 19% of pregnant women in 2022, and about a third of people aged 60 and over.

      “Kurang darah” isn’t the same as “darah rendah”. Darah rendah means low blood pressure. See our guide to low blood pressure.

      Symptoms

      • tiredness and lack of energy
      • shortness of breath, especially on exertion
      • a fast or pounding heartbeat
      • pale skin
      • headaches and dizziness
      • less commonly: brittle nails, hair loss, a sore tongue, restless legs, or craving ice or non-food items (pica)

      Mild anaemia may cause no symptoms and only show up on a blood test.

      Causes

      • Iron deficiency, the most common cause, from:
        • heavy periods (see our guide to period problems) and pregnancy
        • bleeding from the stomach or bowel, from ulcers, long-term NSAID painkillers, piles, or less often bowel or stomach cancer
        • a diet low in iron, or poor absorption, for example from coeliac disease, H. pylori infection, or long-term acid-reducing medicines
        • hookworm infection, especially in rural communities
      • Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, from diet, some medicines or pernicious anaemia
      • Thalassaemia, an inherited condition common in Malaysia. Carriers often have small red cells and mild anaemia, with normal iron levels. See our guide to thalassaemia screening.
      • Long-term conditions, including kidney disease and inflammatory diseases

      Tests

      • A full blood count shows your haemoglobin and the size of your red cells (MCV). See our guide to blood test results.
      • Ferritin is the most useful test for iron stores. Low ferritin confirms iron deficiency, but ferritin can look falsely normal during infection or inflammation, so doctors look at the whole picture.
      • Vitamin B12, folate and a thalassaemia test, if needed.

      Why it matters to find the cause: British gastroenterology guidelines advise that men and women past the menopause with confirmed iron deficiency anaemia should have their stomach and bowel examined by gastroscopy and colonoscopy, as bleeding can be the first sign of cancer. Women who still have periods usually don’t need this unless they have other symptoms. See our guide to bowel cancer.

      Iron tablets (ubat tambah darah)

      Get tested first. Taking iron without knowing the cause can hide the problem, and iron won’t correct anaemia caused by thalassaemia trait unless you are also low in iron.

      • Dose: usually one tablet once a day. If side effects are a problem, your doctor may suggest every other day or a different preparation. Current guidance has moved away from taking it 2 or 3 times a day.
      • How to take it: ideally on an empty stomach, 30 minutes before food or 2 hours after. Take it with food if it upsets your stomach.
      • Keep it 2 hours apart from tea, coffee, milk, eggs and antacids, which reduce absorption.
      • Side effects: constipation, diarrhoea, nausea and dark stools are common. Tarry black or red stools that may contain blood need a doctor straight away. See our guide to constipation.
      • How long: haemoglobin is usually rechecked within about 4 weeks. Keep taking iron for about 3 months after it returns to normal, to refill your stores.
      • Iron injections or drips are used if tablets aren’t tolerated or don’t work, or if iron is needed quickly.

      Keep iron tablets locked away from children. An overdose can be fatal for a young child.

      Iron-rich foods

      Diet helps prevent iron deficiency, but tablets are usually needed to treat it. Malaysia’s Recommended Nutrient Intakes list these sources:

      • Haem iron (best absorbed): meat, poultry and fish, including liver, lean beef, ikan bilis, cockles and tenggiri
      • Non-haem iron: leafy greens such as spinach, pucuk paku and kangkung, beans and chickpeas, tofu, eggs, and bread and fortified cereals
      • Eating vitamin C-rich fruit and vegetables, or some meat or fish, with a meal helps absorb non-haem iron.
      • Tea and coffee reduce absorption. In one study, waiting an hour after a meal halved the effect of tea, so have your teh or kopi between meals rather than with them.
      • Avoid liver in pregnancy, as it contains high levels of vitamin A.

      Pregnancy and children

      • Pregnancy: anaemia is linked to heavy bleeding at delivery, low birth weight and premature birth. Malaysian antenatal clinics give iron and folic acid routinely, free at government clinics. Take them as advised, even if they are unpleasant, and tell the clinic if side effects are a problem. See our guide to early pregnancy.
      • Babies and children: a 2022 national survey sample found anaemia in nearly half of children aged 6 to 59 months, although the estimate is uncertain. Anaemia is linked to poorer learning and development. Very large amounts of cow’s milk reduce iron absorption, so offer iron-rich foods. Ask a doctor before giving iron supplements.

      When to see a doctor

      See a doctor if you feel tired or breathless for no clear reason, have very heavy periods, or look pale.

      Go to an emergency department if you have black, tarry stools, vomiting blood, heavy bleeding, chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness. See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      Where to get a blood test

      A GP can check your blood count and iron levels, find out why you are anaemic, start treatment and refer you for further tests. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including PeKa B40 panel clinics.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a normal haemoglobin level?

      At least 13 g/dL in men, 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women and 11 g/dL in pregnancy, according to the World Health Organization. Labs may use slightly different ranges.

      What are the symptoms of anaemia?

      Tiredness, shortness of breath, a fast heartbeat, pale skin, headaches and dizziness.

      What causes iron deficiency?

      Heavy periods, pregnancy, bleeding from the stomach or bowel, a diet low in iron, or poor absorption.

      Is kurang darah the same as darah rendah?

      No. Kurang darah means anaemia. Darah rendah means low blood pressure.

      How long should I take iron tablets?

      Usually for about 3 months after your haemoglobin returns to normal, to refill your iron stores. Your doctor will check your progress.

      How should I take iron tablets?

      Once a day or on alternate days, ideally on an empty stomach, and 2 hours apart from tea, coffee, milk and antacids.

      Which foods are high in iron?

      Meat, liver, fish, ikan bilis and cockles, and plant foods such as spinach, pucuk paku, kangkung, beans and tofu.

      Should I take iron if I have thalassaemia trait?

      Only if tests show you are low in iron. Thalassaemia trait causes small red cells, but iron won’t correct it on its own.
