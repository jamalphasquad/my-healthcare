      HPV vaccine in Malaysia 2026: the school programme, catch-up options and adults

      The HPV vaccine prevents most cervical cancers, and Malaysia changed its school programme in 2026: girls now get one free dose in Year 6. Girls who missed it can still catch up at a government clinic, boys and adults can pay for it privately, and women who have been vaccinated still need cervical screening.

      Key points
      • From 2026, girls get the free HPV vaccine at school in Year 6 (age 12) instead of Form 1, and the programme now gives a single dose.
      • Budget 2027 replaces the bivalent (2-type) school vaccine with the nonavalent (9-type) vaccine.
      • Girls who missed the school programme can book a dose at a government clinic through MySejahtera.
      • The national programme doesn’t cover boys or adult women. They can be vaccinated at private clinics or through subsidised NGO programmes.
      • Women who have been vaccinated still need cervical screening, with an HPV test every 5 years from age 30.

      Why the HPV vaccine matters

      Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a very common virus spread through sexual contact. Most infections clear on their own. An infection with a high-risk type that lasts for years can cause cervical cancer, and some cancers of the throat, anus and genitals.

      Cervical cancer is one of the most common cancers in Malaysian women. WHO’s cancer agency estimates 1,968 new cases and 964 deaths a year, which makes it the third most common cancer in women. The National Cancer Registry counts only recorded cases and ranks it sixth. It also found that 47% of cases were diagnosed at stage 3 or 4, when treatment is harder.

      As part of its plan to eliminate cervical cancer, Malaysia aims to have 90% of girls vaccinated by age 15 by 2030.

      The school programme: what changed in 2026

      From 2026, the free vaccine is given to girls in Year 6 (age 12) rather than Form 1 (age 13). The Ministry of Health says the earlier date protects girls sooner, in line with WHO advice, and fewer girls are missed when they change school.

      The programme now gives a single dose. Malaysia started with three doses in 2010 and moved to two in 2015. In October 2026, WHO’s vaccine experts confirmed that one dose is the most efficient approach for girls aged 9 to 14.

      Schools currently use a bivalent vaccine, which protects against HPV types 16 and 18, the cause of most cervical cancers. Budget 2027 switches the school programme to a nonavalent (9-type) vaccine from 2027, for about 230,000 Year 6 girls.

      Parents or guardians must give written consent. The school sends home a consent form and an information leaflet about a week before vaccination day.

      If your daughter missed it

      A global vaccine shortage between 2020 and 2024 meant many Malaysian girls missed their school dose, and first-dose coverage fell from over 95% to about 35% in 2024. The Ministry ran a catch-up programme in schools for these girls in 2024 and early 2025.

      Any girl who missed the school programme, including girls in private schools and girls who are home-schooled, can book at a government health clinic through MySejahtera.

      In 2026 the National Cancer Society Malaysia (NCSM) also runs a subsidised programme for boys and girls aged 9 to 14, at RM290 per dose at partner clinics.

      HPV vaccine for boys and adults

      The national programme covers girls only, so boys and adults are vaccinated at private clinics and hospitals. In men, the vaccine helps protect against genital warts and some cancers, and it reduces the spread of the virus.

      The number of doses depends on your age and the vaccine. Under the nonavalent vaccine’s product label, people aged 9 to 14 usually need two doses, and people aged 15 and over usually need three over six months. People with weakened immune systems may need more. Your doctor will tell you which schedule to follow.

      WHO advises that catch-up vaccination should go first to young women up to age 25, then up to 30, and then to boys aged 9 to 14. The vaccine works best before any exposure to HPV, so older adults usually benefit less.

      How much does it cost?

      The school programme is free. News reports in 2026 put the price at private clinics at around RM500, depending on the vaccine and the clinic. NCSM’s subsidised programme for ages 9 to 14 charges RM290 per dose. Ask for the total cost of the full course before you start.

      You can claim vaccinations for yourself, your spouse and your children under the medical expenses tax relief for vaccinations.

      Vaccinated? You still need screening

      The vaccine doesn’t protect against every high-risk HPV type, and it doesn’t clear an infection you already have, so women should still be screened:

      • Aged 30 to 49: an HPV test, repeated every 5 years if negative. You can often take the sample yourself.
      • Aged 21 to 29 and 50 to 65: a Pap smear.

      Our guide to the Pap smear and HPV test explains what happens and what the results mean.

      Government health clinics screen for free. So does the Ministry of Health’s ROSE programme at government hospitals, and LPPKN’s Klinik Nur Sejahtera gives free HPV tests to eligible women aged 30 to 65. About 65% of women in the ROSE programme had not been screened in the previous three years.

      See a doctor if you notice
      • Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause
      • Unusual vaginal discharge
      • Pelvic pain or pain during sex

      Side effects and safety

      The HPV vaccine has been used worldwide for more than 15 years and has a strong safety record. The usual side effects are redness, swelling or pain where the needle went in. Mild fever, headache or joint pain are less common.

      Some young people faint after any injection, so you will be asked to stay seated for about 15 minutes afterwards. Severe allergic reactions are very rare. As a precaution, the vaccine isn’t recommended during pregnancy. If you become pregnant partway through a course, the remaining doses are given after the baby is born.

      Where to get an HPV vaccine or screening

      Girls covered by the national programme are vaccinated at school or at a government clinic. Boys, adults and anyone paying privately can ask at most GP clinics, which can also arrange cervical screening. Call ahead to check the clinic has the vaccine in stock.       Frequently asked questions

      Is the HPV vaccine free in Malaysia?

      Yes, for girls, through the school programme, which from 2026 vaccinates girls in Year 6. Girls who missed it can book at a government clinic through MySejahtera. Boys and adults pay at private clinics or use subsidised NGO programmes.

      How many doses of HPV vaccine do Malaysian schoolgirls get?

      One. The national programme moved to a single dose, which WHO says is effective for girls aged 9 to 14.

      Can adults get the HPV vaccine in Malaysia?

      Yes, at private clinics and hospitals. People aged 15 and over usually need three doses. Ask your doctor whether the vaccine is likely to help you.

      Can boys get the HPV vaccine in Malaysia?

      Yes, privately. The national programme covers girls only, but in 2026 the National Cancer Society Malaysia offered subsidised vaccination for boys and girls aged 9 to 14.

      Do I still need a Pap smear or HPV test if I had the vaccine?

      Yes. The vaccine doesn’t cover every HPV type that causes cancer. Women aged 30 to 49 should have an HPV test every 5 years, and Pap smears are used at ages 21 to 29 and 50 to 65.
