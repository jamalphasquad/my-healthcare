      HPV vaccine in Malaysia 2026: the school programme, catch-up options and adults

      The HPV vaccine prevents most cervical cancers, and Malaysia’s school programme changed in 2026. Here is who gets it free, what to do if your child missed it, options for boys and adults, and why screening still matters after vaccination.

      Key points
      • From 2026, girls get the HPV vaccine free in Year 6 (age 12) at school, instead of Form 1. The programme now gives a single dose.
      • Budget 2027 upgrades the school vaccine from the bivalent (2-type) vaccine to the nonavalent (9-type) vaccine.
      • Girls who missed the school programme can book at government clinics through MySejahtera.
      • Boys and adult women are not covered by the national programme, but can be vaccinated privately or through subsidised NGO programmes.
      • Vaccinated women still need cervical screening: an HPV test every 5 years from age 30.

      Why the HPV vaccine matters

      Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a very common virus spread through sexual contact. Most infections clear on their own, but long-lasting infection with high-risk types can cause cervical cancer, as well as some cancers of the throat, anus and genitals.

      Cervical cancer is one of the most common cancers in Malaysian women. WHO’s cancer agency estimates 1,968 new cases and 964 deaths a year, making it the third most common cancer in women. The National Cancer Registry, which counts recorded cases, ranks it sixth and found that 47% of cases were diagnosed at stage 3 or 4, when treatment is harder.

      Malaysia aims for 90% of girls to be vaccinated by age 15 by 2030, as part of its plan to eliminate cervical cancer.

      The school programme: what changed in 2026

      • Earlier: from 2026, the free vaccine is given to girls in Year 6 (age 12) instead of Form 1 (age 13). The Ministry of Health says this gives protection earlier, in line with WHO advice, and means fewer girls are missed when they change school.
      • One dose: Malaysia moved from three doses (2010) to two (2015) to a single dose. WHO’s vaccine experts confirmed in October 2026 that one dose is the most efficient approach for girls aged 9 to 14.
      • A broader vaccine from 2027: schools currently use a bivalent vaccine, which protects against HPV types 16 and 18, the cause of most cervical cancers. Budget 2027 upgrades this to a nonavalent (9-type) vaccine for about 230,000 Year 6 girls.

      Parents or guardians must give written consent. Schools send home the consent form and an information leaflet about a week before vaccination day.

      If your daughter missed it

      Between 2020 and 2024, a global vaccine shortage meant many Malaysian girls missed their school dose. First-dose coverage fell from over 95% to about 35% in 2024. The Ministry ran a catch-up programme for these cohorts in schools in 2024 and early 2025.

      Girls who missed the school programme, including those in private schools or home-schooled, can book at a government health clinic through MySejahtera.

      The National Cancer Society Malaysia (NCSM) also runs a subsidised programme in 2026 for boys and girls aged 9 to 14, at RM290 per dose at partner clinics.

      HPV vaccine for boys and adults

      The national programme covers girls only. Boys and adults can be vaccinated at private clinics and hospitals. HPV vaccination helps protect men against genital warts and some cancers, and reduces spread of the virus.

      How many doses you need depends on your age and the vaccine used. Under the product label of the nonavalent vaccine, people aged 9 to 14 usually need two doses, and people aged 15 and over usually need three doses over six months. People with weakened immune systems may need more doses. Your doctor will advise on the right schedule.

      WHO advises that catch-up vaccination should prioritise young women up to age 25, then up to 30, followed by boys aged 9 to 14. Vaccination works best before any exposure to HPV, so the benefit for older adults is usually smaller.

      How much does it cost?

      The school programme is free. At private clinics, news reports in 2026 said the HPV vaccine typically costs around RM500, depending on the vaccine and clinic, compared with RM290 per dose through NCSM’s subsidised programme for ages 9 to 14. Ask for the total cost of the full course before you start.

      Vaccination costs for yourself, your spouse and your children can be claimed under the medical expenses tax relief for vaccinations.

      Vaccinated? You still need screening

      The vaccine does not protect against every high-risk HPV type, and it does not clear an existing infection. Women should still be screened:

      • Aged 30 to 49: an HPV test, repeated every 5 years if negative. You can often take the sample yourself.
      • Aged 21 to 29 and 50 to 65: a Pap smear.

      Free options include government health clinics, the Ministry of Health’s ROSE programme at government hospitals, and free HPV tests for eligible women aged 30 to 65 at LPPKN’s Klinik Nur Sejahtera. About 65% of women in the ROSE programme had not been screened in the previous three years.

      See a doctor if you notice
      • Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause
      • Unusual vaginal discharge
      • Pelvic pain or pain during sex

      Side effects and safety

      The HPV vaccine has been used worldwide for more than 15 years and has a strong safety record. Common side effects are redness, swelling or pain at the injection site. Less common are mild fever, headache or joint pain.

      Some young people faint after any injection, so you will be asked to sit for about 15 minutes afterwards. Severe allergic reactions are very rare. The vaccine is not recommended during pregnancy as a precaution. If you become pregnant partway through a course, the remaining doses are given after delivery.

      Where to get an HPV vaccine or screening

      Girls covered by the national programme are vaccinated at school or at a government clinic. For boys, adults or private vaccination, most GP clinics can advise on the HPV vaccine and cervical screening. Call ahead to check they have the vaccine in stock.       Frequently asked questions

      Is the HPV vaccine free in Malaysia?

      Yes, for girls through the school programme, which from 2026 vaccinates girls in Year 6. Girls who missed it can book at a government clinic through MySejahtera. Boys and adults pay at private clinics, or can use subsidised NGO programmes.

      How many doses of HPV vaccine do Malaysian schoolgirls get?

      One. The national programme moved to a single-dose schedule, which WHO says is effective for girls aged 9 to 14.

      Can adults get the HPV vaccine in Malaysia?

      Yes, at private clinics and hospitals. Adults aged 15 and over usually need three doses. Ask your doctor whether vaccination is likely to benefit you.

      Can boys get the HPV vaccine in Malaysia?

      Yes, privately. The national programme covers girls only, but in 2026 the National Cancer Society Malaysia offered subsidised vaccination for boys and girls aged 9 to 14.

      Do I still need a Pap smear or HPV test if I had the vaccine?

      Yes. The vaccine does not cover every cancer-causing HPV type. Women aged 30 to 49 should have an HPV test every 5 years, and Pap smears are used at ages 21 to 29 and 50 to 65.
