      Pantang larang makanan pesakit buah pinggang: a kidney disease diet guide for Malaysians

      People with kidney disease are often given long lists of foods to avoid. In fact, what you need to limit depends on your stage of kidney disease, your blood test results and whether you are on dialysis. Cutting salt helps almost everyone. Other limits, on potassium, phosphate, protein or fluid, are only needed for some people, and the wrong restrictions can do harm. A dietitian can tailor the plan to you.

      Key points
      • There is no one “kidney diet”. Your limits depend on your stage, blood results and whether you are on dialysis, so ask for a dietitian referral.
      • Malaysia’s kidney guideline advises less than 2,400mg of sodium a day, about a teaspoon of salt. Soy sauce, budu and cencalok contain around 5g of sodium per 100g.
      • Only limit potassium if your blood tests show it is high. Never use potassium-based salt substitutes if you have kidney disease.
      • People not on dialysis are usually advised moderate protein (0.6 to 0.8g per kg of body weight a day), while people on dialysis need more (1.0 to 1.2g per kg).
      • Avoid star fruit (belimbing) and belimbing buluh if you have kidney disease: they can cause confusion, seizures and kidney injury.

      There is no one kidney diet

      Diet advice for kidney disease depends on:

      • your stage of chronic kidney disease. See our guide to kidney disease and its stages.
      • your blood test results, especially potassium and phosphate
      • whether you are on dialysis
      • other conditions, such as diabetes

      UK kidney specialists warn that a low-potassium diet is irrelevant for most people with early kidney disease, and that unnecessary limits can lead people to cut out healthy fruit and vegetables. So don’t follow a generic list of “pantang larang” without checking with your doctor or a dietitian.

      In a Malaysian study, only about a third of dialysis centres had access to a dietitian, so ask your doctor for a referral, especially at a government hospital.

      Salt: the change that helps almost everyone

      Malaysia’s kidney disease guideline advises less than 2,400mg of sodium a day, about one teaspoon of salt in total, including salt already in food. Malaysian adults eat about 3,400mg a day on average.

      Watch out for:

      FoodSodium per 100g (average, Malaysian survey)
      Budu and cencalokAbout 5,200mg
      Light soy sauce (kicap cair)About 5,100mg
      Salty soy sauceAbout 4,800mg
      Oyster sauceAbout 3,200mg

      Other high-salt foods include instant noodles, salted fish (ikan masin), salted eggs, pickles, sausages, luncheon meat and nuggets, canned soups, MSG, ready-made cooking pastes and chilli sauces.

      • Cook with herbs, spices, lime, garlic and ginger instead of extra salt or sauces.
      • Taste before adding sauce at the table.
      • Choose fresh rather than processed or preserved foods.

      Never use potassium-based salt substitutes (often sold as “low sodium” or “sodium-free” salt) if you have kidney disease, as they can raise your potassium dangerously.

      Potassium: only if your blood test is high

      Potassium only needs limiting if your doctor tells you your blood potassium is high, which is more common in advanced kidney disease and with some medicines. Both high and low potassium can affect the heart.

      Foods high in potassium include bananas, avocado, dried fruit, coconut and coconut products, potatoes and chips, tomato purée, mushrooms, spinach, fruit juices, chocolate and nuts. Many can still be eaten in smaller portions, for example half a banana.

      Reducing potassium in vegetables and potatoes: cut them into small pieces, soak them in plenty of water, boil them in fresh water and throw the cooking water away.

      Durian: it is high in potassium, and there have been reports of serious harm in people on dialysis after eating it. If you are on dialysis or have a high potassium level, ask your team before eating it.

      Phosphate and protein

      Phosphate is usually limited in later stages and on dialysis, when blood tests show it is high. High phosphate weakens bones and can harden blood vessels.

      • Phosphate added to processed food (look for “phos” in the ingredients) is absorbed almost completely, so processed meats, dark colas and some bottled drinks are the first things to cut.
      • Dairy, sardines, organ meats, nuts and beans are naturally high in phosphate.
      • If you are prescribed phosphate binders, take them with every meal and snack.
      GroupProtein per day
      Stages 3 to 5, not on dialysis (Malaysian guideline)0.6 to 0.8g per kg of body weight, with a dietitian
      Kidney disease caused by diabetesAbout 0.8g per kg
      On haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis1.0 to 1.2g per kg

      Too much protein can strain damaged kidneys, but too little can cause malnutrition, especially on dialysis, so don’t cut it drastically on your own. Plant proteins, such as tofu and lentils, may be easier on the kidneys. Avoid protein shakes and supplements unless your dietitian recommends them.

      How much should I drink?

      Most people with early kidney disease don’t need to limit fluids. A large trial found that drinking extra water didn’t slow kidney disease. Drinking more than you need won’t “flush” the kidneys.

      In advanced kidney disease and on dialysis, you may be given a daily fluid allowance, depending on how much urine you still pass. Cutting fluids when you haven’t been told to can be harmful. Eating less salt makes it easier to stick to a fluid limit, because salt makes you thirsty.

      With diabetes: control carbohydrates and sugar, as you would anyway. A good diabetes diet suits early kidney disease.

      Foods and remedies to avoid

      • Star fruit (belimbing besi): contains a toxin that people with kidney disease can’t clear. It can cause hiccups, confusion, seizures and death. Avoid it completely if you have kidney disease.
      • Belimbing buluh (bilimbi): a Sabah case report described acute kidney injury in a young man with healthy kidneys after eating about 1kg a day, from its oxalate content. People with kidney disease should avoid it, especially as juice or on an empty stomach.
      • Herbal remedies and supplements: in a Malaysian study, about two-thirds of dialysis patients used traditional or complementary medicines, and most didn’t tell their doctor. Herbs can build up, raise potassium, cause fluid overload and interact with medicines. Some herbs containing aristolochic acid cause kidney disease. Tell your doctor about everything you take.
      • Anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen and diclofenac, unless your doctor agrees. See our painkiller guide.

      Where to get help: ask your doctor for a dietitian referral. The Malaysian Dietitians’ Association has kidney-friendly recipe books, including resources from the National Kidney Foundation of Malaysia and Hospital Kuala Lumpur.

      Frequently asked questions

      What foods should kidney patients avoid?

      Most should cut salt and salty sauces, processed meats and instant noodles, and avoid star fruit and herbal remedies. Limits on potassium, phosphate, protein and fluids depend on your stage and blood tests.

      Can kidney patients eat bananas?

      Usually yes in early kidney disease. If your blood potassium is high, you may need smaller portions, such as half a banana, or to avoid them. Ask your doctor or dietitian.

      How much salt can a kidney patient have?

      Malaysia’s kidney guideline advises less than 2,400mg of sodium a day, about a teaspoon of salt in total.

      Should kidney patients drink a lot of water?

      Not necessarily. Most people with early kidney disease don’t need to change how much they drink, and extra water doesn’t protect the kidneys. People on dialysis may need to limit fluids.

      Why is star fruit dangerous for kidney patients?

      It contains a toxin that damaged kidneys can’t remove, which can cause hiccups, confusion, seizures and even death.

      Should kidney patients eat less protein?

      People with stage 3 to 5 kidney disease not on dialysis are usually advised moderate protein. People on dialysis need more protein. A dietitian can set the right amount.

      Can I use low-sodium salt?

      Not if it contains potassium chloride, which most do. It can raise your potassium dangerously.
