      Skin boosters in Malaysia: Profhilo, Rejuran, Juvelook and exosomes explained

      Skin boosters are one of the most searched aesthetic treatments in Malaysia. Here is what each type does, what the evidence says, what it costs, and how to make sure the doctor, clinic and product are all legal.

      Key points
      • “Skin booster” covers several different injectables: hyaluronic acid (such as Profhilo), polynucleotides (such as Rejuran) and collagen stimulators (such as Juvelook and Sculptra).
      • These products are regulated as medical devices. Check that a product is on the Medical Device Authority (MDA) register.
      • Only a registered doctor with a Letter of Credentialing and Privileging (LCP) may inject them, at an MOH-registered clinic.
      • No exosome product appears on the MDA register, and cosmetics in Malaysia are for use on the skin only, not injection.
      • Never have injections at homes, hotels or beauty salons. MOH calls this “checking the 3Ps”: procedure, practitioner and premises.

      What are skin boosters?

      “Skin booster” is a marketing term rather than a medical one. It usually means small amounts of an injectable placed just under the skin to improve hydration, texture, fine lines or firmness, rather than to add volume the way a lip or cheek filler does. The main types work quite differently:

      TypeExamplesHow it works
      Hyaluronic acid (HA)Profhilo, Restylane Skinboosters, NCTF 135 HAHyaluronic acid holds water in the skin, improving hydration and smoothness
      Polynucleotides (PN)RejuranDNA fragments said to support skin repair; mostly studied in Korea
      Collagen stimulatorsJuvelook (PDLLA), Sculptra (PLLA)Microparticles trigger the skin to build collagen gradually over months
      ExosomesVarious “exosome” serumsNot registered as an injectable; see below

      How many sessions, and how long do results last?

      • Hyaluronic acid boosters: in one study of Profhilo, women had two treatments four weeks apart, and improvement was still measurable at 16 weeks.
      • Polynucleotides: a 2024 review found most studies used three to four sessions about two weeks apart. The authors noted the evidence is still limited and of low-to-moderate quality.
      • Collagen stimulators: results build slowly. In the US trial for Sculptra, people had up to four sessions, and many still showed improvement two years later.

      Results vary between people, and most treatments need top-up sessions to maintain them. Be cautious of any promise of a fixed result or a fixed number of months.

      Is it legal? What Malaysian rules say

      Skin booster injectables are regulated in Malaysia as medical devices. Products sold here must be registered with the Medical Device Authority (MDA). As of October 2026, the MDA register lists products including Profhilo, Rejuran, Sculptra, Restylane Skinboosters and NCTF 135 HA. You can search it yourself on the MDA medical device register.

      The Ministry of Health lists filler injections as a minimally invasive aesthetic procedure. They may only be done by a registered doctor holding a Letter of Credentialing and Privileging (LCP), in premises registered under the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998.

      A note on exosomes: searching the MDA register for “exosome” returns no products. In Malaysia, cosmetics are legally for use on the outside of the body, so a cosmetic exosome serum is not approved for injection. Doctors have reported cases of skin damage and scarring after exosome products meant for the skin were injected.

      How to check the doctor, clinic and product (the 3Ps)

      The Ministry of Health urges the public to “check the 3Ps” before any aesthetic procedure:

      1. Practitioner: look the doctor up on the Malaysian Medical Council’s MeRITS register, and check MOH’s national registry of doctors with an LCP.
      2. Premises: the clinic should be registered with MOH. MOH publishes its list of registered private clinics, including those offering aesthetic services.
      3. Procedure and product: ask for the product’s name and check it on the MDA register. Make sure the procedure is one MOH allows. Breast and buttock fillers, for example, are illegal in Malaysia.
      Avoid injections at homes, hotels or salons

      In September 2026, raids in Shah Alam, Pandan Perdana and Kepong found unlicensed “therapists” offering aesthetic treatments in rented homes, promoted on TikTok and Instagram. Cheaper prices from unlicensed operators often mean unregistered products and no one to treat complications.

      Side effects and risks

      Common, usually short-lived effects include bruising, redness, swelling, tenderness and small bumps at the injection points, which typically settle within days to a week.

      Less common but important risks:

      • Lumps and nodules, particularly with collagen stimulators. In the Sculptra trial, 17% of patients developed nodules or bumps, sometimes appearing months later. Unlike hyaluronic acid, these cannot be dissolved with an enzyme.
      • Injection into a blood vessel. This is rare but serious, and can cause skin damage, vision loss or stroke. It is the main reason injections should only be done by trained doctors who can recognise and treat it immediately.
      • Infection and allergic reactions.

      Seek urgent medical care for severe or increasing pain, skin that turns pale, blue or dusky, or any change in your vision after an injection.

      Questions to ask at your consultation

      • Which product will you use, and is it registered with the MDA?
      • Do you hold an LCP for this procedure?
      • What results can I realistically expect, and how many sessions?
      • What are the risks for me, and what happens if there is a complication?
      • What is the total cost, including follow-up sessions?

      A careful doctor will review your medical history, medicines and allergies, explain alternatives, and give you time to decide. Be wary of pressure to book on the spot.

      How much do skin boosters cost?

      Prices depend on the product, the amount used and the number of sessions. In October 2026, clinic adverts in Malaysia commonly listed skin booster treatments from around RM500 to over RM3,000 per session. Ask for a written quote that includes all planned sessions. A much lower price can be a warning sign of unregistered products or an unlicensed provider.

      Frequently asked questions

      Are skin boosters safe?

      When a registered product is injected by a trained, LCP-holding doctor in a registered clinic, side effects are usually mild and short-lived, such as bruising and swelling. Serious complications are rare but possible, which is why the doctor and clinic matter.

      Is exosome injection approved in Malaysia?

      No exosome product appears on the Medical Device Authority register, and cosmetics in Malaysia are legally for use on the skin, not injection. Ask your doctor exactly what product they plan to use and check its registration.

      How long do skin boosters last?

      It depends on the type. Hyaluronic acid boosters are usually repeated every few months, while collagen stimulators work gradually and can last longer. Most people need top-up sessions.

      Can a beautician do skin booster injections in Malaysia?

      No. Filler injections are a medical procedure in Malaysia. They may only be done by a registered doctor with a Letter of Credentialing and Privileging (LCP), in an MOH-registered clinic.

      How much is a skin booster in Malaysia?

      In October 2026, clinic adverts commonly listed skin boosters from around RM500 to over RM3,000 per session, depending on the product and number of sessions. Ask for a written quote.
