      Chickenpox (cacar air): symptoms, stages, how long it’s contagious, and the vaccine

      Chickenpox (cacar air) is a very contagious virus that causes an itchy, spotty rash. Most children recover within a week or two, but it can be more serious for adults, pregnant women, newborns and people with weak immunity. The vaccine isn’t part of Malaysia’s free programme, so many children still catch it.

      Key points
      • Chickenpox is caused by the varicella-zoster virus. Spots appear 10 to 21 days after contact, usually around 2 weeks.
      • It is contagious from 1 to 2 days before the rash until every blister has crusted over, usually about 5 days after the spots appear. Stay home until then.
      • Use paracetamol for fever, and calamine, antihistamines and cool baths for itch. Don’t give ibuprofen unless a doctor advises it, and never give aspirin to children.
      • Adults, pregnant women, newborns and people with weak immunity should see a doctor early, as antiviral medicine works best within 24 hours of the rash.
      • The chickenpox vaccine is given as 2 doses from age 12 months. It isn’t in the free National Immunisation Programme, but private clinics offer it.

      What is chickenpox?

      Chickenpox is caused by the varicella-zoster virus, a member of the herpes family. It is also known in Malay as cacar air, and in some places as jintungan or bertih. It spreads very easily through coughs and sneezes, and by touching or breathing in virus from the fluid in the blisters.

      Most people only get chickenpox once and are then immune. But the virus stays in the body, and can wake up years later as shingles (kayap).

      Chickenpox symptoms and rash stages

      Symptoms appear 10 to 21 days after contact, usually about 2 weeks. A mild fever, aches, tiredness and loss of appetite often come a day or two before the rash. Then the spots go through stages:

      1. Red spots appear, often first on the face, scalp, chest, tummy or back, then spread over the body, sometimes inside the mouth.
      2. The spots fill with fluid to become very itchy blisters, which break within a day or two.
      3. The blisters dry into scabs, which fall off over 1 to 2 weeks.

      New spots keep appearing for a few days, so you often see spots, blisters and scabs at the same time. Chickenpox is usually milder in children and more severe in teenagers and adults, in whom pneumonia is the most common serious complication. In children, the most common complication is a bacterial skin infection from scratching.

      How long is chickenpox contagious?

      A person with chickenpox can spread it from 1 to 2 days before the rash appears until all the blisters have crusted over, usually about 5 days after the spots first appear.

      Keep your child home from nursery, kindergarten (tadika) or school, and stay off work yourself, until every spot has crusted. Keep away from pregnant women, newborn babies and people with weak immunity during this time.

      Home care: easing the itch and fever

      • Paracetamol for fever and discomfort.
      • Don’t give ibuprofen or other anti-inflammatory painkillers unless a doctor advises it, as they may raise the risk of serious skin infections with chickenpox. Never give aspirin to anyone under 16.
      • Calamine lotion or cooling gels on the spots, and antihistamines from a pharmacy to reduce itching and help sleep.
      • Bathing is fine and helps. A cool or lukewarm bath soothes itching. Pat the skin dry rather than rubbing.
      • Keep nails short, and put socks or mittens on young children’s hands at night to stop scratching, which can cause infection and scars.
      • Drink plenty of fluids, and wear loose cotton clothes.

      There is no need to follow special food restrictions (pantang). A normal, varied diet and plenty of fluids are best.

      When to see a doctor

      See a doctor within 24 hours of the rash appearing if you are an adult or teenager, pregnant, have a weakened immune system, or the person is a newborn baby. Antiviral medicine (aciclovir) can make chickenpox milder, but works best when started within 24 hours of the rash.

      Get urgent medical help if

      the skin around the blisters becomes red, hot, swollen or very painful; there is difficulty breathing or chest pain; a child is very drowsy, confused, has a stiff neck or a fit, or is showing signs of dehydration; or a newborn baby has chickenpox. Call 999 or go to an emergency department.

      Chickenpox in pregnancy

      Most women are already immune from childhood chickenpox. If you aren’t, chickenpox in pregnancy can cause pneumonia in the mother, and infection in the first 20 weeks carries a small risk (about 1–2%) of birth defects affecting the baby’s limbs, eyes or brain. Chickenpox around the time of delivery can make a newborn seriously ill.

      If you are pregnant and have been near someone with chickenpox or shingles, and you aren’t sure you have had chickenpox, see your doctor straight away. A blood test can check your immunity, and preventive treatment may be offered. Don’t wait for a rash.

      The chickenpox vaccine in Malaysia

      • The chickenpox (varicella) vaccine is not part of Malaysia’s free National Immunisation Programme, but private clinics and hospitals offer it.
      • It is given as 2 doses, from age 12 months. Ask your doctor about the interval between doses for your child’s age. Teenagers aged 13 and over and adults who have never had chickenpox also need 2 doses.
      • Two doses are about 90% effective at preventing chickenpox, and those who still catch it usually have a much milder illness.
      • Given within 3 to 5 days of contact with chickenpox, the vaccine can prevent or reduce illness in people who aren’t immune.
      • Healthcare workers who aren’t immune are advised to be vaccinated.

      Paediatricians in Malaysia have called for the vaccine to be added to the national programme. Some countries have recently added it to their routine schedules, including the UK from January 2026. See also our baby vaccination schedule.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are the first signs of chickenpox?

      A mild fever, tiredness, aches and loss of appetite, followed within a day or two by red spots that turn into itchy, fluid-filled blisters, often starting on the face, chest or back.

      How long is chickenpox contagious?

      From 1 to 2 days before the rash appears until all the blisters have crusted over, usually about 5 days after the spots first appear.

      Can I bathe with chickenpox?

      Yes. A cool or lukewarm bath helps the itch. Pat the skin dry gently afterwards.

      Can I give ibuprofen for chickenpox?

      It is best to avoid ibuprofen unless a doctor advises it, as it may raise the risk of serious skin infections. Use paracetamol for fever, and never give aspirin to children.

      Is the chickenpox vaccine free in Malaysia?

      No. It isn’t in the National Immunisation Programme, but private clinics and hospitals offer it, given as 2 doses from age 12 months.

      Can you get chickenpox twice?

      It is uncommon. Most people are immune after one infection, but the virus stays in the body and can return later as shingles (kayap).

      Is chickenpox dangerous in pregnancy?

      It can be, if you aren’t immune. See a doctor straight away if you are pregnant and have been exposed, as preventive treatment may be offered.
