      Umrah and Haj vaccines for Malaysians (vaksin umrah): meningitis jab, certificates and health checks

      Saudi Arabia requires pilgrims to be vaccinated against meningococcal meningitis, and the Malaysian Ministry of Health says Malaysian Haj and Umrah pilgrims must have the jab. It needs to be done at least two weeks before you travel, and the certificate has to show the right details. Here is what to arrange, and how to look after your health on the journey.

      Key points
      • Saudi Arabia requires a meningococcal ACWY vaccine for Umrah and Haj pilgrims, given at least 10 days before arrival. The Malaysian Ministry of Health advises having it at least two weeks before departure.
      • A conjugate vaccine is valid for 5 years and a polysaccharide vaccine for 3 years. The certificate must show the vaccine name and date. If the type isn’t shown, it counts as valid for 3 years only.
      • Saudi Arabia also requires COVID-19 vaccination proof from Umrah pilgrims aged over 65, pregnant women and people with certain chronic conditions, and advises a flu vaccine for all.
      • Haj pilgrims must pass a health exam before receiving their offer. Saudi rules exclude people with conditions such as kidney failure on dialysis, advanced dementia, or late or high-risk pregnancy.
      • Heat is one of the biggest dangers. More than 1,300 people died of heat-related causes during Haj 2024. Drink regularly, avoid direct sun and ask your doctor about any water tablets you take.

      Which vaccines do you need for Umrah and Haj?

      These are Saudi Arabia’s published requirements for the 1447H (2026) season, the latest available at the time of writing. Check for updates before you travel, especially for Haj 2027.

      VaccineRequirementApplies to
      Meningococcal ACWYRequired. At least 10 days before arrival.All pilgrims
      COVID-19Proof required for some Umrah pilgrims. Saudi Haj rules refer to “specific groups”.For Umrah: over-65s, pregnant women and people with listed chronic conditions

      Polio and yellow feverOnly for travellers from listed countriesMalaysia isn’t on these lists

      In Malaysia: in May 2024 the Ministry of Health said Malaysian pilgrims “are required to take meningococcal vaccines” for both Haj and Umrah. For the 2025 Haj season, the ministry also made COVID-19 and flu vaccines compulsory for pilgrims aged 65 and over or at high risk. Requirements for 2027 hadn’t been confirmed when this guide was written, so check with Tabung Haji or your Umrah agency.

      The meningitis jab: timing and your certificate

      Meningococcal disease spreads easily in crowds and can be fatal: about 1 in 6 people with bacterial meningitis die. The World Health Organization reported 11 confirmed cases linked to Umrah between January and March 2025. None of those people had been vaccinated.

      When to have it: Saudi rules say at least 10 days before arrival. The Malaysian Ministry of Health advises at least two weeks before departure. Following the Malaysian advice meets both, and allows time for your body to build protection.

      Vaccine typeAccepted for
      Conjugate (ACWY or ACWYX)5 years
      Polysaccharide3 years
      Type not shown on the certificate3 years only

      Your certificate should:

      • clearly show the vaccine name and the date you had it
      • have your name spelled as in your passport
      • be an official vaccination certificate, not just a payment receipt

      Keep it with your passport and carry it on your trip. Saudi rules differ in how they describe checks for each country, and the WHO advises pilgrims to carry proof. Ask your agency how they will check it. If your earlier vaccine is still within its valid period, you don’t need another dose, as long as your certificate shows this.

      Where to get the vaccine in Malaysia

      • Umrah: the Ministry of Health said in 2024 that Umrah pilgrims should get the vaccine at private healthcare facilities. Many private GP clinics offer it, but stock varies, so call first and ask for the vaccine type and whether a certificate is included.
      • Haj: vaccination is available at government facilities or privately. In the 2025 season, the COVID-19 vaccine was free at selected clinics, and the flu vaccine was free at selected government facilities for pilgrims aged 60 and over with a chronic illness. Check the current arrangements with Tabung Haji.

      Prices at private clinics vary, so ask what is included. Allow several weeks if you are going in a busy period.

      The Haj health exam

      Malaysian Haj pilgrims must pass a health examination before they can receive their offer. In recent seasons:

      • the exam is done at premises registered with Tabung Haji, and is free at government facilities
      • you bring your MyKad and offer letter
      • your status is shown by SMS, THiJARI or MySejahtera
      • people who don’t meet the standard are advised to postpone

      Conditions that Saudi Arabia says should not travel (1447H rules):

      • kidney failure needing dialysis
      • heart failure with symptoms on minimal effort
      • lung disease needing oxygen
      • cirrhosis with liver failure
      • severe neurological or psychiatric disorders that affect thinking, and dementia
      • pregnancy in the last trimester, or high-risk pregnancy
      • active infections such as open tuberculosis
      • active cancer on chemotherapy, or severe immune-suppressing treatment

      Stable high blood pressure and diabetes don’t automatically rule you out, but get them under control well before the exam. Haj 2027 is expected around mid-May 2027, subject to the sighting of the moon, with the first flights from Malaysia around April.

      Other vaccines to consider

      • Influenza: strongly recommended by the US CDC for pilgrims. Respiratory infections are very common during Haj.
      • Pneumococcal: pneumonia is among the most common reasons pilgrims are admitted to hospital. CDC advice covers those aged 65 and over and younger people with chronic illness. Ask your doctor.
      • Hepatitis B: worth considering, as sharing razor blades at the end of the pilgrimage carries a risk. Use only licensed barbers and a new blade.
      • Tetanus: check whether you are up to date.

      Side effects, pregnancy and who should check first

      • Common side effects: a sore or red arm, muscle pain, headache and tiredness, usually lasting a day or two. Serious reactions are rare.
      • Mild illness generally doesn’t stop you being vaccinated. If you have a fever or are moderately unwell, ask your doctor whether to wait.
      • Don’t have the vaccine if you have had a life-threatening allergic reaction to a previous dose or to one of its ingredients.
      • Pregnancy: speak to your doctor. Saudi authorities have advised that pregnant women, children, frail older people and those seriously ill consider postponing.
      • Children: Saudi rules don’t set a minimum age, and travel health guidance covers those aged 1 and over. Ask your doctor about the right vaccine for your child.

      Staying well on your trip

      • Heat: temperatures can reach 50°C. Drink fluids regularly, avoid direct sun and rest in the shade. If you take water tablets (diuretics), ask your doctor before you go whether they need adjusting. Know your temperature numbers and danger signs.
      • “Haj cough”: coughs and chest infections spread easily. Wash your hands, and wear a mask in crowds. See a doctor for fever, breathlessness or a cough that is getting worse.
      • MERS: avoid camels and raw camel products. About 35% of people with this infection die. Seek medical advice if you develop a fever or cough within 14 days of returning, and tell the doctor you have been to Saudi Arabia.
      • Medicines: carry them in their original packaging, with documents listing your conditions and treatment. Malaysian Haj pilgrims have also been registered for an International Patient Summary through MySejahtera.
      • Diabetes: pack your medicines, syringes, glucose meter and fast-acting carbohydrate. Ask your doctor how to adjust doses for long walking days.
      • Feet: long walks can cause blisters. People with diabetes or numb feet should wear protective footwear and cover any sore.
      • Tummy upsets: keep to safe food and drink, and see our guide to diarrhoea (cirit birit).

      Find a clinic for your vaccine

      Many GP clinics give the meningitis vaccine for Umrah, and can also review your medicines before you travel. Call first to check that the vaccine is in stock and that a certificate is provided. Our directory lists GP clinics across Malaysia, including clinics open at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is the meningitis vaccine compulsory for Umrah from Malaysia?

      Yes. Saudi Arabia requires it for pilgrims, and the Malaysian Ministry of Health said in 2024 that Malaysian Haj and Umrah pilgrims are required to have it.

      How long before Umrah should I get the vaccine?

      At least two weeks before you leave, as advised by the Malaysian Ministry of Health. Saudi Arabia’s minimum is 10 days before arrival.

      How long is the meningitis vaccine valid for Umrah?

      A conjugate vaccine is accepted for 5 years and a polysaccharide vaccine for 3 years. If the certificate doesn’t show the vaccine type, it is accepted for 3 years only.

      Can I get the Umrah vaccine at a Klinik Kesihatan?

      The Ministry of Health said in 2024 that Umrah pilgrims should go to private healthcare facilities. Haj pilgrims can be vaccinated at government facilities.

      Do I need a flu vaccine for Umrah?

      Saudi Arabia advises it, and it is especially worthwhile for older people and those with chronic illness. Proof isn’t listed as a requirement for Umrah.

      Can I perform Umrah while pregnant?

      Speak to your doctor first. Saudi Haj rules exclude pregnancy in the last trimester or high-risk pregnancy, and authorities have advised pregnant women to consider postponing.

      What is “Haj cough”?

      A common term for coughs and chest infections that spread among pilgrims in crowded conditions. See a doctor for fever, breathlessness or a cough that is getting worse.
