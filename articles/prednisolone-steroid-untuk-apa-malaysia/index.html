      Prednisolone untuk sakit apa? What this steroid treats, how to take it safely, and side effects

      Prednisolone is a corticosteroid, a steroid medicine that reduces inflammation and calms an overactive immune system. Doctors prescribe it for many conditions, from asthma attacks to arthritis. It works well, but it has side effects, especially with long courses, and it must not be stopped suddenly after more than a few weeks. It isn’t the same as the anabolic steroids some people misuse for bodybuilding.

      Key points
      • Prednisolone treats inflammation and immune conditions, including asthma and COPD flare-ups, severe allergies and hives, eczema, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, inflammatory bowel disease, gout flares and some kidney conditions.
      • Take it in the morning with or after breakfast. Don’t stop suddenly if you have taken it for more than 3 weeks or at a high dose. Your doctor will reduce the dose gradually.
      • Short courses usually cause only mild side effects such as a bigger appetite, poor sleep, mood changes and higher blood sugar. Long-term use can weaken bones, raise blood pressure and increase the risk of infection.
      • Avoid anti-inflammatory painkillers unless your doctor agrees, as together they raise the risk of stomach bleeding. Avoid live vaccines while taking it.
      • The Ministry of Health has found prednisolone and dexamethasone hidden in unregistered traditional products. Check for a MAL number and hologram before buying any remedy.

      Prednisolone untuk sakit apa? What it treats

      Prednisolone is used for conditions where inflammation or the immune system is causing harm, including:

      • Breathing: flare-ups of asthma and COPD
      • Allergy and skin: severe allergic reactions and hives, severe eczema flares, psoriasis, and blistering skin conditions
      • Joints and muscles: rheumatoid arthritis, polymyalgia rheumatica, and gout flares when other painkillers aren’t suitable
      • Immune conditions: lupus, vasculitis and sarcoidosis
      • Bowel: ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease
      • Kidneys: nephrotic syndrome, particularly in children
      • Nerves: Bell’s palsy, ideally started within 72 hours
      • Other uses: preventing transplant rejection, Addison’s disease, and as part of some cancer treatments

      Courses range from a few days, for an asthma attack, to a year or more, for polymyalgia rheumatica. Prednisolone is a prescription medicine. Never take tablets prescribed for someone else.

      How to take prednisolone

      • Take it once a day in the morning, with or after breakfast, unless your doctor says otherwise. This reduces stomach upset and sleep problems.
      • Swallow the tablets whole with water.
      • Don’t stop suddenly if you have taken it for more than 3 weeks or at a high dose. Your body stops making its own steroid hormones while you take it, so the dose is lowered gradually. Stopping too fast can cause weakness, fever, muscle pain and dangerously low blood pressure.
      • Carry a steroid card if you take it for more than 3 weeks, and tell any doctor, dentist or nurse treating you. People who depend on daily steroids may need a higher dose during serious illness or surgery.
      • Short courses of 3 weeks or less are unlikely to cause long-term side effects, and can usually be stopped as prescribed.

      Side effects

      Common, especially with short courses:

      • a bigger appetite and weight gain
      • difficulty sleeping, mood changes, irritability or anxiety
      • indigestion, nausea or headache
      • higher blood sugar, and fluid retention

      With long-term or high-dose use:

      • a rounder face and weight gain (Cushing’s syndrome)
      • weaker bones, and slowed growth in children
      • more infections
      • high blood pressure and diabetes. See our blood sugar guide.
      • cataracts and glaucoma
      • thin skin, easy bruising and slow wound healing

      Get medical help urgently if you have:

      • black, tarry stools, or vomit with blood in it
      • signs of infection, such as a fever or sore throat
      • severe mood changes, low mood, or thoughts of harming yourself. About 5 in 100 people have serious mental health effects.
      • contact with chickenpox, shingles or measles, if you haven’t had chickenpox, while taking prednisolone or within 3 months of stopping. See our guides to chickenpox and shingles.
      • blurred vision

      Other medicines, pregnancy and children

      • Anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen and diclofenac raise the risk of stomach ulcers and bleeding when taken with prednisolone. Ask your doctor before taking them. See our painkiller guide.
      • Diabetes medicines: prednisolone raises blood sugar, so you may need to check it more often or adjust your treatment.
      • Warfarin, some antibiotics, liquorice, and traditional or herbal medicines can interact. Tell your doctor and pharmacist everything you take.
      • Vaccines: avoid live vaccines, such as MMR, chickenpox, BCG, yellow fever and oral typhoid, while taking prednisolone. Non-live vaccines such as flu are safe.
      • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: prednisolone can be used if needed, and is usually fine while breastfeeding. Your doctor will weigh up the benefits and risks.
      • Children can take it, but long-term use may slow growth, so it is monitored.

      Hidden steroids in unregistered products

      Steroids are controlled medicines in Malaysia and should only be used with a doctor’s prescription. Dexamethasone and prednisolone are never allowed in traditional medicines. Yet the Ministry of Health has repeatedly found them hidden in unregistered products:

      • In December 2025, the ministry warned that an unregistered powder sold online contained dexamethasone, prednisolone and diclofenac, with risks including kidney damage, diabetes, high blood pressure and hormonal problems.
      • In 2016, unregistered traditional products sold for joint, back and muscle pain were found to contain dexamethasone, with reports of Cushing’s syndrome (“moon face”) and liver failure.
      • In May 2026, Singapore’s health authority reported people hospitalised with kidney injury, Cushing’s syndrome and adrenal failure after taking pain relief products laced with steroids.

      A warning sign: a remedy that brings unusually fast relief for long-standing pain or skin problems may contain a hidden steroid. Before buying any remedy, check for a MAL registration number and the hologram security label, and search it on NPRA’s website. If you have been taking an unregistered product for a long time, don’t stop it suddenly. See a doctor, as your body may need the steroid dose reduced gradually.

      Frequently asked questions

      Prednisolone 5mg untuk sakit apa?

      Prednisolone treats inflammation and immune conditions such as asthma flare-ups, severe allergies, eczema, arthritis, lupus, bowel inflammation and gout flares. The dose depends on the condition, and 5mg tablets are combined to make up the prescribed dose.

      Is prednisolone a painkiller?

      Not exactly. It reduces inflammation, which can ease pain in conditions such as arthritis or gout, but it isn’t a general painkiller.

      Should I take prednisolone in the morning or at night?

      In the morning, with or after breakfast. Taking it at night can cause sleep problems.

      Can I stop prednisolone suddenly?

      Not if you have taken it for more than 3 weeks or at a high dose. Your doctor will lower the dose gradually. Short courses can usually be stopped as prescribed.

      Does prednisolone cause weight gain?

      It often increases appetite, and long-term use can cause weight gain and a rounder face. Short courses usually have a smaller effect.

      Can I take ibuprofen with prednisolone?

      Only if your doctor agrees, because together they raise the risk of stomach ulcers and bleeding. Paracetamol is usually safer.

      How do I know if a product has hidden steroids?

      You can’t tell by looking. Buy only registered products with a MAL number and hologram, and be suspicious of remedies that work unusually fast for long-term pain.
