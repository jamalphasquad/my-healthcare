      Pap smear and HPV test in Malaysia: who needs one, how often, what to expect and free options

      A Pap smear and the newer HPV test check the cervix (the neck of the womb) for changes that could turn into cancer years later. Both take only a few minutes. Yet fewer than half of Malaysian women aged 30 to 65 have been screened recently. Here is who needs it, what to expect, and how to get it free or at low cost.

      Key points
      • Cervical cancer is the 3rd most common cancer in Malaysian women, with about 2,000 new cases and nearly 1,000 deaths a year. Almost all cases are caused by the human papillomavirus (HPV).
      • Ministry of Health guidance: sexually active women aged 20 to 65 should have a Pap smear yearly for 2 years, then every 3 years if the results are normal.
      • The HPV DNA test is more sensitive. It is recommended from age 30, and if it is negative you usually don’t need another for 5 years. You can often take the sample yourself.
      • Pap smears are free at government health clinics, and free HPV tests are available to eligible women through LPPKN and Program ROSE.
      • Women who have had the HPV vaccine still need screening.

      Why cervical screening matters

      Cervical cancer develops slowly. Long-lasting infection with high-risk types of HPV, a very common virus spread through sexual contact, can cause abnormal cells on the cervix. Over 10 to 15 years, some of these can turn into cancer. Screening finds the virus or the abnormal cells early, when simple treatment prevents cancer from developing.

      • International estimates for 2024 put cervical cancer as the 3rd most common cancer in Malaysian women, with 1,968 new cases and 964 deaths.
      • Screening coverage is improving but still low: the National Health and Morbidity Survey found that 43.2% of women aged 30 to 65 had been screened in 2023, up from 23.1% in 2019.
      • Many cases in Malaysia are still found at a late stage, when treatment is harder.

      Who needs a Pap smear or HPV test, and how often?

      TestWhoHow often
      Pap smearWomen aged 20 to 65 who have ever been sexually activeYearly for the first 2 years, then every 3 years if normal
      HPV DNA testWomen aged 30 and over (MOH guidance: 30 to 49; LPPKN and Program ROSE offer it to 30 to 65)Every 5 years if negative

      Under the Ministry of Health’s HPV testing guideline, women aged 21 to 29 and 50 to 65 are still advised to have Pap smears. The ministry is gradually moving towards HPV testing as the main screening test, so ask the clinic which one suits you.

      • You still need screening if you have had the HPV vaccine, are in a long-term relationship, or haven’t had sex for some years.
      • If you have never had any sexual contact, your risk is very low. Discuss with your doctor whether you need screening.
      • After a hysterectomy that removed the cervix for a non-cancer reason, you usually don’t need screening. Check with your doctor if you aren’t sure.
      • Over 65, you can usually stop if your recent results have been normal.
      • If you are pregnant, tell the doctor or nurse. Routine screening is often postponed until after the birth.

      Pap smear vs HPV test: what’s the difference?

      • A Pap smear (cervical cytology) looks at cells from the cervix under a microscope for abnormal changes. Malaysia has mostly moved from the conventional smear on a glass slide to liquid-based cytology, where the sample is rinsed into a vial of liquid, which gives clearer results.
      • An HPV DNA test looks for the high-risk HPV types that cause almost all cervical cancers. It picks up risk earlier than a Pap smear, so a negative result means you can safely wait 5 years. The sample can be taken by a doctor or nurse, or by you with a soft swab (self-sampling).

      If your HPV test is positive, the same sample or a new one is usually checked with cytology to decide the next step.

      What happens during a Pap smear

      1. You undress from the waist down and lie on the examination bed with your knees bent. You can ask for a female doctor or nurse, or for someone to be with you.
      2. The doctor or nurse gently inserts a speculum (sometimes called mulut itik, “duck’s beak”) into the vagina to see the cervix.
      3. A small soft brush is used to collect cells from the cervix. This takes a few seconds.

      The test itself takes less than 5 minutes, and the visit about 10 minutes. It can feel uncomfortable but shouldn’t be painful. Tell the doctor if it hurts. You can ask them to stop at any time, or to use a smaller speculum. Light spotting afterwards is normal.

      How to prepare:

      • Book the test for a time when you aren’t having your period, ideally in the middle of your cycle.
      • For 24 to 48 hours before, avoid sex, and don’t use vaginal creams, pessaries, lubricants or douches.

      Understanding your results

      ResultWhat it meansUsual next step
      Normal / HPV negativeNo abnormal cells or high-risk HPV foundRoutine screening (3 years for Pap, 5 years for HPV)
      UnsatisfactoryThe sample couldn’t be readRepeat the test, usually within 3 months
      ASC-US or LSILMild cell changes, usually caused by HPV and often clearing by themselvesRepeat test, often in 1 year, or colposcopy
      ASC-H or HSILMore serious cell changes that could become cancer if untreatedColposcopy
      HPV 16 or 18 positiveThe two highest-risk HPV typesColposcopy
      Other high-risk HPV positiveA high-risk type other than 16 or 18Cytology on the sample, then a repeat test in 1 year or colposcopy

      An abnormal result doesn’t mean you have cancer. Most HPV infections clear within 1 to 2 years. A colposcopy is a closer look at the cervix with a magnifying instrument, done in a clinic. If abnormal cells are found, they can be removed with a quick procedure, such as LLETZ (LEEP), which usually prevents cancer.

      Where to get a free or low-cost Pap smear or HPV test

      • Government health clinics (Klinik Kesihatan): Pap smears have been free at government facilities since 1998, and some clinics offer HPV testing.
      • LPPKN (Klinik Nur Sejahtera): free HPV DNA self-sampling tests for eligible women aged 30 to 65 at participating clinics, under a government subsidy.
      • Program ROSE: HPV self-sampling for women aged 30 to 65, free or subsidised depending on nationality and income, with results sent to your phone. It runs with partner hospitals and clinics.
      • PERKESO Health Screening Programme: eligible contributors aged 40 and over can have a Pap smear as part of the free screening. See our guide to free health screening in Malaysia.
      • Private GP and women’s clinics: you can usually be seen quickly. In January 2026, The Star reported typical private prices of around RM40 for a Pap smear and around RM250 for an HPV DNA test. Prices vary, so ask when you book.

      The HPV vaccine and screening

      Malaysia has vaccinated schoolgirls against HPV since 2010, with coverage above 90%. From 2026 the school programme moved from Form 1 to Year 6 girls. The vaccine prevents most, but not all, cancer-causing HPV infections, and it doesn’t clear an infection you already have. That is why vaccinated women still need screening. See our guide to the HPV vaccine in Malaysia.

      Symptoms that need a doctor

      Screening is for women without symptoms. Don’t wait for your next test if you have:

      • bleeding between periods, after sex, or after the menopause
      • unusual or smelly vaginal discharge
      • pain in the pelvis or during sex

      These are usually caused by something less serious, but they should be checked. Women should also be breast aware: see our guide to breast cancer screening.

      Where to get screened

      Many GP clinics offer Pap smears and HPV tests, often with a female doctor available if you ask. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open at weekends. Call ahead to check which tests they offer.

      Frequently asked questions

      How often should I have a Pap smear in Malaysia?

      If you are 20 to 65 and have been sexually active, yearly for the first 2 years, then every 3 years if the results are normal. If you have an HPV test and it is negative, the next one is usually in 5 years.

      Is a Pap smear painful?

      It can be uncomfortable but shouldn’t be painful, and takes less than 5 minutes. Tell the doctor if it hurts. You can ask them to stop, or to use a smaller speculum.

      What is the difference between a Pap smear and an HPV test?

      A Pap smear looks for abnormal cells on the cervix. An HPV test looks for the high-risk virus that causes those changes, so it picks up risk earlier and is repeated less often.

      Where can I get a free Pap smear?

      At government health clinics. Eligible women aged 30 to 65 can also get a free HPV test through LPPKN or Program ROSE, and PERKESO contributors aged 40 and over can have a Pap smear in its free health screening.

      How much is a Pap smear at a private clinic?

      Prices vary. In January 2026 The Star reported around RM40 for a Pap smear and around RM250 for an HPV DNA test at private clinics.

      Can I have a Pap smear during my period?

      It is best to book for a time when you aren’t having your period, as blood can make the sample harder to read.

      Do I need a Pap smear if I’ve had the HPV vaccine?

      Yes. The vaccine doesn’t protect against every cancer-causing HPV type, so vaccinated women still need regular screening.

      Does an abnormal Pap smear mean cancer?

      No. Most abnormal results are mild changes caused by HPV that often clear on their own. Some need a closer look (colposcopy) and simple treatment to stop them turning into cancer.
