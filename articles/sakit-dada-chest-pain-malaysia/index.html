      Sakit dada sebelah kiri, kanan atau tengah: chest pain causes and when to call 999

      Sakit dada means chest pain. Many causes are not serious, such as a strained muscle, heartburn or a panic attack. But heart disease is the leading cause of death in Malaysia, and heart attack pain isn’t always on the left. Doctors advise treating chest pain as heart-related until proven otherwise. If you are unsure, call 999.

      Key points
      • Heart attack pain is usually in the centre of the chest, not just the left, and can spread to the jaw, either arm, the back or the upper tummy. It often comes with sweating, nausea or breathlessness.
      • Women, older people and people with diabetes may have only breathlessness, tiredness, dizziness or sweating, without obvious chest pain.
      • Call 999 for chest pain that is sudden, severe, doesn’t go away, or comes with sweating, breathlessness or feeling faint. Don’t drive yourself.
      • Malaysia’s heart attack guideline advises chewing one 300mg aspirin tablet straight away if you aren’t already taking aspirin and aren’t allergic.
      • Pain that gets worse when you press on your chest or move is more likely to be muscular. Burning after meals suggests heartburn. But only tests can rule out the heart.

      Is it a heart attack?

      Ischaemic heart disease is the leading cause of death in Malaysia, causing 17,421 deaths in 2024. It is the top cause of death in men and in people aged 41 to 59. Malaysians tend to have heart attacks about 6 years younger than people in international studies.

      Typical heart attack pain, according to Malaysia’s heart attack guideline:

      • usually in the centre of the chest
      • feels like pressure, squeezing, tightness or crushing, and may burn like indigestion
      • may spread to the jaw, neck, either arm, the back or the upper tummy
      • lasts more than a few minutes and doesn’t go away with rest
      • often comes with sweating, nausea, breathlessness or feeling faint

      It isn’t always in the chest. Older people, women and people with diabetes may have only tiredness, breathlessness, dizziness, sweating or fainting. In a Malaysian study, patients who were women, had unusual symptoms or went to a GP first took longer to reach hospital.

      See our guide to heart attack and stroke.

      What to do if you think it’s your heart

      1. Call 999 or get someone to take you to the nearest hospital emergency department. Don’t drive yourself.
      2. Aspirin: Malaysia’s heart attack guideline advises chewing and swallowing one 300mg aspirin tablet straight away, if you aren’t already taking aspirin regularly and aren’t allergic to it. Plain aspirin is preferred. Don’t give aspirin to children.
      3. Use your own heart medicines, such as a GTN spray, as your doctor has advised.
      4. Sit down, rest and stay with someone until help arrives.

      Every minute counts, because treatment works best in the first hours. Hospitals do a heart tracing (ECG) within minutes of arrival, and repeat blood tests (troponin) to look for heart damage.

      Chest pain on the left, right or centre

      WherePossible causes
      Centre (tengah)Heart attack or angina, heartburn and GERD, pericarditis, aortic dissection
      Left (kiri)Heart, muscle strain, costochondritis, pleurisy, pneumonia, collapsed lung, shingles, panic attack
      Right (kanan)Muscle strain or broken rib, pneumonia, pleurisy, pulmonary embolism, collapsed lung, gallstones, shingles

      Location doesn’t rule out the heart. Heart pain can spread to the right arm as well as the left, and sometimes isn’t in the chest at all. Don’t assume right-sided or central pain is safe.

      Other serious causes

      • Pulmonary embolism (a blood clot in the lung): sudden breathlessness, pain that is worse when you breathe in, coughing up blood, and sometimes a swollen, painful calf. The risk is higher after long journeys of 4 hours or more, and after a hospital stay.
      • Aortic dissection (a tear in the main artery): sudden, severe, tearing pain in the chest or upper back, sometimes with fainting or stroke-like weakness. Uncontrolled high blood pressure is a major risk.
      • Collapsed lung (pneumothorax): sudden sharp pain on one side with breathlessness, most common in tall, slim young men and smokers.
      • Pericarditis: sharp, stabbing pain that is worse when lying down, breathing in or swallowing, and eases when you lean forward.
      • Pneumonia and pleurisy: sharp pain when you breathe in or cough, with fever and coloured phlegm. See our guide to pneumonia.

      All of these need emergency care.

      Common, less serious causes

      • Muscle strain or costochondritis (inflamed rib cartilage): sharp pain at the front or side of the chest that gets worse when you move, breathe deeply or press on the area, often after exercise or lifting.
      • Heartburn or reflux: burning behind the breastbone after eating or when lying down, with a sour taste, regurgitation or bloating.
      • Anxiety or a panic attack: sharp chest pain with worry, a fast heartbeat, sweating and fast breathing. See our guides to anxiety and palpitations.
      • Shingles: burning or tingling on one side of the chest, followed a few days later by a blistering rash. Antiviral medicine works best within 3 days of the rash. See our shingles guide.
      • Gallstones: pain under the right ribs or in the upper tummy, sometimes felt in the lower chest, lasting more than 30 minutes.

      These clues can help, but don’t diagnose yourself. Heart pain can feel like heartburn, and people having a heart attack often feel anxious. If you have risk factors such as diabetes, high blood pressure, smoking or a family history of heart disease, get checked.

      Chest pain in children and young people

      Chest pain in children is rarely due to the heart. It is most often caused by the chest wall muscles or cartilage, asthma, pneumonia, reflux or anxiety. See a doctor promptly if a child has chest pain:

      • during exercise, or with fainting or near-fainting
      • that doesn’t go away
      • with a heart condition or past Kawasaki disease
      • with a family history of sudden unexplained death or heart muscle disease

      When to call 999

      Call 999 for chest pain that:

      • is sudden and doesn’t go away, or lasts more than 10 minutes
      • feels like pressure, squeezing, tightness or burning
      • spreads to your arm, neck, jaw, back or stomach
      • comes with sweating, nausea, breathlessness, light-headedness or fainting
      • is sudden, severe and tearing, or comes with coughing up blood or a swollen calf

      See a doctor the same day for chest pain that comes and goes with exercise and settles with rest, which can be angina, or for any chest pain you are unsure about. See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      Find a clinic for a heart check

      For chest pain now, call 999 or go to an emergency department, not a clinic. For a check-up of your heart risk, such as blood pressure, cholesterol, sugar and an ECG, a GP can help. Our directory lists clinics across Malaysia.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is sakit dada in English?

      Chest pain. Sebelah kiri means the left side, sebelah kanan the right side, and tengah the centre.

      Is left-sided chest pain always the heart?

      No. It is often muscular or caused by heartburn or anxiety. But heart attack pain is usually central and can spread to either side, so you can’t tell at home. Call 999 if unsure.

      Can right-sided chest pain be a heart attack?

      Yes, it is possible. Heart pain can spread to the right arm, and right-sided pain can also be serious lung problems such as a blood clot. Get urgent help if it is severe or comes with breathlessness.

      Should I take aspirin for chest pain?

      Malaysia’s heart attack guideline advises chewing one 300mg aspirin tablet straight away for a suspected heart attack, if you aren’t already taking aspirin and aren’t allergic. Call 999 first.

      How do I know if chest pain is muscular?

      Muscular pain usually gets worse when you press on the area, move or breathe deeply, often after exercise or lifting. But only tests can rule out the heart.

      Can gastric pain feel like chest pain?

      Yes. Reflux causes burning behind the breastbone, especially after meals. But heart attack pain can also feel like indigestion, so don’t assume.

      When should I go to hospital for chest pain?

      Straight away for sudden, severe or lasting chest pain, or chest pain with sweating, breathlessness, nausea or fainting. Call 999 and don’t drive yourself.
