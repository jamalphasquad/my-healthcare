      Kencing berdarah (blood in urine): causes, tests and when it could be serious

      Kencing berdarah means blood in the urine (haematuria). Sometimes you can see it, and sometimes it is found only on a urine test. A urine infection is the most common cause, but stones, an enlarged prostate, kidney conditions and cancers can all cause bleeding. That’s why doctors advise getting it checked, even if it happens only once and stops on its own.

      Key points
      • Blood in the urine can look pink, red or dark brown like weak tea. Beetroot, dragon fruit, food dyes and some medicines can colour urine red without any blood.
      • A urine infection is the most common cause. Others include kidney stones, an enlarged prostate, kidney inflammation, injury, hard exercise and blood thinners.
      • Always get visible blood in the urine checked, even if it stops. About 1 in 5 adults with visible blood in the urine is found to have bladder cancer.
      • In a Malaysian hospital study, the most common causes found were an enlarged prostate, stones and urine infections, and no cause was found in about 1 in 5 people.
      • Get help urgently for lots of blood or clots, being unable to pass urine, or blood with fever, back or side pain, or vomiting.

      What does blood in urine look like?

      • Visible blood (gross haematuria): urine that looks pink, red, or dark brown like weak black tea. Sometimes there are clots.
      • Non-visible blood (microscopic haematuria): urine looks normal, but blood is found on a dipstick or under a microscope. A positive dipstick is usually confirmed with a laboratory test.

      Red urine that isn’t blood:

      • Foods: beetroot, dragon fruit, blackberries, rhubarb and food dyes
      • Medicines: rifampicin, used to treat tuberculosis, and nitrofurantoin, used for urine infections
      • Blood from somewhere else: a period, or bleeding from the vagina or back passage that mixes with urine

      If you have eaten dragon fruit or beetroot, the colour should clear within about 48 hours. If it doesn’t, or you haven’t eaten any of these foods, see a doctor.

      Causes of kencing berdarah

      • Urinary tract infection (UTI): the most common cause, usually with burning, needing to pass urine often, or tummy pain
      • Kidney or bladder stones (batu karang): often with severe pain in the side that spreads to the groin
      • Enlarged prostate: common in older men
      • Kidney inflammation (glomerulonephritis), including IgA nephropathy. This often causes visible blood during a sore throat or stomach upset, and mainly affects young adults. See our guide to kidney disease.
      • Injury to the kidneys or bladder, or vigorous exercise
      • Blood-thinning medicines such as warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, aspirin and clopidogrel, and blood disorders
      • Infections such as TB, and schistosomiasis, caught from fresh water in some parts of Africa, Asia and the Middle East
      • Cancer of the bladder, kidney or prostate

      Could it be cancer?

      Most blood in the urine isn’t cancer, but it is an important early warning sign of bladder and kidney cancer. UK urologists report that about 1 in 5 adults with visible blood in the urine, and about 1 in 12 with non-visible blood, are found to have bladder cancer.

      In a study of 390 patients at a Malaysian university hospital, the causes found were an enlarged prostate (23%), stones (12%) and urine infections (11%). No cause was found in 22%. New bladder cancer was found in about 5 in 100 people with visible blood. The authors recommend fully investigating visible blood in the urine, especially over the age of 50.

      Higher risk:

      • Smoking: smokers have up to 4 times the risk of bladder cancer, and smoking causes about half of bladder cancers in the UK. It also increases the risk of kidney cancer.
      • age over 60, and being male
      • long-term exposure to certain industrial chemicals
      • past schistosomiasis

      UK guidance advises urgent investigation for anyone aged 45 or over with visible blood in the urine and no infection, or blood that continues or comes back after an infection is treated. In Malaysia, bladder and kidney cancer were each diagnosed in about 2,000 men between 2017 and 2021.

      What tests to expect

      At the clinic:

      • questions about your symptoms, smoking, work and medicines
      • a physical examination and blood pressure check
      • a urine test for infection, and urine microscopy
      • blood tests for kidney function, blood count and clotting, and a PSA test for men where appropriate

      If an infection is found and treated, the urine is often tested again 6 to 12 weeks later to make sure the blood has gone.

      Specialist tests (urologist):

      • Ultrasound of the kidneys and bladder
      • CT urogram: a detailed scan with a contrast injection. Mention any allergies or kidney problems.
      • Cystoscopy: a thin camera passed into the bladder. A flexible cystoscopy takes about 10 to 15 minutes with numbing gel, and you can usually go home straight afterwards. A little blood and stinging for a few days afterwards is normal.
      • Urine cytology: looking for abnormal cells in the urine

      On blood thinners? You still need to be checked. These medicines can make an underlying problem bleed, but they rarely cause bleeding on their own. Don’t stop them without speaking to your doctor.

      Blood in urine in children and pregnancy

      Children: cancer is rare. Common causes are urine infections, kidney inflammation, viral illness, injury and stones. One important cause is post-infectious glomerulonephritis, most often at ages 5 to 12, which appears 1 to 2 weeks after a strep throat or a few weeks after a skin infection. Urine looks tea- or cola-coloured, and there may be puffy eyelids in the morning. See a doctor promptly, and urgently if your child is passing less urine or is swelling up.

      In newborn babies, orange or pink “brick dust” in the nappy is usually urate crystals rather than blood, but ask your doctor if you are unsure.

      Pregnancy: a urine infection is a common cause of blood in the urine in pregnancy, and needs treatment. Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy should also be checked. Call 999 for heavy bleeding, severe pain or feeling faint.

      When to get help

      Go to an emergency department or call 999 if:

      • there is a lot of blood, or large clots
      • you can’t pass urine
      • you have blood in your urine with fever, shivering, pain in your back or side, or vomiting
      • you have severe tummy pain, or feel faint

      See a doctor within a few days for any blood you can see in your urine, even if it has stopped, or if blood has been found on a urine test. Don’t ignore it just because it is painless.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is kencing berdarah in English?

      Blood in the urine, or haematuria. It can be visible, or found only on a urine test.

      Is blood in urine always serious?

      Not always. It is often caused by an infection or a stone. But it can be an early sign of bladder or kidney cancer, so always get it checked.

      Can dragon fruit turn urine red?

      Yes. Dragon fruit, beetroot and some food dyes can make urine pink or red for up to about 48 hours. If the colour lasts longer, see a doctor.

      Should I worry if the blood stopped by itself?

      You should still see a doctor. Bleeding from a bladder or kidney tumour can come and go.

      Can a UTI cause blood in urine?

      Yes, a urine infection is the most common cause. Your urine should be retested after treatment to make sure the blood has gone.

      What test will the urologist do?

      Usually an ultrasound or CT scan of the kidneys, and a cystoscopy, which is a camera check of the bladder.

      I take blood thinners. Do I still need checking?

      Yes. Blood thinners can make an underlying problem bleed, so you still need investigating. Don’t stop them without advice.
