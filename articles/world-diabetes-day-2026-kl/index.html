      World Diabetes Day 2026: how and where to get your blood sugar checked in KL

      Two in five Malaysian adults with diabetes don’t know they have it. This year’s World Diabetes Day, on Saturday 14 November, is about acting early. Here is which blood sugar test to ask for, what the results mean under Malaysian guidelines, and where to get tested in Kuala Lumpur, often for free.

      Key points
      • World Diabetes Day is on Saturday 14 November 2026. The theme for 2026–27 is “Early action on diabetes”.
      • 15.6% of Malaysian adults have diabetes, and two in five of them don’t know it.
      • Malaysia diagnoses diabetes at an HbA1c of 6.3% or above, lower than the 6.5% used in many other countries. 5.7% to 6.2% is prediabetes.

      This year’s theme: early action

      The International Diabetes Federation has chosen “Early action on diabetes” as the World Diabetes Day theme for 2026 and 2027. It focuses on finding diabetes sooner and lowering the risk for people who don’t have it yet. Globally, about 1 in 9 adults has diabetes, and up to half already have complications by the time they are diagnosed.

      In Malaysia, the National Health and Morbidity Survey (NHMS) 2023 found diabetes in 15.6% of adults, about 3.6 million people. Two in five didn’t know they had it, and more than half of those who did know didn’t have their blood sugar under control. On 6 October 2026, the Health Minister told Parliament that more than 80% of adults aged 18 to 29 with diabetes are undiagnosed. We covered those figures in undiagnosed diabetes and high blood pressure in young Malaysians.

      Who should get tested

      Malaysia’s Clinical Practice Guidelines recommend a blood sugar test for everyone from age 30, repeated every year if normal. Get tested earlier, at any adult age, if you:

      • have a BMI over 23 (lower than the international overweight cut-off of 25, because Asians tend to develop diabetes at lower weights)
      • have a parent, brother or sister with diabetes
      • had diabetes in pregnancy
      • have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), heart disease, or abnormal cholesterol
      • are mostly inactive, or take medicines such as long-term steroids

      If you have prediabetes, you should be tested every year.

      Which test to ask for

      TestHow it worksFasting?
      HbA1cShows your average blood sugar over the last 2–3 monthsNo
      Fasting plasma glucoseBlood sugar after an overnight fastYes, at least 8 hours
      Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)Blood sugar before and 2 hours after a 75 g glucose drinkYes; plain water only, rest during the test
      Finger-prick (capillary) glucoseA quick screen with a glucometerEither

      HbA1c is the most convenient, because you don’t need to fast. It is less reliable if you are anaemic, have kidney disease, take iron supplements or steroids, or have a haemoglobin variant such as thalassaemia trait. Your doctor may use a glucose test instead.

      A finger-prick test at a pharmacy, event or at home is only a first screen. If it reads 5.6 mmol/L or higher fasting, or 7.8 mmol/L or higher at any time, see a doctor for a proper blood test.

      What your results mean

      These are the cut-offs in Malaysia’s Clinical Practice Guidelines (2020), for blood taken from a vein:

      TestNormalPrediabetesDiabetes
      HbA1cBelow 5.7%5.7% to 6.2%6.3% or above
      Fasting glucoseBelow 6.1 mmol/L6.1 to 6.9 mmol/L7.0 mmol/L or above
      2 hours after glucose drink (OGTT)Below 7.8 mmol/L7.8 to 11.0 mmol/L11.1 mmol/L or above
      Random glucose——11.1 mmol/L or above, with symptoms

      Malaysia uses an HbA1c of 6.3% to diagnose diabetes, lower than the 6.5% used in many countries. Local research found 6.5% would miss too many Malaysians with diabetes.

      If you have no symptoms, diabetes is diagnosed only after two abnormal results, either two different tests on the same blood sample or the same test on two occasions. One abnormal result is enough if you also have symptoms such as thirst, passing a lot of urine, or weight loss.

      If you have prediabetes

      Prediabetes means your blood sugar is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetes range. This is where early action works best. In a large US trial, a lifestyle programme cut the number of people with prediabetes who went on to develop type 2 diabetes by 58%, and by 71% in people over 60.

      Malaysian guidelines recommend:

      • Losing at least 7% of your body weight if you are overweight. For a 75 kg person, that is about 5 kg.
      • At least 150 minutes of activity a week, such as brisk walking for 30 minutes on five days.
      • Cutting sweet drinks and refined carbohydrates. Fill half your plate with vegetables, a quarter with protein and a quarter with whole grains, following the Suku-Suku Separuh guide.
      • A blood sugar test every year. Your doctor may also suggest medicine if your risk is high.

      Free and low-cost tests in Kuala Lumpur

      • Klinik Kesihatan: government health clinics offer free blood sugar, blood pressure, BMI and cholesterol checks under the National Health Screening Initiative. Book in the MySejahtera app under Appointments, then NHSI. Your results appear in the app. More than 2.65 million people have been screened since 2022.
      • PeKa B40: free screening, including blood sugar, for Sumbangan Tunai Rahmah recipients aged 40 and above, and their spouses. Check your eligibility with your IC number on the PeKa B40 website.
      • PERKESO Health Screening Programme: a free, one-time screening for PERKESO contributors aged 40 to 59, booked through the SEHATi app.
      • Diabetes Malaysia’s KL event: Diabetes Malaysia lists a World Diabetes Day state event in Kuala Lumpur on Saturday 14 November, 9am to 4pm. The venue had not been announced at the time of writing. Call Diabetes Malaysia on 03-7957 4062 for details.

      For more on these programmes, see free health screening in Malaysia 2026. Any GP clinic can also do an HbA1c or fasting glucose test.

      When high blood sugar is an emergency

      Very high blood sugar can cause diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or a hyperosmolar state (HHS). Both are medical emergencies, and they are sometimes the first sign of undiagnosed diabetes, especially in older people.

      Go to an emergency department or call 999 if someone has
      • Vomiting or stomach pain with high blood sugar
      • Fast or deep breathing, or a fast heartbeat
      • Fruity-smelling breath
      • Drowsiness or confusion
      • Extreme thirst and passing a lot of urine, with weakness

      Don’t drive yourself. See our guide to clinic or emergency department.

      Where to get a blood sugar test

      Any GP clinic can do an HbA1c or fasting glucose test and explain your results. Ask whether you need to fast before you go. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      When is World Diabetes Day 2026?

      Saturday 14 November 2026. The theme for 2026 and 2027 is “Early action on diabetes”.

      What HbA1c level means diabetes in Malaysia?

      An HbA1c of 6.3% or above, confirmed by a second abnormal result if you have no symptoms. An HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.2% is prediabetes.

      Do I need to fast for a diabetes test?

      Not for an HbA1c test. For a fasting glucose test or an oral glucose tolerance test, fast for at least 8 hours beforehand. Plain water is fine.

      Can a glucometer diagnose diabetes?

      No. A finger-prick reading is a first screen only. If it is high, a doctor will confirm with a blood test from a vein, such as fasting glucose, HbA1c or an OGTT.

      Where can I get a free diabetes test in KL?

      At government health clinics (book through MySejahtera under NHSI), through PeKa B40 if you are an STR recipient aged 40+, or through PERKESO’s screening programme if you are a contributor aged 40–59.

      Can prediabetes be reversed?

      Often, yes. Losing at least 7% of body weight and doing 150 minutes of activity a week can bring blood sugar back to normal and greatly lowers the risk of type 2 diabetes.
