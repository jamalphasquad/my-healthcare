      Slipped disc and sciatica (sakit pinggang): symptoms, what helps, and red flags

      A slipped disc happens when the soft centre of one of the cushions between the bones of the spine bulges out and presses on a nerve. In the lower back this causes sciatica: pain, tingling or numbness running down the buttock and leg. It can be very painful, but most people get better within weeks to a few months by staying active, with time and simple treatment.

      Key points
      • A slipped (herniated or prolapsed) disc doesn’t actually slip: its soft centre bulges through the tough outer layer. Many people have disc bulges on a scan without any pain.
      • Sciatica is pain, tingling or numbness down the back of one leg, often worse than the back pain, and worse with coughing or sneezing.
      • Most people improve within a few weeks to a few months. Keep moving: bed rest doesn’t help.
      • Scans usually aren’t needed at first. Physiotherapy and exercise are the main treatments, with injections or surgery for severe pain that doesn’t improve.
      • Go to an emergency department for numbness around the bottom or genitals, problems passing urine or controlling the bladder or bowels, or weakness in both legs.

      What is a slipped disc?

      Between each bone of the spine is a disc, a cushion with a tough outer ring and a soft, jelly-like centre. In a slipped disc (also called a herniated or prolapsed disc), the centre bulges or pushes through the outer ring. It most often happens in the lower back, at the two lowest levels, and less often in the neck.

      It only causes symptoms if it presses on or irritates a nerve. Disc bulges are very common with age: in one large review of scans, they were seen in 30% of 20-year-olds and 84% of 80-year-olds with no back pain at all. So a bulge on a scan doesn’t always explain pain.

      Low back pain is very common in Malaysia. A 2023 review of Malaysian studies found high rates among nurses and drivers in particular, linked to age, weight, heavy lifting, posture and long working hours.

      Symptoms of a slipped disc and sciatica

      • lower back pain, sometimes sudden after lifting or bending
      • sciatica: pain in the buttock and down the back of one leg, sometimes into the foot. It can be sharp, shooting or burning, and the leg pain is often worse than the back pain.
      • tingling, pins and needles or numbness in the leg or foot
      • weakness in the leg or foot
      • pain that is worse when coughing, sneezing or sitting for a long time

      A slipped disc in the neck causes neck pain with pain, tingling or weakness down the arm and into the hand.

      Red flags: when to go to hospital

      Go to an emergency department straight away (don’t drive yourself) if you have back pain with:

      • numbness or tingling around your bottom, genitals or inner thighs (saddle numbness)
      • difficulty passing urine, or loss of control of your bladder or bowels
      • sciatica in both legs, or severe or worsening weakness in your legs or feet
      • back pain after a serious fall or accident

      These can be signs of cauda equina syndrome, where the nerves at the bottom of the spine are badly squashed. It needs emergency surgery to prevent permanent damage.

      See a doctor soon for back pain with fever, unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, a swelling in the back, or pain that is worse at night or doesn’t ease with rest. See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      What helps

      • Stay active. Keep moving and carry on with daily activities as much as you can. Bed rest doesn’t speed recovery and can slow it.
      • Avoid sitting or lying still for long. Get up and walk regularly.
      • Heat, such as a heat pack or warm shower, can ease muscle spasm.
      • Gentle exercise and stretches, and return to work as soon as you can, with adjustments if needed.

      Painkillers: the UK’s NICE guideline advises against paracetamol on its own for low back pain, as it doesn’t help much. Anti-inflammatory painkillers (NSAIDs) can help back pain at the lowest dose for the shortest time, though the benefit for sciatica is limited. Avoid long-term strong opioid painkillers. NICE also advises against nerve-pain medicines such as gabapentin and pregabalin for sciatica, as there is no overall evidence of benefit and some evidence of harm. Discuss options with your doctor.

      Physiotherapy, injections and surgery

      • Scans: an MRI usually isn’t needed in the first weeks unless there are red flags. It is used if symptoms don’t improve, or if an injection or surgery is being considered.
      • Physiotherapy and exercise are the main treatment. Hands-on therapy such as manipulation or massage may help as part of an exercise programme, not on its own. Back belts and corsets aren’t recommended.
      • Epidural steroid injection: an injection near the nerve may be considered for severe, acute sciatica.
      • Surgery (usually a microdiscectomy, removing the part of the disc pressing on the nerve) is considered when severe leg pain doesn’t improve with other treatment, or urgently for red flags. In a major trial, early surgery relieved leg pain faster, but after a year outcomes were similar to waiting, and most people in both groups had recovered.

      In Malaysia, care usually starts with a GP, who can refer you to physiotherapy at a government health clinic or hospital, or to an orthopaedic or spine specialist.

      Be cautious with unlicensed bone-setting or forceful manipulation for a suspected slipped disc. A Malaysian orthopaedic surgeon has warned that patients have been made worse, with a risk of nerve damage. See a doctor first.

      Preventing back problems

      • Lift with your legs: bend your knees, keep the load close to your body, and avoid twisting.
      • Take regular breaks from sitting and driving, and use back support when seated.
      • Stay active and keep to a healthy weight. See our BMI calculator.
      • Stop smoking, which is linked to disc problems.

      Workers injured at work may be covered by PERKESO, which runs rehabilitation and return-to-work services.

      Where to get back pain checked

      A GP can examine you, check for red flags, advise on pain relief, issue an MC if needed and refer you for physiotherapy, a scan or a specialist. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is a slipped disc?

      A bulge of the soft centre of a spinal disc through its outer layer, which can press on a nerve. It is also called a herniated or prolapsed disc.

      What are the signs of a slipped disc?

      Back pain with pain, tingling or numbness running down one leg (sciatica), often worse when coughing, sneezing or sitting.

      How long does a slipped disc take to heal?

      Most people improve within a few weeks to a few months without surgery.

      Should I rest in bed with a slipped disc?

      No. Staying as active as you can helps more than bed rest. Avoid sitting or lying still for long periods.

      Do I need an MRI for sciatica?

      Usually not at first. An MRI is used if there are red flags, if symptoms don’t improve, or before an injection or surgery.

      When does a slipped disc need surgery?

      When severe leg pain or weakness doesn’t improve with other treatment, or urgently for red flags such as bladder or bowel problems.

      When is back pain an emergency?

      With numbness around the bottom or genitals, problems passing urine or controlling the bladder or bowels, weakness in both legs, or after a serious accident.

      Is massage or bone-setting good for a slipped disc?

      Unlicensed bone-setting or forceful manipulation can make things worse. Hands-on therapy may help as part of an exercise programme from a qualified physiotherapist.
