      Klinik Kesihatan in Malaysia: waktu operasi, RM1 fees, appointments and services

      A Klinik Kesihatan is a government health clinic run by the Ministry of Health. It is the first stop for most public healthcare in Malaysia, from coughs and diabetes care to antenatal check-ups and baby vaccinations. Here is how the opening hours work, what you pay, how to book, and when another kind of clinic may suit you better.

      Key points
      • Malaysia had 1,131 Klinik Kesihatan at the end of 2024, along with Klinik Komuniti, rural clinics and maternal and child health clinics.
      • Malaysians pay RM1 to see a doctor and RM5 to see a specialist. People aged 60 and over are treated free as outpatients, and other groups may be exempt.
      • Most Klinik Kesihatan keep government office hours. About 49 have extended hours on weekday evenings and Saturday mornings, and a few are also open on Sundays and public holidays.
      • You can book through MySejahtera for many services, but walk-ins are still accepted. Booked patients are prioritised, and fever or infectious cases don’t need an appointment.
      • Non-citizens can attend any government clinic but pay much more, listed by the ministry at RM40 for general outpatient care and RM120 for a specialist, with tests charged extra.

      What is a Klinik Kesihatan?

      A Klinik Kesihatan (KK) is a primary care clinic run by the Ministry of Health (KKM). Ministry data to December 2024 lists 1,131 Klinik Kesihatan, 1,656 rural clinics (Klinik Desa), 205 Klinik Komuniti and 77 maternal and child health clinics. Klinik Kesihatan range from very large clinics seeing more than 800 patients a day to small ones seeing fewer than 50.

      Waktu operasi: when are Klinik Kesihatan open?

      Hours vary by clinic, so check your own clinic before you go. We couldn’t find one current national list.

      • Ordinary hours: Klinik Kesihatan generally keep government office hours on working days. Klinik Kesihatan Jalan Masjid, for example, opens from 8am to 5pm on weekdays.
      • Kedah, Kelantan and Terengganu have a Friday and Saturday weekend, so check which days your clinic is open.
      • Johor returned to a Saturday and Sunday weekend from 1 January 2025.
      • Extended hours (waktu lanjutan): 49 clinics were running extended hours in March 2025, usually on weekdays from 5pm to 9.30pm and on Saturdays from 9am to 1pm. Hours differ between clinics, and some close at 9pm.
      • Sundays and public holidays: six pilot clinics also opened on these days as of March 2025: Bandar Botanik, Ampang, Taman Ehsan, Kuala Lumpur, Greentown and Mahmoodiah.
      • Penang, October 2026: some clinics in Seberang Jaya and Bayan Baru open on weekday evenings and Saturday mornings, KK UTC Komtar and KK Penang Sentral open at weekends, and eight Klinik Komuniti open daily from noon to 9pm.

      If your Klinik Kesihatan is closed and you need a doctor, a private clinic may be open. See our lists of clinics open on Sunday and 24-hour clinics.

      Other groups that may be exempt, according to ministry statements and hospital notices, include:

      • government staff, with a letter
      • students or children under 21, with a school letter
      • babies under 1 year old
      • antenatal and postnatal mothers
      • people registered as OKU or receiving welfare help
      • people with infectious diseases, and blood donors

      Sources differ on the exact rules for children, so bring your documents and ask at the counter. For non-citizens, the fee covers registration, consultation and medicine, and laboratory tests and other services cost extra. Permanent residents and foreign spouses with a Malaysian marriage certificate have been listed as paying RM5 for a specialist.

      What services can you get?

      • general outpatient care for common illness
      • diabetes, high blood pressure and other long-term conditions
      • antenatal and postnatal care
      • baby and child vaccinations
      • family planning and contraception
      • mental health care and help to stop smoking
      • pre-employment, study, Haj and pre-marital health checks
      • dental care, at clinics with a dental service
      • prescription refills through MyUBAT, including delivery or drive-through collection at some clinics

      Not every clinic offers every service. PeKa B40 health screening is carried out at registered private clinics, according to the government portal.

      Temujanji: how to book

      1. Update the MySejahtera app.
      2. Open Appointments, then choose Book Appointment.
      3. Choose the service.
      4. Choose the clinic. The app shows clinics near you.
      5. Pick a date and time, then confirm.

      Can you still walk in? Yes. In January 2026 the ministry rejected claims that walk-ins had ended. Walk-in patients are assessed first, and those with appointments are prioritised. Emergencies, fever and infectious cases don’t need an appointment.

      Bring your MyKad or passport, any referral letter or screening form, and documents showing that you are exempt from fees. Aim to arrive about 30 minutes early for an appointment.

      Waiting times: the deputy health minister said in 2026 that waits had been up to 3 hours in clinics before a new digital clinic system. After the change, 81% of patients at those clinics saw a doctor within an hour. These figures don’t cover every clinic, and 91 Klinik Kesihatan were operating over capacity in 2025.

      Klinik Kesihatan, private clinic or hospital?

      • Choose a Klinik Kesihatan for long-term conditions, pregnancy care, baby vaccinations, and affordable care when you can wait.
      • Choose a private GP for a quick visit, or when you need a doctor at night or at a weekend. Your employer’s panel clinic may be free for you.
      • Go to a hospital emergency department or call 999 for chest pain, stroke symptoms, severe breathlessness, heavy bleeding or a seizure. Don’t wait to book. See our guide on clinic or emergency department.

      Doctors at government clinics can issue a medical chit (MC) after examining you, and can refer you to a hospital specialist when needed.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are the opening hours of a Klinik Kesihatan?

      Most keep government office hours on working days. Some have extended hours on weekday evenings and Saturday mornings, and a few open on Sundays and public holidays. Check your own clinic.

      How much is a Klinik Kesihatan visit?

      Malaysian citizens pay RM1 to see a doctor and RM5 to see a specialist. People aged 60 and over are treated free as outpatients.

      Can I go to a Klinik Kesihatan without an appointment?

      Yes. Walk-ins are still accepted, although those with appointments are prioritised. Fever, infectious illness and emergencies don’t need an appointment.

      How do I book a Klinik Kesihatan appointment?

      In the MySejahtera app, open Appointments, choose Book Appointment, then pick the service, clinic, date and time.

      Are Klinik Kesihatan open on Saturdays or Sundays?

      Clinics with extended hours open on Saturday mornings. Only a few pilot clinics have also opened on Sundays and public holidays.

      Can foreigners go to a Klinik Kesihatan?

      Yes, but they pay unsubsidised rates, listed at RM40 for general outpatient care and RM120 for a specialist. Tests cost extra.

      What is the difference between Klinik Kesihatan and Klinik Komuniti?

      Both are government clinics and are counted separately by the ministry. Klinik Kesihatan are usually larger and offer a wider range of services.

      Can a Klinik Kesihatan give an MC?

      Yes. A government medical officer can issue a medical certificate after examining you.
