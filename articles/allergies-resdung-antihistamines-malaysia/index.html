      Allergies and resdung (allergic rhinitis): antihistamines explained, nasal sprays and sinusitis

      Allergic rhinitis, often called resdung in Malaysia, is inflammation inside the nose caused by an allergy. In Malaysia’s humid climate it tends to last all year, mainly because of house dust mites. Antihistamines such as cetirizine, loratadine and chlorpheniramine are among the most searched medicines in the country, but they aren’t all the same, and they aren’t always the best treatment.

      Key points
      • Allergic rhinitis causes sneezing, an itchy, runny or blocked nose and itchy, watery eyes. In Malaysia, house dust mites are the most common trigger, followed by cockroaches and pet fur.
      • Non-drowsy antihistamines such as cetirizine and loratadine work about equally well. Cetirizine is a little more likely to cause sleepiness.
      • Chlorpheniramine is an older antihistamine that causes drowsiness, so it isn’t recommended for allergic rhinitis by Malaysian allergy specialists. Don’t drive after taking it.
      • A steroid nasal spray works better than antihistamine tablets for a blocked nose and persistent symptoms, but takes 1 to 2 weeks of regular use for full effect.
      • Decongestant nasal sprays should be used for no more than 5 to 7 days, or they can make the blockage worse.

      What is resdung (allergic rhinitis)?

      In allergic rhinitis, the immune system overreacts to harmless particles in the air, called allergens, causing inflammation in the nose.

      Symptoms start within minutes of contact:

      • sneezing, often in bouts
      • an itchy, runny or blocked nose
      • itchy, red, watery eyes. See our guide to red eyes.
      • an itchy throat or roof of the mouth, and a cough from mucus dripping down the throat
      • tiredness and poor sleep, which can affect work and school

      In Malaysia, symptoms often last all year rather than coming in seasons. The Malaysian Society of Allergy and Immunology says the humid climate favours house dust mites, and cockroaches and animal fur are also major allergens. In a study of children with rhinitis at Hospital UKM, about 7 in 10 were allergic to house dust mites.

      Allergic rhinitis often goes with asthma and eczema, and can make asthma harder to control. See our guide to eczema and hives. Haze and smoke can irritate the nose and make symptoms worse: see our haze guide.

      Antihistamines compared: cetirizine, loratadine and chlorpheniramine

      Antihistamines block histamine, a chemical released during an allergic reaction. They help sneezing, itching, a runny nose and itchy eyes, and also hives, but they don’t do much for a blocked nose.

      • Other non-drowsy antihistamines include fexofenadine, desloratadine and levocetirizine. According to the NHS, they all seem to work about as well as each other, so the choice depends on side effects and what suits you.
      • Avoid alcohol with antihistamines, as it increases drowsiness.
      • Children: doses depend on age and weight, and young children usually need a liquid. Malaysia’s Drug Control Authority says cough and cold medicines containing antihistamines shouldn’t be given to children under 2, and only on a doctor’s or pharmacist’s advice for those aged 2 to 6. Ask a pharmacist for the right product and dose.
      • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: the NHS considers cetirizine and loratadine reasonable choices, but some manufacturers advise avoiding them, so check with your doctor or pharmacist first.
      • In Malaysia, these antihistamines are supplied by pharmacists, without a doctor’s prescription.

      Nasal sprays and rinses

      Steroid nasal sprays are the most effective treatment for persistent or moderate to severe allergic rhinitis, and work better than antihistamine tablets for a blocked nose. The Malaysian allergy consensus recommends them as a main treatment.

      • They start working within hours, but give their full effect after 1 to 2 weeks of daily use, so keep using them.
      • Side effects are mostly local, such as dryness and the occasional nosebleed.
      • How to use: blow your nose, tilt your head slightly forward, close one nostril, put the nozzle in the other nostril pointing slightly outwards (away from the middle of the nose), spray while breathing in gently, then breathe out through your mouth.
      • See a doctor if there is no improvement after 1 to 2 weeks.

      Decongestant nasal sprays (such as xylometazoline) clear a blocked nose quickly, but don’t use them for more than 5 to 7 days. Longer use can cause rebound congestion that makes the blockage worse. They aren’t suitable for young children. Oral decongestant tablets aren’t suitable for young children or in pregnancy, and people with high blood pressure should ask a pharmacist first.

      Salt-water (saline) rinses or sprays wash out allergens and mucus and are safe to use regularly.

      Reducing dust mites and other triggers

      • Use allergy-proof covers on mattresses and pillows.
      • Wash bedding weekly in hot water (60°C or above).
      • Dust with a damp cloth and vacuum regularly, ideally with a HEPA filter. Reduce soft toys, carpets and clutter in the bedroom.
      • Keep the home dry and well ventilated, and deal with damp and mould.
      • Keep pets out of the bedroom, and control cockroaches.

      Allergy testing (a skin prick test or blood test) can identify your triggers. For severe allergic rhinitis that doesn’t improve with a steroid spray and antihistamines, specialists may offer allergen immunotherapy, injections or tablets under the tongue that gradually reduce the allergy over several years.

      Sinusitis or allergies?

      Sinusitis is inflammation of the sinuses, usually after a cold. It causes a blocked nose, thick yellow or green mucus, pain or pressure around the cheeks, eyes or forehead, a reduced sense of smell, and sometimes fever.

      • Most sinusitis is caused by viruses and clears within 2 to 4 weeks without antibiotics. Only a small minority of cases are bacterial.
      • Saline rinses, painkillers and a steroid nasal spray can help.
      • Antibiotics are considered for severe symptoms, or symptoms lasting more than about 10 days and getting worse.
      • See a doctor if symptoms last more than 3 weeks or keep coming back. Sinusitis lasting more than 3 months may need an ENT specialist.

      Get urgent help for swelling or redness around the eye, double or reduced vision, a severe headache, a stiff neck, confusion or a very high fever.

      A cold usually clears in about a week. Allergies tend to cause more sneezing and itching, clear runny mucus, no fever, and last as long as you are exposed to the trigger. See also our guides to flu and a cough that won’t go away.

      Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis)

      Call 999 immediately for swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing or wheezing, difficulty swallowing, feeling faint, or pale or blue lips, especially after a food, medicine or insect sting. If the person has an adrenaline auto-injector, use it straight away, then lie them down with their legs raised. Antihistamine tablets are not enough for anaphylaxis.

      Where to get allergy advice

      A GP can confirm allergic rhinitis, recommend the right antihistamine or steroid spray, treat sinusitis and refer you for allergy testing or to an ENT specialist. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states, including clinics open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      What is resdung in English?

      Resdung usually refers to allergic rhinitis: sneezing, an itchy, runny or blocked nose caused by an allergy, often to house dust mites.

      What is cetirizine used for?

      It is a non-drowsy antihistamine for allergic rhinitis, hives and itchy skin. The usual adult dose is 10 mg once a day.

      Cetirizine or loratadine: which is better?

      They work about equally well. Cetirizine is slightly more likely to cause sleepiness, so loratadine may suit people who need to stay alert.

      Does chlorpheniramine make you sleepy?

      Yes, it is an older antihistamine that often causes drowsiness. Don’t drive or operate machinery after taking it.

      What is the best treatment for a blocked nose from allergies?

      A steroid nasal spray used every day. It takes 1 to 2 weeks to work fully. Antihistamine tablets help less with blockage.

      How long can I use a decongestant nasal spray?

      No more than 5 to 7 days. Longer use can cause rebound congestion that makes your nose more blocked.

      Do I need antibiotics for sinusitis?

      Usually not. Most sinusitis is viral and clears within 2 to 4 weeks. Antibiotics are considered for severe or worsening symptoms after about 10 days.

      Can I take antihistamines while pregnant?

      Some, such as loratadine and cetirizine, are often considered suitable, but check with your doctor or pharmacist first.
