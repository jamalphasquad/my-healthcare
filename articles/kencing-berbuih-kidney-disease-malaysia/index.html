      Kencing berbuih (foamy urine) and kidney disease: early signs, tests and protecting your buah pinggang

      Kencing berbuih means foamy urine. A little foam after a fast stream is normal. Foam that keeps appearing, needs more than one flush or comes with swelling can be a sign of protein in the urine, an early marker of kidney disease. Most people with early chronic kidney disease (penyakit buah pinggang kronik) feel completely well, so simple urine and blood tests are the only way to find it.

      Key points
      • Occasional foam that clears with one flush is usually harmless. See a doctor if foamy urine keeps happening, gets worse, or comes with swelling, tiredness or poor appetite.
      • Chronic kidney disease is usually silent until it is advanced. A Malaysian survey found only 4 in 100 people with it knew they had it.
      • It is found with a urine test for protein (often an albumin-to-creatinine ratio) and a blood test for eGFR. Results must stay abnormal for more than 3 months to confirm it.
      • Diabetes and high blood pressure cause more than 8 in 10 new cases of kidney failure in Malaysia. People with either should have their kidneys checked at least once a year.
      • Protect your kidneys by controlling blood pressure and sugar, avoiding regular anti-inflammatory painkillers and unregistered herbal products, cutting salt, and not smoking.

      Kencing berbuih: when is foamy urine a problem?

      Usually harmless:

      • a fast, forceful stream
      • concentrated, dark urine when you haven’t drunk enough
      • toilet cleaner left in the bowl

      Occasional foam that clears with one flush is usually normal.

      Possibly protein in the urine (proteinuria): damaged kidneys can let protein leak into the urine, which makes it froth. Diabetes, high blood pressure, lupus and chronic kidney disease can all cause this.

      See a doctor if foamy urine:

      • happens regularly or is getting worse
      • needs more than one flush to clear, or looks thick and white
      • lasts more than a few days
      • comes with swollen ankles, feet or hands, unusual tiredness, nausea or poor appetite

      The first step is a simple urine test. If you also see blood in your urine, see our guide to kencing berdarah.

      What is chronic kidney disease?

      Your kidneys filter about 140 litres of blood a day, removing waste as urine. They also balance fluids and minerals and make hormones that control blood pressure, red blood cell production and bone health.

      Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means the kidneys have been damaged or working less well for more than 3 months. Malaysia’s Ministry of Health calls it “common but silent”. Most people have no symptoms until the disease is advanced.

      Later symptoms can include swollen ankles or feet, tiredness, itchy skin, nausea, poor appetite, changes in how often you pass urine, breathlessness, cramps and weight loss.

      Malaysia’s numbers:

      • A national survey estimated that about 15 in 100 adults had CKD in 2018, up from 9 in 100 in 2011. The health minister said in 2026 that more than 5 million Malaysians have it.
      • More than 55,000 people were on dialysis at the end of 2024, and about 9,500 to 10,000 people start dialysis every year.
      • Diabetes caused 55% and high blood pressure 31% of new cases of kidney failure in 2024.

      Kidney tests and what the results mean

      Malaysia’s guideline on CKD recommends two simple tests:

      • Urine test for protein: a dipstick test, or a more sensitive albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) on an early-morning urine sample, especially if you have diabetes.
      • Blood test for eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate), worked out from your creatinine level, age and sex. It shows how well your kidneys filter. Creatinine on its own isn’t enough.

      A positive urine test can be temporary, for example after hard exercise, during a urine infection, fever or a period, or in pregnancy. So abnormal results are repeated within 3 months to confirm them.

      Urine protein (ACR) is graded as A1 (below 3mg/mmol, normal to mildly raised), A2 (3 to 30mg/mmol, moderately raised) or A3 (above 30mg/mmol, severely raised). The higher the protein and the lower the eGFR, the greater the risk. See our guide to blood test results.

      Sakit buah pinggang: is it kidney pain?

      Kidney disease itself is usually painless. Most lower back pain comes from muscles, joints or the spine rather than the kidneys. See our guide to back pain and sciatica.

      Pain from the kidneys is usually felt in the side or back, just under the ribs. See a doctor urgently if this kind of pain comes with fever, shivering, vomiting or blood in the urine, as it may be a kidney infection. Sudden, severe, coming-and-going pain that spreads to the groin is often a kidney stone.

      Who should be checked?

      At least once a year, according to Malaysia’s guideline, if you have:

      • diabetes
      • high blood pressure

      Checking may also be worthwhile if you:

      • are over 65
      • are obese, or have heart disease or metabolic syndrome
      • have a family history of kidney disease
      • have gout, lupus, kidney stones or an enlarged prostate
      • take painkillers or stomach acid medicines (PPIs) long term
      • have had blood or protein found in your urine by chance

      Health screening schemes such as PeKa B40 and the SOCSO health screening programme include blood and urine tests. Check what your scheme covers.

      How to protect your kidneys

      • Control blood pressure. Malaysia’s guideline sets lower targets for people with diabetes or protein in the urine, often around 130/80. Your doctor will set yours.
      • Control blood sugar. An HbA1c of about 7% is a common target for people with diabetes and kidney disease, adjusted for each person.
      • Take kidney-protecting medicines if prescribed. ACE inhibitors or ARBs, and SGLT2 inhibitors, can slow kidney damage in many people with diabetes or protein in the urine. Doctors adjust doses of other medicines to your kidney function.
      • Avoid regular anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, mefenamic acid and celecoxib. Ask a pharmacist about safer options. See our guide to painkillers.
      • Be careful with herbal products. Some herbs containing aristolochic acid have caused kidney disease, and products can be contaminated with heavy metals. “Natural” doesn’t mean safe for the kidneys.
      • Cut salt to less than 2,400mg of sodium a day, about a teaspoon of salt. Avoid very high-protein diets if your kidneys are already affected.
      • Stop smoking, stay active and keep to a healthy weight.

      What about water? Staying hydrated is sensible, but drinking extra water doesn’t prevent kidney disease, and too much can lower your blood sodium. If you have advanced kidney disease, your doctor may limit how much you drink.

      Get your kidneys checked

      A GP can do a urine test and an eGFR blood test, check your blood pressure and sugar, and refer you to a kidney specialist (nephrologist) if needed. Our directory lists clinics across Malaysia, including those open late and at weekends.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is foamy urine a sign of kidney problems?

      Occasional foam is usually harmless. Foam that keeps coming back, needs more than one flush or comes with swelling can mean protein in the urine, and should be checked with a urine test.

      What is the English for kencing berbuih?

      Foamy or frothy urine.

      What is a normal eGFR?

      An eGFR of 90 or more is normal. A result below 60 for more than 3 months means chronic kidney disease, as does a higher eGFR with protein in the urine.

      Can kidney disease be cured?

      Chronic kidney disease usually can’t be reversed, but controlling blood pressure and sugar and the right medicines can slow it down and prevent kidney failure.

      Does drinking more water protect the kidneys?

      Staying hydrated is sensible, but extra water doesn’t prevent kidney disease, and too much can be harmful. People with advanced kidney disease may need to limit fluids.

      Do medicines damage the kidneys?

      Regular anti-inflammatory painkillers and some herbal products can. Many other medicines, including some for blood pressure and diabetes, protect the kidneys. Your doctor adjusts doses to your kidney function.

      How often should I check my kidneys if I have diabetes?

      At least once a year, with a urine protein test and an eGFR blood test.
