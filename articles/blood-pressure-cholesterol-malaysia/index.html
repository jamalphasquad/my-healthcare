      High blood pressure and cholesterol: normal levels (tekanan darah and bacaan kolesterol normal), and how to lower them

      High blood pressure (darah tinggi) and high cholesterol rarely cause symptoms, yet together they are the main reasons Malaysians have heart attacks and strokes. Below are the normal readings, the usual causes, the changes that bring the numbers down, and what to expect if you need medicine.

      Key points
      • According to NHMS 2023, 29.2% of Malaysian adults have high blood pressure and 33.3% have high cholesterol, and many of them don’t know it.
      • High blood pressure usually has no signs, so only a blood pressure check can tell you. Repeated readings of 140/90 or above count as high.
      • There is no safe way to bring blood pressure down in 5 minutes. A very high reading with chest pain, breathlessness, weakness or vision changes is an emergency, so call 999.
      • Cutting salt to less than 1 teaspoon a day, losing weight, exercising and drinking less alcohol can each lower blood pressure by several points.
      • Statins lower LDL cholesterol by about 20–55%. Real statin side effects are less common than many people think.
      • Your LDL target depends on your overall heart risk, so there is no single normal figure. You usually don’t need to fast for a cholesterol test.

      How common are they in Malaysia?

      The National Health and Morbidity Survey (NHMS) 2023 found that:

      • 29.2% of adults have high blood pressure, including 11.9% of adults (about 2.7 million people) who don’t know they have it. Fewer than half of those being treated have it under control.
      • 33.3% have high cholesterol, and about half of them don’t know.
      • More than 60% of adults aged 18 to 39 with diabetes, high blood pressure or high cholesterol didn’t know they had it.

      Both conditions lead to ischaemic heart disease, which is Malaysia’s leading cause of death. Screening picks them up early. See our guide to free health screening.

      Normal blood pressure levels (tekanan darah normal)

      Blood pressure is written as two numbers. The top one (systolic) is the pressure when your heart beats, and the bottom one (diastolic) is the pressure between beats. Malaysia’s hypertension guidelines group readings like this:

      Reading (mmHg)Category
      Below 120/80Optimal
      120–129 / 80–84Normal
      130–139 / 85–89At risk
      140–159 / 90–99Stage 1 (mild) hypertension
      160–179 / 100–109Stage 2 (moderate)
      180 / 110 or higherStage 3 (severe)

      A single high reading doesn’t mean you have hypertension. Doctors confirm it with repeated readings, often taken at home (see our guide to checking your blood pressure at home). Once you are on treatment, the target for most people is below 140/90. Some people with diabetes, kidney disease or heart disease aim lower.

      Signs of high blood pressure (tanda darah tinggi)

      High blood pressure is often called a “silent disease” because most people who have it feel completely well. Headaches, dizziness and tiredness aren’t reliable signs. Only a blood pressure check can tell you.

      When high blood pressure is an emergency

      A reading of 180/110 or higher needs prompt medical attention. Call 999 or go straight to an emergency department if it comes with chest pain, breathlessness, a severe headache, confusion, weakness or numbness, trouble speaking, or changes in your vision. If you have no symptoms, sit quietly for 5 minutes and measure again. If it is still very high, see a doctor the same day.

      What causes high blood pressure? (Punca darah tinggi)

      Most high blood pressure has no single cause. It builds up over time with:

      • Too much salt. Malaysians eat around 8 to 9.5 g of salt a day, well above the recommended limit
      • Being overweight or inactive
      • Alcohol and smoking
      • Getting older, or a family history of it
      • Stress

      Sometimes another condition or a medicine is to blame, especially in younger people. Examples are kidney disease, sleep apnoea (heavy snoring with daytime sleepiness), thyroid or hormone problems, and medicines such as anti-inflammatory painkillers, steroids, decongestants and the contraceptive pill. Tell your doctor about any traditional or herbal remedies you take as well.

      How to lower blood pressure

      Can you lower blood pressure in 5 minutes? Not safely. Slow, deep breathing may bring it down a little for a short time, and doing it regularly can help by a few points. There is no quick fix, though, and Malaysian guidelines warn that bringing very high blood pressure down too fast can itself cause a stroke or heart attack. If a reading is very high, follow the emergency advice above.

      According to Malaysia’s hypertension guidelines, these changes work over weeks to months:

      ChangeTypical effect on blood pressure
      Cut salt to less than 5 g (about 1 teaspoon) a dayUp to about 8/3 mmHg lower in Asian adults with hypertension
      Lose weightAbout 1 mmHg lower per kg lost
      A diet with plenty of fruit, vegetables and low-fat dairySeveral points lower
      150 minutes of moderate exercise a weekSeveral points lower
      Cut down alcoholAbout 3/2 mmHg lower

      For most Malaysians, the biggest difference comes from cooking with less salt, soy sauce and stock cubes, and eating fewer processed and hawker foods.

      Blood pressure medicines

      If lifestyle changes aren’t enough, or your blood pressure is already high, your doctor will prescribe one or more medicines. The main types are ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers, diuretics and beta blockers. Side effects worth knowing about:

      • ACE inhibitors (names often end in “-pril”) give some people a dry, persistent cough. If that happens, your doctor can switch you to an ARB.
      • Calcium channel blockers such as amlodipine can cause ankle swelling, flushing or headaches.
      • Beta blockers can make you tired and give you cold hands and feet.

      Most people need treatment for life. Don’t stop your medicine when your readings become normal, because that usually means it is working. If you have side effects, talk to your doctor.

      Normal cholesterol levels (bacaan kolesterol normal)

      Malaysia’s dyslipidaemia guideline flags these levels as abnormal:

      TestFlagged as abnormalIn mg/dL
      Total cholesterolAbove 5.2 mmol/LAbove about 200
      HDL (“good”) cholesterolBelow 1.0 (men) or 1.2 (women)Below about 39 or 46
      TriglyceridesAbove 1.7 mmol/LAbove about 150
      LDL (“bad”) cholesterolDepends on your risk (see below)Optimal is below about 100
      • The guideline describes an LDL of 1.3 to 1.8 mmol/L as physiologically normal, and below 2.6 as optimal for adults.
      • Non-HDL cholesterol (total cholesterol minus HDL) is a useful extra measure, especially if you have diabetes or high triglycerides. Its target is your LDL target plus 0.8.
      • To convert cholesterol from mmol/L to mg/dL, multiply by about 38.7. For triglycerides, multiply by about 88.6.

      For the other numbers on your report, see our guide to blood test results.

      Your LDL target depends on your risk

      Your doctor first works out your risk of a heart attack or stroke. Heart disease, diabetes or kidney disease automatically put you at high or very high risk. Otherwise, the doctor uses a score based on your age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes and smoking to estimate your 10-year risk.

      RiskLDL target (2017 guideline)2023 update
      Low (under 10% in 10 years)Below 3.0 mmol/LBelow 3.0 mmol/L
      Intermediate (10 to 20%)Below 3.0 mmol/LReported as below 2.6 mmol/L
      High (over 20%, diabetes, kidney disease)2.6 mmol/L, or a 50% reduction1.8 mmol/L or less, and at least a 50% reduction
      Very high (heart attack, stroke, narrowed arteries)Below 1.8 mmol/L, or a 50% reduction1.4 mmol/L or less, and at least a 50% reduction

      An LDL above 4.9 mmol/L puts you at high risk on its own. The 2023 targets are taken from published summaries of the update, so your doctor may use slightly different figures. See our guide to signs of heart disease.

      Cholesterol tests: who, when and do I need to fast?

      All adults over 30 should have a full lipid profile. Start from age 18 if you have a family history of early heart disease, diabetes, obesity around the waist or a known inherited cholesterol problem.

      Most people don’t need to fast, because the results differ by only about 0.2 mmol/L. You may be asked to fast if your triglycerides were very high before, or if you are having other tests that need fasting.

      If your results are normal, a repeat test every few years is typical. If you start or change treatment, your doctor will recheck in 1 to 3 months, then every 6 to 12 months.

      The 2023 National Health and Morbidity Survey found high cholesterol in a third of adults in Malaysia, about 7.5 million people, and about half of them didn’t know. Some schemes offer free screening. See our guide to free health screening.

      Inherited high cholesterol (familial hypercholesterolaemia) affects about 1 in 100 Malaysians. Suspect it if your LDL is very high, if a parent or sibling had heart disease before 55 (men) or 65 (women), or if you have fatty lumps on the tendons of your fingers or heels. Your family members should be tested too.

      How to lower cholesterol

      • Cut saturated fat to less than 10% of your energy. Santan, coconut oil and palm oil are high in it, and so are fatty meat and fried food. A review of 16 trials found coconut oil raised LDL compared with other vegetable oils.
      • Avoid trans fats, which are in some biscuits, pastries and fried snacks.
      • Eat more fibre, aiming for 20 to 30g a day from vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains. Oats with 3g of beta-glucan a day lower LDL by about 0.25 mmol/L. Juicing removes the fibre, so eat fruit whole.
      • Eat less refined carbohydrate, such as white rice and sugary drinks, especially if your triglycerides are high.
      • Regular aerobic exercise raises HDL and lowers triglycerides.
      • If you are overweight, losing even 5 to 10% of your weight helps. See our BMI calculator.
      • Stopping smoking raises HDL.

      For most people, cholesterol in food such as eggs has only a weak effect on blood cholesterol. The American Heart Association says one egg a day is reasonable as part of a healthy diet.

      Diet changes take weeks to months to work. Statins usually reach their full effect within about a month, and doctors recheck cholesterol 1 to 3 months after you start.

      Cholesterol medicines and side effects (kesan sampingan ubat kolesterol)

      Statins lower LDL by about 21 to 55%, depending on the type and dose. Common generic statins are atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and pravastatin. If you don’t reach your target, your doctor may add another medicine such as ezetimibe or an injection (PCSK9 inhibitors).

      • In blinded trials, muscle aches were almost as common on placebo (26.6%) as on statins (27.1%), and more than 90% of reported muscle symptoms weren’t caused by the statin. Most people who do get symptoms can take a different statin.
      • Fewer than 3 in 100 people have a mild rise in liver tests. Serious liver problems are very rare.
      • Statins slightly increase the chance of developing diabetes, but the benefit for the heart is much greater.
      • Stop statins before trying for a baby, and don’t take them while pregnant or breastfeeding.
      • Grapefruit can interact with some statins.

      Red yeast rice contains monacolin K, which is the same substance as the statin lovastatin. In Malaysia these products must be registered, are limited to 10mg of monacolin K a day, and must carry warnings. Check for a MAL number, and don’t take them with a prescribed statin.

      High-dose fish oil lowers triglycerides, but it doesn’t replace a statin for lowering LDL.

      Don’t stop a statin on your own. If you have side effects, ask your doctor about changing the dose or type.

      Where to get your blood pressure and cholesterol checked

      Any GP clinic can check your blood pressure in a few minutes and arrange a cholesterol test. Government health clinics offer free checks, and many people qualify for free screening. Our directory lists GP clinics across Kuala Lumpur, Selangor and other states.

      Frequently asked questions

      What are the signs of high blood pressure? (Tanda darah tinggi)

      Usually there are none. High blood pressure is a “silent disease”, so only a blood pressure check can tell you. Very high blood pressure with chest pain, breathlessness, a severe headache, weakness or vision changes is an emergency.

      How can I lower my blood pressure quickly? (Cara menurunkan darah tinggi dengan cepat)

      You can’t lower it safely within minutes, and bringing very high blood pressure down too fast can be dangerous. Over weeks, cutting salt, losing weight, exercising and drinking less alcohol all help. If a reading is 180/110 or higher and you have symptoms, call 999.

      What is a normal cholesterol level? (Kadar kolesterol normal)

      Malaysian guidelines flag total cholesterol above 5.2 mmol/L, HDL below 1.0 (men) or 1.2 (women), and triglycerides above 1.7. Your LDL target depends on your heart risk.

      What are the side effects of cholesterol medicine? (Kesan ubat kolesterol)

      Statins can cause muscle aches in some people, mild changes in liver tests and a slightly higher chance of diabetes. Serious side effects are rare. Talk to your doctor instead of stopping the medicine yourself.

      Can I stop my blood pressure medicine when my readings are normal?

      No. Normal readings usually mean the medicine is working, and most people need it long term. Talk to your doctor before changing anything.

      How fast can I lower my cholesterol? (Menurunkan kolesterol dengan cepat)

      Statins reach their full effect in about a month, while diet changes take weeks to months. Your doctor will usually recheck your cholesterol 1 to 3 months after a change.

      What is a normal LDL level?

      For most adults, an LDL below 2.6 mmol/L is optimal. People at low or intermediate risk aim for below 3.0, and people at high or very high risk aim much lower, down to 1.4 mmol/L.

      Do I need to fast for a cholesterol test?

      Usually not. Your doctor may ask you to fast if your triglycerides were very high before, or if you are having other tests that need fasting.

      Are eggs bad for cholesterol?

      For most people, eggs have only a small effect on blood cholesterol. About one egg a day is reasonable as part of a healthy diet.

      Is red yeast rice safe?

      It contains the same substance as a statin. Only use registered products with a MAL number, and don’t take it with a prescribed statin.
