      Normal blood sugar levels and HbA1c: charts, prediabetes and early signs of diabetes

      About 1 in 6 Malaysian adults has diabetes, and 2 in 5 of them don’t know it. A simple blood test can tell you whether your sugar is normal, in the prediabetes range, or high enough to be diabetes. Here are the numbers doctors in Malaysia use, and what to do about your result.

      Key points
      • Normal fasting blood sugar is below 6.1 mmol/L, and a normal HbA1c is below 5.7%.
      • Malaysia diagnoses diabetes at an HbA1c of 6.3% or more, lower than the 6.5% used by WHO and the American Diabetes Association. Fasting sugar of 7.0 mmol/L or more also means diabetes.
      • Prediabetes is a fasting sugar of 6.1–6.9 mmol/L or an HbA1c of 5.7–6.2%. It usually has no symptoms, and can often be reversed.
      • Warning signs include thirst, passing a lot of urine, waking at night to urinate, tiredness, weight loss and itching or infections around the genitals. Many people have no symptoms at all.
      • Malaysian guidelines advise testing from age 30, yearly, and earlier if you are overweight or have a risk factor.

      Normal blood sugar levels chart (Malaysia)

      These are the cut-offs in Malaysia’s Clinical Practice Guidelines on the Management of Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (6th edition), for blood taken from a vein and tested in a laboratory.

      TestNormalPrediabetesDiabetes
      Fasting blood sugar (8+ hours without food)Below 6.1 mmol/L6.1–6.9 mmol/L7.0 mmol/L or more
      2 hours after a glucose drink (OGTT)Below 7.8 mmol/L7.8–11.0 mmol/L11.1 mmol/L or more
      HbA1cBelow 5.7%5.7–6.2%6.3% or more
      Random blood sugar (any time)––11.1 mmol/L or more

      mmol/L or mg/dL? Malaysia uses mmol/L. To convert, multiply by 18: for example, 7.0 mmol/L is about 126 mg/dL, and 11.1 mmol/L is about 200 mg/dL. HbA1c is shown as a percentage, or sometimes in mmol/mol (6.3% is 45 mmol/mol).

      Why Malaysia uses 6.3%, not 6.5%

      The World Health Organization and the American Diabetes Association diagnose diabetes at an HbA1c of 6.5%. A large Malaysian study found that 6.5% missed too many people with diabetes here, so the Malaysian guideline uses 6.3%, which works across Malay, Chinese and Indian Malaysians. Malaysia also counts a fasting sugar from 6.1 mmol/L as prediabetes, like WHO, while the American Diabetes Association uses 5.6 mmol/L. So an online chart from another country may not match what your doctor tells you.

      What is HbA1c, and what is the normal range?

      HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin) shows your average blood sugar over the past 2 to 3 months, by measuring how much sugar has stuck to your red blood cells. You don’t need to fast for it, and one result isn’t thrown off by what you ate yesterday.

      • Below 5.7%: normal.
      • 5.7% to 6.2%: prediabetes. Your doctor may suggest an oral glucose tolerance test to check further.
      • 6.3% or above: diabetes, confirmed with a second test if you have no symptoms.
      • If you have diabetes: the usual target is below 7.0% (see targets below).

      The test must be done in a laboratory using a standardised method. The quick fingerprick HbA1c machines some clinics use are fine for monitoring diabetes, but Malaysian guidelines say they shouldn’t be used to diagnose it.

      Prediabetes: the warning stage

      Prediabetes means your blood sugar is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetes range. It almost never causes symptoms, but without changes many people go on to develop type 2 diabetes.

      The good news is that it can often be reversed. Losing 5–10% of your body weight if you are overweight, being active for at least 150 minutes a week, and cutting down on sugary drinks, white rice portions and refined snacks all lower your risk. If you have prediabetes, get your blood sugar checked every year.

      Early signs of diabetes (tanda kencing manis)

      Up to half of people with diabetes have no symptoms. In Malaysia’s National Health and Morbidity Survey 2023, 15.6% of adults (about 3.6 million people) had diabetes, and 5.9% didn’t know it. Among diabetics aged 18 to 29, 84% hadn’t been diagnosed. When symptoms do appear, they include:

      • feeling very thirsty
      • passing a lot of urine, and waking up at night to urinate
      • feeling tired and lethargic
      • feeling hungry more often
      • losing weight without trying
      • itching around the vagina, or soreness and swelling at the tip of the penis, often from repeated yeast infections
      • blurred vision, cuts that heal slowly, and frequent skin or urine infections

      If you have these signs, see a doctor for a blood test rather than waiting. Read more in our guide to undiagnosed diabetes in young adults.

      Blood sugar targets if you have diabetes

      MeasureTarget for most adults with type 2 diabetes
      Fasting or before meals4.4–7.0 mmol/L
      After meals (at least 90 minutes after eating)4.4–8.5 mmol/L
      HbA1cBelow 7.0%

      Your doctor will set a target that suits you. A tighter HbA1c target of 6.5% or below may suit younger, newly diagnosed people at low risk of low sugar. A less strict target of 7.1–8.0% is safer for older people, those with serious heart or kidney disease or dementia, or those who have had severe low sugar episodes.

      Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)

      For people on diabetes medicines, especially insulin or certain tablets, blood sugar can drop too low. Hypoglycaemia is a reading below 3.9 mmol/L, and below 3.0 mmol/L is more serious. Signs include shaking, sweating, a fast heartbeat, hunger, confusion and irritability.

      What to do for low sugar

      Take 15 g of fast-acting sugar, such as half a glass of fruit juice or regular (not diet) soft drink, or 3 teaspoons of sugar in water. Recheck after 15 minutes, and repeat until your reading is above 3.9 mmol/L, then eat a snack or meal. If someone is drowsy, confused or unconscious, don’t give food or drink by mouth: call 999.

      Who should get tested, and how often

      • Everyone from age 30, and every year after that if the result is normal. This is earlier and more often than in some other countries, because diabetes is so common in Malaysia.
      • At any age if your BMI is 23 or more, or your waist is 80 cm or more (women) or 90 cm or more (men), plus any risk factor: a parent, brother or sister with diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, low HDL or high triglycerides, PCOS, an inactive lifestyle, diabetes in a past pregnancy, or a baby born weighing 4 kg or more.
      • Every year if you have prediabetes.
      • Children and teenagers who are overweight and have a risk factor, from puberty or age 10.

      Free screening is available at Klinik Kesihatan, and through PeKa B40 if you receive STR and are 40 or over. See our guides to free health screening and PeKa B40.

      Home glucometer vs lab test

      A home glucometer uses a drop of blood from your fingertip. It is very useful for monitoring diabetes day to day, but it is less precise than a laboratory test: good meters can still be about 15% out. Heat and humidity can also affect strips, so store them as instructed.

      A fingerprick result is a screening result only. If a random fingerprick reading is 7.8 mmol/L or more, or a fasting reading is 5.6 mmol/L or more, Malaysian guidelines say you should have a proper blood test from a vein (fasting sugar, OGTT or HbA1c) to confirm.

      Is one test enough to diagnose diabetes? If you have symptoms, one high result from a vein blood test can be enough. If you don’t, a second abnormal result is needed, either a repeat test on another day or a glucose and HbA1c from the same sample.

      Blood sugar in pregnancy

      Diabetes can also develop during pregnancy (gestational diabetes). In Malaysia, it is diagnosed with a glucose tolerance test if the fasting level is 5.1 mmol/L or more or the 2-hour level is 7.8 mmol/L or more. Women with risk factors are tested at their first antenatal visit, and women aged 25 or over are tested at 24–28 weeks. HbA1c isn’t used to diagnose it.       Where to get your blood sugar checked

      Most GP clinics can test your blood sugar and HbA1c, often while you wait for a fingerprick result or within a day or two for a lab result.       Frequently asked questions

      What is a normal blood sugar level?

      Under Malaysian guidelines, a normal fasting blood sugar is below 6.1 mmol/L, and below 7.8 mmol/L two hours after a glucose drink. A normal HbA1c is below 5.7%.

      What is the normal HbA1c range?

      Below 5.7% is normal. 5.7% to 6.2% is prediabetes, and 6.3% or above is diabetes in Malaysia. For people with diabetes, the usual target is below 7.0%.

      Why is the HbA1c cut-off for diabetes 6.3% in Malaysia?

      A Malaysian study found that the 6.5% cut-off used by WHO and the American Diabetes Association missed too many people with diabetes here, so the Malaysian guideline uses 6.3%.

      What blood sugar level is diabetes?

      A fasting blood sugar of 7.0 mmol/L or more, a level of 11.1 mmol/L or more two hours after a glucose drink or at any time with symptoms, or an HbA1c of 6.3% or more. Without symptoms, a second test is needed to confirm.

      What are the early signs of diabetes (tanda kencing manis)?

      Thirst, passing a lot of urine, waking at night to urinate, tiredness, hunger, unexplained weight loss, and itching or infections around the genitals. Many people have no symptoms, so regular testing matters.

      What blood sugar level is too low?

      Below 3.9 mmol/L is low (hypoglycaemia), and below 3.0 mmol/L is more serious. Take 15 g of fast-acting sugar, recheck after 15 minutes, and call 999 if the person is drowsy or unconscious.

      How to convert mmol/L to mg/dL?

      Multiply mmol/L by 18. For example, 5.5 mmol/L is about 99 mg/dL and 7.0 mmol/L is about 126 mg/dL.

      At what age should I check my blood sugar?

      Malaysian guidelines advise testing from age 30, then yearly. Test earlier if you are overweight and have a risk factor such as a family history of diabetes or high blood pressure.
